# The site in German and Arabic: `tools/i18n/`, `i18n/`, `public/de/`, `public/ar/`

Phyein Usul is written in English. The German and Arabic pages are made from the English ones by a generator, so the English
page stays the only source and a translation can never silently describe an older page. Since 23 October 2026 the home page,
the simulations catalog, the Portfolios page (batch 1) and the simulation pages with their lecture notes, walkthroughs and
data panels (batch 2; all eleven since Fig. 11 joined on 24 October) exist in all three languages; Iron, Cotton and the Head exists in English, German and Arabic
(`portfolios/impact/index.html`, `de.html`, `ar.html`, made by its own `portfolios-src/impact/build.py` from `text_en.py`,
`text_de.py`, `text_ar.py`), and so does the Hydraulic Press Study (`portfolios/hydraulics/de.html`, `ar.html`, below). The words used for physics terms are fixed in
[glossary.md](glossary.md).

| Address | Made from | Made by |
|---|---|---|
| `/de/index.html`, `/ar/index.html` | `index.html` | `tools/i18n/build.py` |
| `/de/simulations.html`, `/ar/simulations.html` | `public/simulations.html` | the same |
| `/de/portfolios.html`, `/ar/portfolios.html` | `public/portfolios.html` | the same |
| `/de/simulations/*.html`, `/ar/simulations/*.html` | `public/simulations/*.html` | the same |
| `scripts/walk/de/*.js`, `scripts/walk/ar/*.js` | `scripts/walk/*.js` and `i18n/<lang>/walk.json` | the same |

The generated pages are committed (Cloudflare only runs `vite build`, which copies `public/` as it is), and so are the files the
generator writes beside them: `public/scripts/i18n/strings-de.js`, `strings-ar.js` and `public/styles/*.rtl.css`.
**Never edit a generated file;** edit the English page or a table and run the generator.

## How to run it

```sh
python3 tools/i18n/build.py            # write every German and Arabic page, the string files and the mirrored stylesheets
python3 tools/i18n/build.py --check    # the same, and exit 1 if any piece of English has no current translation
python3 tools/i18n/build.py --todo de  # print, as JSON, every English piece German still lacks (per page, and the script strings)
python3 tools/i18n/fill.py de portfolios.html file.py   # store TR = {key: translation} for that page; the English is recorded
```

It needs Python 3 and Node (to read `scripts/catalog.js`), and `npx` fetches `rtlcss@4.3.0` the first time. Run it after any
change to one of the three English pages, to `scripts/catalog.js`, to a script that uses `T('…')`, or to a stylesheet that
has an `.rtl.css` copy, and commit what it writes.

## How a page is cut into pieces

The picture: the English page is a printed sheet, and the translator lays a transparent overlay on it that replaces only the
words. The layout, links, scripts, maths and numbers underneath stay the same sheet.

- A **piece** is the inner HTML of the outermost element that holds words of its own (text that is its direct child), with its
  inline markup: a paragraph, a list item, a heading, a caption, a button, a link. The attributes `alt`, `title`, `aria-label`,
  `placeholder` and the `content` of `<meta name="description">` and `<meta name="author">` are pieces too.
- Left alone: `script`, `style`, `svg`, `code`, `pre`, `math`, `textarea`, anything inside an element with `translate="no"` or
  class `notex`, and text without two Latin letters in a row.
- Each piece is keyed by the first 12 hex digits of the SHA-1 of its English with whitespace collapsed. `i18n/<lang>/<page>.json`
  stores, per key, `src` (the English it was translated from) and `tr` (the translation). A translation is used only while its
  `src` equals the English on the page now.
- When the English changes, the old translation no longer matches: the page shows the new English followed by a small yellow dot
  (`span.i18n-pending`, title "translation being updated"), and `--check` fails and names the piece. Nothing out of date is
  ever shown as current.

What else the generator does to each page:

- sets `<html lang="de" dir="ltr">` or `<html lang="ar" dir="rtl">`;
- rewrites relative addresses one level up (`styles/site.css` becomes `../styles/site.css`), except links to pages that exist in
  the same language, which stay inside it;
- on Arabic pages, loads `styles/<name>.rtl.css` instead of `styles/<name>.css`, adds `fonts/arabic.css`, and wraps every number
  with its unit, and every number range, in the Unicode isolates LRI … PDI (U+2066, U+2069), so "12 MPa" and
  "1 700–2 100 cm⁻³" keep their left-to-right order inside right-to-left text (maths and tags are left alone);
- fills the language switch and the `hreflang` alternates between the markers `<!--I18N-SWITCH-->…<!--/I18N-SWITCH-->` and
  `<!--I18N-ALTERNATES-->…<!--/I18N-ALTERNATES-->` (the English pages get them too, on the lines that already held them, so no
  line number cited in the docs moves);
- adds `scripts/i18n/strings-<lang>.js` and `<meta name="translated-from" content="English index.html, commit aa642f4">` to the
  head: the last commit that changed the English page (with `+` if it had uncommitted changes when the page was generated).

## Words inside scripts: `T()` and `i18n/<lang>/strings.json`

Some words are written by JavaScript, not by the page: `fold.js` (More, Less, Expand all, "7 roles") and the catalog's inline
script (card labels, the count, the empty message), and every word of `scripts/catalog.js`. These go through one table per
language.

- A script writes `T('Expand all')` or `T('Open Fig. {n}', { n: 3 })`. `T` looks the English up in `window.I18N` and returns it
  unchanged when there is no table (the English pages) or no entry. `{name}` placeholders are filled from the second argument.
- Counted words go through `fold.js`'s `plural(n, 'step', 'steps')`. On a translated page the entry for `'steps'` is an object of
  plural forms chosen by `Intl.PluralRules`: German `one`/`other`; Arabic `zero`, `one` (خطوة واحدة), `two` (خطوتان),
  `few` 3–10 (`{n} خطوات`), `many` 11–99 (`{n} خطوةً`), `other` 100 and up. A form that contains `{n}` places the number itself.
  The simulation pages count the same way through `_t('{n} sources', { n: n })`: `sim.js`'s translator picks the form when the
  entry is such an object and the call passes `n` (Fig. 1's source count: «مصدران», «16 مصدرًا»).
- The catalog's entries are translated by their English text: `T(s.title)`, `T(s.summary)`, `T(c)` for each concept. Search
  looks through both the translated and the English words, so on the Arabic page both "فوضى" and "chaos" find the double
  pendulum (measured: German "Schwebung" 3 of 10, Arabic "الضربات" 3 of 10, English "beats" 3 of 10).
- `strings-<lang>.js` also sets `window.I18N_ROOT` (the site root, from the script's own address) and `window.I18N_HAVE` (the
  pages that exist in this language). The catalog uses them so that a card for a simulation not yet translated opens the English
  page and its thumbnail loads from the shared `images/`.
- `--check` scans `fold.js` and `simulations.html` for every literal `T('…')` and every `plural(…, 'one', 'many')`, reads
  `catalog.js` with Node, and requires an entry for each: 174 strings on 23 October 2026, all present in both languages.

## The simulation pages (batch 2)

- **Lecture notes and page text** are pieces like any other page (`i18n/<lang>/simulations__<page>.html.json`).
- **Words the page's own script writes** (status lines, canvas labels, button states, the texts of the data-panel hooks) go
  through `_t('…')`: the pages already use `T` for `Sim.theme`, so `sim.js` defines `window._t` and `window.T` as the same
  function. The calls were inserted with a JavaScript parser (acorn), only where a string reaches the reader: joined into
  displayed text, a branch of `a ? 'x' : 'y'`, assigned to `textContent`, `innerHTML` or `title`, drawn with `fillText`, or a
  hook text (`what`, `drawn`, `note`, `label`, `blocked`); never in a comparison, a key or a selector. Every insertion is on its
  own line's place, so no line number moves.
- **The data panel** (`datalog.js`) passes its own words through `T()`, and also what a page declares: the dataset title, the
  kicker, group titles and notes, the `about` paragraph, each variable's `long_name` and, since 24 October 2026, the page's
  ledger (`scripts/ledger/<page>.js`: headline words, section titles and notes, book titles, notes and `short` labels, and
  the `_t('…')` words of its checks). `--check` collects those from the pages and the ledger files
  (`long_name: '…'`, `title:`, `note:`, `short:`, `about:`, `kicker:`), and the slider names a page hands to its `sliderCheck(input,
  'wavelength')`, which the page passes through `_t()` in the Check line "read from the … slider now".
- **Walkthroughs** stay data: `scripts/walk/<key>.js` is read with Node, its prose (intro, step titles, `where`, `note`, the meaning
  of each symbol) is replaced from `i18n/<lang>/walk.json`, and the code, formulas and ⟦key|…⟧ markers are copied unchanged into
  `scripts/walk/<lang>/<key>.js`. `sim.js` loads that file on a translated page (`window.I18N_WALK`).
- **The data panel runs right to left** on an Arabic page since 24 October 2026: it takes the page's `lang` and `dir`, while
  its numbers, units, Δ and time stamps are `dir="ltr"` and the alignment of numbers is marked `/*rtl:ignore*/` in `data.css`,
  so `data.rtl.css` does not mirror it and the decimal points line up. Before, the panel was fixed to `dir="ltr"`, which
  scrambled mixed Arabic and Latin text.
- **Figures read left to right** in Arabic too: canvases, SVG, legends, readouts, slider values (`<output>`, "24 px") and the pages' inline maths (`span.m`) are isolated
  left-to-right runs (`styles/i18n.css`); without that, canvas text inherits right-to-left and "x(t)" draws as "x)t(".

How batch 2 was translated: by parallel Haiku agents, one per page and language pair, each with a written brief (glossary terms,
markup, maths and numbers kept) and a checker that rejects any piece whose tags, maths segments, numbers, ⟦…⟧ markers,
placeholders or edge spaces differ from the English; `tools/i18n/ingest.py` stores only checked output. The texts are
machine-quality drafts reviewed for structure, not yet read line by line by a native teacher.

## The Hydraulic Press Study (batch 3)

`python3 tools/i18n/hydraulics.py` (and `--check`, `--todo de`, `--ingest`) writes `portfolios/hydraulics/de.html` and `ar.html`.
The study's English source in `portfolios-src/hydraulics/` is never changed: the generator copies the folder to a temporary place,
and there
- translates the two simulation pages, the four theory sources (`theory/*.src.html`, maths as `$…$` kept byte for byte) and the
  theory template, from `i18n/<lang>/hydraulics__<file>.json`;
- wraps the pages' visible strings in `_t()` (`wrap.cjs`, here also arrays of prose such as the symbol tables), and puts the
  translations from `i18n/<lang>/hydraulics_strings.json`, with the site's own string table for the data panel, first in each
  frame's head; the servo page's Web Worker, which it builds from its own source, receives the same table;
- routes the words the servo page prints from data rather than from literals through `_t()` as well (`code_words()`): the mode
  names of the stepper and the mode pill, the pill's force/position, and the "live" tag of its symbol table;
- on Arabic, sets the frames right to left, mirrors their inline styles with rtlcss, and gives the house font names Arabic letters
  (`@font-face` rules for IBM Plex Sans, IBM Plex Mono, Instrument Serif and Latin Modern Roman, limited to the Arabic block, that
  point at the self-hosted Noto Naskh Arabic and Amiri), so no style of the study had to change;
- runs the study's own `build_theory.py` (KaTeX at build time; `npm install` in `portfolios-src/hydraulics/tools` first) and
  `build_combined.py --site`, renamed to `<lang>.html`, with translated tab labels, title, brand line ("from Pascal to a servo loop") and back link;
  the textbook's counters under its title (chapters, numbered equations, figures) are translated in `build_theory.py` first.

Measured on 23 October 2026 in Chromium, both languages, all three tabs, 1100 and 390 px: no script error, no KaTeX error, no
horizontal scroll; the words "the/and/of/with/which" fell from 193, 103 and 27 per frame to 10, 1 and 15 (the theory's are the
titles of cited English books). The German and Arabic Portfolios pages link the study's own language version.

## How the translations were checked

Batch 1 was translated by hand and reviewed. Batches 2–4 were drafted by Haiku agents and then reviewed page by page by stronger
models (Opus for Figs. 2 and 9, Sonnet for the rest), each correction applied by `tools/i18n/apply_review.py` only if it keeps the
markup, maths, numbers and markers of the English. The first review (Figs. 2 and 9) changed 143 entries, among them three real
errors in Arabic (for example "a thousand radians" for a thousandth); the drafts are not trusted without a review.

## The turn on a language switch

Changing language turns the page half a turn about the vertical axis: a rotation by π about y maps (x, y, z) to (−x, y, −z), so
the projected page is mirrored left to right, which is what English to Arabic does to the layout. It uses cross-document View
Transitions: `styles/i18n.css` opts in (`@view-transition { navigation: auto; }`) and sets two keyframes (the old page turns to
90°, the new one from −90°, 0.28 s each); `scripts/flip.js`, loaded in the head of every page that has a language switch, skips the
transition whenever the language does not change, so ordinary links switch as before. The browser photographs the old page once
and turns two pictures on the graphics card; no script runs per frame (about 5 MB of GPU memory for half a second at 1440 × 900).
Readers who ask for reduced motion, and browsers without cross-document view transitions (Firefox in October 2026), get the plain
switch. Measured in Chromium: English → Arabic and Arabic → German start and finish a transition; German home → catalog none.

## Right to left

Arabic pages are mirror images of the English ones, made in three layers:

1. `dir="rtl"` on `<html>` turns text, flex rows and grids round.
2. Mirrored copies of the house stylesheets, `styles/{site,fold,catalog,portfolio,paper,sim,data}.rtl.css`, exchange every
   physical left and right (margins, borders, positions, corner radii, `text-align`), made by
   [rtlcss](https://rtlcss.com) 4.3.0; arrows drawn with CSS `content` are turned round as well. Without them the skip link,
   parked at `left: -9999px`, widened the Arabic home page to 11 099 px (measured).
3. `styles/i18n.css` holds the few rules a translated page needs: the language switch, the Arabic fonts, no letter-spacing
   (it breaks joined letters) and no slanted Arabic (`font-synthesis-style: none`; Latin words keep their italic face), maths,
   code and key figures kept left to right.

Fonts: German uses the house fonts. Arabic uses only the self-hosted Amiri (titles) and Noto Naskh Arabic (text and labels) from
`fonts/arabic.css`, placed after the house fonts in each stack, so Latin letters, digits and maths keep IBM Plex, Instrument
Serif, Latin Modern and KaTeX; `unicode-range` limits the Arabic faces to Arabic letters.

## Conventions

- **Name.** Arabic pages give the name as نعمة سائر قدسية; English and German keep Numa Koudsie.
- **Numbers** are unchanged: Western digits, the decimal point in all three languages (as the figures, the maths and the data panel
  print them), SI units with a space ("12 MPa").
- **German** addresses the reader as "Sie", as German textbooks and university pages do.
- **Studies that stay English:** the Droplet Collection and Rotor Pendula pages (generated from `portfolios-src/`, no longer app
  copies) have no German or Arabic version; their Portfolios entries are translated and link the English page.
