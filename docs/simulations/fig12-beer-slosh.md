# Fig. 12 · A Pint of Stout on a Suspension (`public/simulations/beer-slosh.html`)

A pint of Guinness in the real glass, drawn from its engineering elevation with the two sculpted panels of the 2010 glass, rides
a quarter-car: a body on one sprung wheel whose rim rolls on a speed bump, a kerb, cobbles or an ISO 8608 road, pulled along by a drive,
and pushed by the reader. The road pushes the wheel along the normal to its surface; the horizontal part of that push, the kick, is what
sloshes the stout. The stout sloshes in the three-dimensional linear modes of that cavity; a side view shows the cart and the glass (the
glass enlarged), a vertical cut shows the velocity field; every source of damping has its own term and switch. A strut actuator and the
drive motor under a skyhook damper or an LQR designed from the whole model, cart and stout together, keep the pint level. Catalog field
`control`. First version (hand-carried glass, then a carriage "waiter") 5 November 2026; a half-car in 3D (three.js, a Blender glass)
later that day; the one-wheel side view 6 November 2026, after Numa's reference: a cup on a one-wheel cart over a bumpy road.

## The glass (`geom.py`, `glass.py`, Blender)

No drawing of the Guinness glass is published; retailers give 160 × 85 mm and 568 mL. `profile_drawing.csv` is the elevation outline of
dimensions.com's "Irish Imperial Pint Glass" (149 mm, 76 mm, the 473 mL version) sampled from its vector drawing; `geom.py` scales it to
160 mm tall and 85.0 mm wide (two factors, 1.074 and 1.043), takes the wall 2.0 mm thick normal to the profile and the floor 8 mm above
the foot, and adds the two sculpted panels seen in product photographs of the clear glass: inside a V-shaped crease from 16 mm up at the
centre line to the sides at 80 mm, the wall is set back A = 2.0 mm (estimated from the photographs' shading; the one opinion in the geometry),
blending into the round body between 60 and 100 mm. Results: waist 61.5 mm at Z = 40 mm, widest 85.0 mm at 124.5 mm, rim 80.7 mm;
brim 576.2 mL (quadrature), Blender's closed mesh 575.3 mL at 64 segments (the polygon factor 0.9984 accounts for the difference); 568 mL at 158.2 mm (British pint glasses are brim measure); 500 mL at 144.1 mm.
The panels remove 14 mL from the 590 mL of the body of revolution. `glass.py` builds both surfaces as rings of R(z, θ) in Blender (bpy 5.2.3; the volume check on 0.5 mm rings, the exported
surfaces on 2 mm rings and 64 segments, 365 kB), measures the cavity volume independently, and exports `glass.glb`.

## The modes (`modes3d.py`, `check_modes3d.py`, `build_modes3d.py`)

The cavity is not a body of revolution, so the Steklov problem ∫∇Φ·∇v dV = λ∫_S Φv dS is solved in three dimensions on 27-node
(triquadratic) hexahedra mapped from (s, θ, ζ), r = s R_i(z, θ), the axis collapsed to one node per level, graded toward the surface; the
interior is condensed out (Schur complement) to a dense symmetric problem on the surface nodes. The same mesh gives the Stokes–Joukowski
potentials χ_x, χ_y (Laplace with ∂_nχ = (e × r)·n on the whole boundary, free surface included) for the rotation of the glass about its
foot. Per mode: λ, N = ∫ψ², Q_x, Q_y (translation), Q_rx, Q_ry (rotation), the wall, floor and surface integrals of |∇_tΦ|² over E, the
dominant azimuthal number and orientation (from the rim's Fourier components), and the shapes as Fourier series in θ. Modes are scaled
to max |ψ| = 1 on the rim, found from the rim's trigonometric interpolant, with the dominant component positive so that the sign is the
same from fill to fill. The page does not rely on that: `interpGlass()` aligns the next fill's shape with this one's by the sign of their
dot product before interpolating, and treats a Fourier component trimmed from one fill as zero there. (The quick-mesh file of the first
cart version predated the sign rule and flipped (1,1) between neighbouring fills, which cancelled the slosh halfway between them; the code
review of round 3 found it. The store's `rim_max` shows max|ψ(rim)| = 1 for every mode at every fill, and `test.cjs` checks it at 490 mL.)

Gate (`python3 tools/fig12/check_modes3d.py`, production mesh 8 × 28 × 18, 33 189 nodes, about 3 min per solve): cylinder R = 40 mm,
D = 100 mm, λ₁₁ within 6 × 10⁻⁶ of the exact k tanh kD, every kept mode (m ≤ 3) within 1.2 × 10⁻⁴; E = λN to 10⁻⁴; Q₁₁ within 1.5 × 10⁻⁶
of 2R/(ξ² − 1) (the wall-normalised value, 33.473 mm); the wall integral within 5 × 10⁻⁶ of the exact Bessel integral; the Stokes–Joukowski
inertia changes by 10⁻⁵ between the coarse and the production mesh. On the glass with the panels off, against the two-dimensional solver
`modes.py` (kept for this check): every kept mode within 1.6 × 10⁻⁴. PASS, 5 November 2026.

Finding: at the default fill the panels do nothing measurable (the x and y copies of (1,1) differ by 0.003 %, purity 1.0000); at 300 mL the
split is 0.1 %. The slosh lives in the top two centimetres and the panels end ten centimetres down. `trim_modes.py` therefore drops the
Fourier components below 2 × 10⁻³ of the dominant one (227 of 576 kept; the installed file is the production mesh, 475 kB).

## The plant (`plant.py`, `check_plant.py`, `road.py`)

Quarter-car: body M_b = 10 kg on one strut, k_s = 3000 N/m, c = 60 N s/m; one wheel of 1.5 kg and radius R_w = 80 mm on a tyre of 50 kN/m
(20 N s/m); a tray h_g = 0.25 m above the body's mass centre that stays level, with the glass, 0.42 kg, on it; the drive holds the speed v
with −c_drive ẋ_c, c_drive = 200 N s/m, and the wheel rolls with c_x = 5 N s/m. Coordinates (x_c, z_b, z_w, a_n), x_c the cart's lead on
its steady travel, X = vt + x_c the axle's position along the road. The stout's kinetic energy ½ρ∫|∇Φ|² with Φ = ẋ_c x + ż_b z +
Σ(ȧ_n/λ_n)Φ_n gives the coupling ρQ_nN_n between x_c and the x-modes and the modal masses (1 + κ)ρN/λ; ż_b couples to no wave (∫ψ_n = 0),
its effect is the pumping term. `check_plant.py`: hand-assembled M, C, K equal the SymPy Hessians exactly; the modal row equals (12) with
ẍ_O = ẍ_c to 10⁻¹⁶; a free roll over the bump keeps its energy to 6 × 10⁻⁶ and reaches √(v² − 2gh) at the top; the envelope at a sharp step
is the circle round the edge. Free motions at 500 mL: 2.54 Hz heave (ζ 0.15), the x- and y-slosh pairs, 29.5 Hz wheel hop. Sloshing masses
81 g and 2.3 g.

The road and the kick. `road.Axle` and the page's `buildRoad()` sample the road every 2 mm over 30 m and take the axle's path as the
envelope z_a(X) = max over |X − x′| ≤ R_w of [z_r(x′) + √(R_w² − (X − x′)²)] − R_w: the rim, not a point, meets the road, so a kerb is climbed
on a circle round its edge. The tyre load is N = Mg + k_t(z_a − z_w) + c_t(ż_a − ż_w), clamped at 0 (the wheel lifts; the linear tyre's
pull is cancelled on the wheel and the page counts the lift-offs); the road's push is along the normal of the axle's path, so its
horizontal part, the kick, is −N z_a′. Product of two changing quantities, it is a force added at every RK stage, not a matrix entry.
Over the 30 mm bump at 1.5 m/s it reaches 30 N; the crossing takes 0.33 s, the slowest slosh period is 0.31 s.

The page assembles the same matrices in `assemble()` and integrates `M q̈ + C q̇ + K q = F` with RK4 at 1 ms (dense M⁻¹ precomputed), the
kick, the Faraday term −ρN_n z̈_O a_n with z̈_O lagged one substep and clamped at −0.8 g, the controller held between samples (ZOH), and
the reader's push (drag sideways along the road up to 80 N, down on the body up to 150 N; buttons 40 N for 0.3 s along, 120 N down).
Modes past twice the radius are reset and the page says so. The rough road is the class-C sum of 120 sines scaled so that its highest
point in the first 3 m equals the slider (13 mm by default) and faded in over 0.2 m; `road.py` generates the same numbers. `restart()`
resets the road under the wheel and the stored forces.

The side view (`drawCart()`): the cart and the road to scale, the glass 3.2 times larger (said on the picture and in the store), cut
along the road; the view follows the cart, so the road slides past; the strut drawn longer than its working length; what runs over the
rim drawn as a few drops on a ballistic arc (a picture, the model stops at the rim); the kick, the motor and the reader's push as arrows,
1.5 px per newton. Read-backs: the wheel's hub at the stored axle height, and the stout under its surface at the front wall. Drag on the
picture to push; `touch-action: pan-y` keeps vertical swipes for the page.

## The control (`controller3.py`, `gate_ctl3.py`, `notes/fig12/gate-ctl3.cjs`)

Two moving-coil actuators, Bℓ = 20 N/A, I_max = 8 A (160 N each): u_s in the strut (up on the body, down on the wheel) and u_m, the drive
motor along the road. Skyhook: u_s = −c_sky ż_b, c_sky = 300 N s/m, motor idle. LQR in suspension coordinates (Hrovat) with the stout:
x = (d, w, a_n | ẋ_c, ż_b, ż_w, ȧ_n), d = z_b − z_w, w = z_w − z_a, 9 states for two x-modes; the cart's position is not a state (the motor
holds a speed, not a timetable; a position no force restores would be a marginal mode). In the linear model the strut cannot reach the
slosh at all (level tray, ∫ψ_n = 0); it acts only through the kick, by steadying N. Only the motor can answer the kick itself, and the
design finds that: its force mirrors the kick, and the cart's largest acceleration over the bump falls from 1.37 to 0.43 m/s². Cost
η_rim²/σ_η² + w²/σ_w² + ż_b²/σ_v² + u_s²/σ_u² + u_m²/σ_m² with σ_η = 0.5 mm, σ_w = 1 mm, σ_v = 50 mm/s, σ_u = σ_m = 20 N; A and B by
probing the linear map on unit states; ZOH over T_s = 10 ms by one matrix exponential; the discrete Riccati equation by iteration
(about 900 iterations cold, warm-started from the last solution), redone at the next sample after a slider moves; τ_cl = 1.12 s.

Gate: `node notes/fig12/gate-ctl3.cjs out.json` dumps the page's gains and nine runs (bump, kerb, cobbles × three laws);
`python3 tools/fig12/gate_ctl3.py out.json` designs the same controller offline and compares: gains to 2 × 10⁻⁹, τ_cl identical, every
peak rim wave within 0.1 % (the kick's sampled peak on the kerb differs by 5 %, because the two programs sample a 3 ms spike at different
instants). PASS.

Scenario numbers (`note_numbers.py`, 6 s runs, 500 mL, 1.5 m/s): bump 30 mm 19.8 / 13.5 / 0.9 mm (passive / skyhook / LQR), LQR 50 N in
the strut and 24 N in the motor; kerb 20 mm 12.9 / 9.9 / 2.8 mm, motor 121 N; cobbles 6 mm 1.8 / 1.8 / 0.7 mm; rough 13 mm 5.2 / 4.9 /
0.4 mm. On the bump the kick alone gives 10.6 mm, the pumping alone 0; slower or faster 4.4 mm at 0.5 m/s, 11.0 mm at 3 m/s; a drive of
2000 N s/m brings the passive cart to 2.5 mm, one of 20 N s/m lets it reach 23.8 mm. Head at rest 2.1 mm above the rim, dome limit 8 mm:
the LQR keeps the pint, the skyhook and the passive cart do not.

## The store and the data panel

`Sim.dataset` 'A pint of stout on a suspension', dims `k` (14 modes), `q` (17 coordinates), `j` (17 radial nodes), `kk` (12 gains per actuator).
Groups: what you set; the glass and the pour; the modes; the damping; the cart; the control; the state now; the drawing. 53 checks
(per-mode ones at three indices) recompute from the stored inputs; all agree in English, German and Arabic at 1440 and 390 px
(`node notes/fig12/test.cjs <out> <width> [de/|ar/]`), which also pushes the cart with a scripted drag on the side view and drives the bump
under the three laws, requiring passive > skyhook > LQR and the LQR under 2 mm; the kick's sign (back on the way up, forward on the way
down, nothing on a smooth road); the kerb's envelope; a quiet first sample after a restart; the rough road's height and start; the
deferred design; and max|ψ(rim)| = 1 between two fills. Read-backs (3 px probes, alpha first): the trace's last point, the stout under the
surface in the cut, the wheel's hub and the stout at the front wall in the side view. `node notes/fig12/regress.cjs`: the read-backs pass on
the drawing and fail on a blank canvas; a law switch does not kick the cart; the LQR converged; the straight glass is straight and
Bessel-exact; the road is flat before 1 m; a road change restarts; a released push is zero; the tyre never pulls over a 60 mm kerb.
`node notes/fig12/perf.cjs`: 27 frames per second on a 390 px phone window with the CPU slowed four times, simulated time at 0.96 of real.

## Values that remain uncertain

The panel depth (2 mm, from photographs); the head's liquid fraction (0.15, no source); the dome the head holds above the rim (8 mm, an
opinion on a slider); the transfer of Sauret et al.'s foam friction to a round glass (a factor of three either way); the contact-line rate
(0.05 1/s, from a water tank); Guinness's surface tension (beers 41–48 mN/m); the cart's own numbers, which are a choice, above all how firmly the drive holds the speed,
which decides how much of the kick the stout feels (a factor of ten between 20 and 2000 N s/m); the rigid wheel's normal for an elastic
tyre. The drawing's two scale factors (1.074 in height, 1.12 in width) are stated in the note.
