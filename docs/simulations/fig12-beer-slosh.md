# Fig. 12 · A Pint of Stout, Carried (`public/simulations/beer-slosh.html`)

A Guinness tulip pint glass with 500 mL of stout under 18 mm of head, carried by the reader's pointer or shaken by a shaker
table. The stout sloshes in the linear modes of the measured glass shape; a vertical cut shows the velocity field inside; every
source of damping has its own term and switch. Catalog field `control`: since the second version (5 November 2026, later) a waiter, a carriage driven by a moving-coil motor under an LQR controller, can carry the glass instead of the reader's hand (note section 10). Added 5 November 2026.

## The geometry pipeline (Blender and a photograph)

No drawing of the glass is published; retailers give 160 × 85 mm and 568 mL.
1. `profile_from_photo.py` reads a side-on product photograph (MBS Wholesale, `guiness_2.jpg`, 500 × 500 px), takes the outermost
   dark pixels of each row as the edge, and writes the half-width per row (`outer_from_photo.csv`).
2. `glass.py` rescales so that the rim plane and the foot plane (centres of the two ellipses the camera sees, rows 41.5 and 449.0)
   are 160 mm apart (0.3926 mm/px), drops the rows distorted by the ellipses, smooths with a spline to 0.2 mm rms, takes the
   wall 2.0 mm thick (normal to the wall) and the cavity floor 8 mm above the foot, and writes `glass_profile.json`. It then
   revolves the cavity in Blender (bpy 5.2.3, 96 segments) and measures its volume independently: 603.3 mL against 603.7 mL by
   integration (the difference is the polygon factor 0.99929). 568 mL reaches 151.9 mm, 8.1 mm below the rim. Widest outer
   diameter 84.1 mm (published 85 mm). It also exports `glass.glb`.

## The modes

`modes.py` solves the Steklov problem (note eq. 9–10) with quadratic triangles on a mesh mapped from (s, z), r = s R_i(z),
graded towards the surface, condensing the interior (Schur complement) to a dense symmetric problem on the surface nodes.
`check_cylinder.py` is its gate: eigenvalues of a 40 × 100 mm cylinder within 2.4 × 10⁻⁴ for nine modes (3 × 10⁻⁷ for (1,1)),
the forcing coefficient equal to 2R/((ξ²−1)J₁(ξ)) = 0.057528 m, and the damping integrals equal to the exact Bessel integrals
(Wolfram Language) to 7 × 10⁻⁵. Kept modes: (m, n) = (0,1), (0,2), (1,1), (1,2), (1,3), (2,1), (2,2), (3,1); with sin copies, 14 oscillators.

## The live model

Per oscillator, `ä + 2γȧ + ω²(1 + z̈/g)a = −λQẍ/(1 + κ)` (note eq. 12), with ω² = (gλ + σλ³/ρ)/(1 + κ), κ = M_f λ/ρ the head's
added mass (eq. 11), and γ = γ_w + γ_s + γ_v + γ_c + γ_f (eq. 14–18; store ids `g_w`, `g_s`, `g_b`, `g_f`, and `mu_f` for κ, kept from the first version). The glass follows the pointer through a critically damped follower of natural
frequency Ω_h; the shaker adds A w(t) sin(2πft) with w = 1 − e^(−t/0.3 s). Classical RK4, h = 1 ms, as many steps per frame as
real time passed (capped at 50 ms). For the straight glass the page computes the same quantities from Bessel functions.

Gate of the live integrator: `node notes/fig12/gate.cjs out.json x 0.2 f1 8` then `python3 tools/fig12/check_dynamics.py out.json`:
the page's RK4 against SciPy DOP853 (rtol 10⁻¹¹), largest difference 5 × 10⁻⁸ of the largest amplitude for the sideways
resonance, 7 × 10⁻⁷ for the Faraday run (5 November 2026).

## The store and the data panel

`Sim.dataset` 'A pint of stout, carried', dims `k` (8 radial shapes), `osc` (16 slots: cos copies then sin copies), `j` (25 radii).
Groups: what you set; the glass and the pour; the modes; the damping; the state now; the drawing. Checks recompute from the
stored inputs: D, ν, μ(T), M_f, κ, ω, f, γ_w, γ_s, γ_v, γ_f, γ, ζ, M_e, M, I, g_eff, η_max, Z_l, Z_h (38 checks counting the
per-mode ones at three indices, all identical in English, German and Arabic at 1440 and 390 px, `node notes/fig12/test.cjs`). The groups
include "The waiter". Read-backs: the last point of the wall trace (ink at the predicted height) and the
stout colour within 3 px of a point 4 px under the surface on the axis of the cut (3 px so that a velocity arrow
crossing the point does not fail it; a 1 px probe failed once in about 300 samples). Read values pins a grid point of the cut and shows u_r, u_θ, u_z. One round
is one period of mode (1,1).

## Values that remain uncertain

The head's liquid fraction (0.15, no source), the transfer of Sauret et al.'s foam friction from a 3 mm-bubble slot to a
round glass of stout (a factor of three either way), the contact-line rate (0.05 1/s, from a water tank), and Guinness's own
surface tension (beers 41–48 mN/m). The glass profile carries the photograph's perspective (widest diameter 84.1 mm against the
published 85 mm).

## The waiter (section 10 of the note)

`design()` builds, for the present fill and stout, the state-space model of the carriage and the three m = 1 modes per axis: the
carriage equation (19), M ẍ + Σ ρπ Q_n N_n ä_n = F with N_n = ∫ ψ_n² r dr (Simpson on the 25 stored radii), reduced to (20) with
the sloshing masses m_n; the modal equation (12). It discretises with a zero-order hold over T_s (matrix exponential by scaling and
squaring), solves the discrete Riccati equation by iteration, and estimates the slowest closed-loop time constant from Gelfand's
formula. Every T_s, `waiterForce()` sets F = M r̈ − K(s − s_path), limited to Bℓ I_max, for x (cos copies) and y (sin copies); the
planned path follows the target with rate Ω_r. Carriage 0.60 kg, glass 0.42 kg, Bℓ = 12 N/A (page constants, P.Mc, P.mg, P.Bl).
Offline reference: `tools/fig12/controller.py`; `tools/fig12/waiter_fig.py` draws Figure 5. Gate: `node notes/fig12/gate-waiter.cjs out.json`
gives the page's gains (equal to SciPy's to 3e-10) and a 20 cm carry (peak 2.626 mm, arrival 1.453 s, both as offline).

## Reviews (5 November 2026)

Two rounds of independent review of the note (referee, `notes/fig12/referee-r1.md`, `referee-r2.md`) and of the code
(`code-review-r1.md`, `code-review-r2.md`), and one of the waiter (`waiter-review.md`); each finding fixed or answered.
`node notes/fig12/regress.cjs` re-runs the code findings as tests and exits 1 on a failure. Kept on purpose: the 3D view keeps
`touch-action: none`, so a finger on it carries the glass in both directions on a phone (the page scrolls everywhere else);
per-frame allocations in the drawing are small and were measured not to matter.
