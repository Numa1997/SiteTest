# Glossary: English, German, Arabic

The words the site uses for physics and for its own parts, in the three languages, so that every page says the same thing the
same way. The German column uses the terms of German university physics, the Arabic column those of Arabic university physics as
taught in the Levant, with the Egyptian variant where it differs and is common. The terms were chosen while translating
(23 October 2026) and have not yet been checked entry by entry against a published dictionary; a correction goes here first,
then into the tables. Machine tables: [i18n/de/strings.json](../i18n/de/strings.json),
[i18n/ar/strings.json](../i18n/ar/strings.json); how they are used: [i18n.md](i18n.md).

## Writing conventions

- Numbers keep Western digits and the decimal point in all three languages, as the figures, the maths and the data panel print
  them; SI units with a space. In Arabic text the generator keeps each number with its unit left to right.
- German addresses the reader as "Sie".
- Arabic has no italic and no letter-spacing; names of people are written in Arabic letters in running text (هويغنز، ستوكس)
  and stay Latin where they label a product, an app or a file (Droplet Collection, Rotor Pendula, Python, SciPy), in headings too.
- Mechanics and dynamics are الميكانيك and الديناميك (Levant forms), on every page.
- One simulation has one name: the home page plates use the catalog's titles.
- The name: نعمة سائر قدسية on Arabic pages, Numa Koudsie in English and German.
- "Take control" is تولَّ التحكّم (4 November 2026). The first rendering, تحكّم بنفسك, is a word-for-word copy and means "restrain yourself, keep your temper" in Arabic. The noun التحكّم is the one Microsoft's Arabic Teams pages use for "control" (طلب / منح / استعادة التحكم, support.microsoft.com/ar-sa); the verb تولَّ ("take over") is the one DeepL gives in its idiom for "take the controls". The phrase as a whole is not printed in either source.
