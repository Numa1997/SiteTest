Two rods on a cart: every term, and how each one was checked

What this is. The equations behind Fig. 11 are derived here from the setup up, one term at a time. Each result is followed by an independent check: a symbolic derivation in Mathematica that never saw the hand derivation, a comparison with a printed source, or a limit we already know. The second half covers the new work, getting the rods back up after they have fallen. It explains why the old "60° and give up" rule is wrong, what a certified catch region is, how the swing-up is planned, and what happens for arbitrary pushes. Every statement is marked FACT (computed or sourced, with a handle), INFERENCE (my reasoning from facts) or CHOICE (a number I picked, with its measured effect).

01The machine, and what moves

A cart of mass \(m_0\) rolls on a horizontal rail. Its position is \(x\), and a motor pushes it with a horizontal force \(u\), \(|u|\le u_\text{max}\). A free hinge on the cart carries a uniform rod (mass \(m_1\), length \(L_1\)). A second free hinge at that rod's top carries another uniform rod (\(m_2\), \(L_2\)). Each angle \(\theta_i\) is measured from the upward vertical and is positive leaning right. Three numbers fix the configuration, \(\boldsymbol\theta=(x,\theta_1,\theta_2)\), and six fix the state, \(\mathbf s=(\boldsymbol\theta,\dot{\boldsymbol\theta})\).

Picture it as a broom on your palm with a second broom hinged to its top. You can only move your hand sideways along a line. Moving the hand right drags the bottom of the lower rod right, so that rod turns left about its own centre of mass. The upper rod feels this only through the middle hinge. One input therefore reaches three coordinates, indirectly and differently. That is what underactuated means.

02Kinetic and potential energy

The origin is at the lower hinge, with \(y\) pointing up. A rod leaning by \(\theta\) puts its far end \(L\sin\theta\) to the side and \(L\cos\theta\) up. A uniform rod has its centre of mass at its middle, so

\[ x_1=x+\tfrac12L_1\sin\theta_1,\quad y_1=\tfrac12L_1\cos\theta_1,\qquad x_2=x+L_1\sin\theta_1+\tfrac12L_2\sin\theta_2,\quad y_2=L_1\cos\theta_1+\tfrac12L_2\cos\theta_2. \tag{1}\]

Take the time derivative (chain rule: \(\tfrac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dt}\sin\theta=\dot\theta\cos\theta\)), then square and add. With \(\cos a\cos b+\sin a\sin b=\cos(a-b)\) this gives

\[ v_1^2=\dot x^2+L_1\dot x\dot\theta_1\cos\theta_1+\tfrac14L_1^2\dot\theta_1^2,\] \[ v_2^2=\dot x^2+L_1^2\dot\theta_1^2+\tfrac14L_2^2\dot\theta_2^2+2L_1\dot x\dot\theta_1\cos\theta_1+L_2\dot x\dot\theta_2\cos\theta_2+L_1L_2\dot\theta_1\dot\theta_2\cos(\theta_1-\theta_2). \tag{2}\]

By König's theorem, a moving, turning body carries the kinetic energy of its centre of mass, \(\tfrac12mv^2\), plus that of turning about the centre of mass, \(\tfrac12I\dot\theta^2\). For a uniform rod, \(I=\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}\frac mL s^2\,\mathrm ds=\frac{mL^2}{12}\).

Check 2.1FACTWolfram: Integrate[(m/L) s^2, {s,-L/2,L/2}] → L^2 m/12, and about the end, Integrate[(m/L) s^2,{s,0,L}] → L^2 m/3. Wolfram|Alpha itself could not be reached from either road I tried (see §11), so the integral was run in the Wolfram Language kernel instead.
\[ T=\tfrac12m_0\dot x^2+\tfrac12m_1v_1^2+\tfrac1{24}m_1L_1^2\dot\theta_1^2+\tfrac12m_2v_2^2+\tfrac1{24}m_2L_2^2\dot\theta_2^2=\tfrac12\dot{\boldsymbol\theta}^{\mathsf T}\mathbf D(\boldsymbol\theta)\dot{\boldsymbol\theta}, \tag{3}\] \[ \mathbf D=\begin{pmatrix}d_1&d_2\cos\theta_1&d_3\cos\theta_2\\ d_2\cos\theta_1&d_4&d_5\cos(\theta_1-\theta_2)\\ d_3\cos\theta_2&d_5\cos(\theta_1-\theta_2)&d_6\end{pmatrix},\qquad V=f_1\cos\theta_1+f_2\cos\theta_2, \tag{4}\] \[ d_1=m_0+m_1+m_2,\ d_2=(\tfrac12m_1+m_2)L_1,\ d_3=\tfrac12m_2L_2,\ d_4=(\tfrac13m_1+m_2)L_1^2,\ d_5=\tfrac12m_2L_1L_2,\ d_6=\tfrac13m_2L_2^2,\ f_{1}=(\tfrac12m_1+m_2)L_1g,\ f_2=\tfrac12m_2L_2g. \tag{5}\]

Each constant has a meaning you can see. \(d_1\) is all the mass that moves when the cart moves. \(d_4\) is the lower rod turning about its bottom end, \(\tfrac13m_1L_1^2\), plus the upper rod's whole mass carried at radius \(L_1\). The coefficient \(\tfrac16\) comes from adding \(\tfrac18\) (centre-of-mass motion) and \(\tfrac1{24}\) (spin), and it equals \(\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac13\). \(d_2\) couples the cart with the lower angle: the lower rod's mass acts at its middle and the upper rod's mass at its top. \(f_1,f_2\) are the weights times the heights of the centres of mass at \(\theta=0\). \(V\) is largest when both rods stand up, so the balance point is the top of an energy hill.

03Equations of motion

Lagrange's equations are \(\frac{\mathrm d}{\mathrm dt}\partial_{\dot q_i}\mathcal L-\partial_{q_i}\mathcal L=Q_i\) with \(\mathcal L=T-V\). The generalised force \(Q_i\) is the work the outside forces do in a small change \(\delta q_i\), divided by \(\delta q_i\). The motor does work \(u\,\delta x\) and nothing when only an angle changes, so \(\mathbf Q=(u,0,0)^{\mathsf T}\). The hinge forces never appear, because they act at points that do not slide along them and so do no work. Here is the \(x\) row worked out. \(\partial_{\dot x}T=d_1\dot x+d_2\cos\theta_1\dot\theta_1+d_3\cos\theta_2\dot\theta_2\) is the total sideways momentum, and \(\partial_xT=\partial_xV=0\). Differentiating each product in time gives

\[ d_1\ddot x+d_2\cos\theta_1\ddot\theta_1+d_3\cos\theta_2\ddot\theta_2-d_2\sin\theta_1\dot\theta_1^2-d_3\sin\theta_2\dot\theta_2^2=u. \tag{6}\]

This is Newton's law for the whole machine sideways. The \(\sin\cdot\dot\theta^2\) terms are the sideways parts of the centripetal accelerations of the two centres of mass. The angle rows go the same way (the lecture note on the page shows every cancellation). Together they give

\[ \mathbf D(\boldsymbol\theta)\ddot{\boldsymbol\theta}+\mathbf C(\boldsymbol\theta,\dot{\boldsymbol\theta})\dot{\boldsymbol\theta}+\mathbf G(\boldsymbol\theta)=\mathbf Hu,\quad \mathbf C=\begin{pmatrix}0&-d_2\sin\theta_1\dot\theta_1&-d_3\sin\theta_2\dot\theta_2\\0&0&d_5\sin(\theta_1-\theta_2)\dot\theta_2\\0&-d_5\sin(\theta_1-\theta_2)\dot\theta_1&0\end{pmatrix},\ \mathbf G=\begin{pmatrix}0\\-f_1\sin\theta_1\\-f_2\sin\theta_2\end{pmatrix},\ \mathbf H=\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\\0\end{pmatrix}. \tag{7}\]

04Near the top: linearisation, fall rates, steerability

Write every angle and rate as \(O(\epsilon)\). Then \(\sin\theta=\theta+O(\epsilon^3)\), \(\cos\theta=1+O(\epsilon^2)\), and every entry of \(\mathbf C\dot{\boldsymbol\theta}\) is a sine times a squared rate, so \(O(\epsilon^3)\). To first order,

\[ \mathbf D_0\ddot{\boldsymbol\theta}=\mathbf F\boldsymbol\theta+\mathbf Hu,\quad \mathbf F=\operatorname{diag}(0,f_1,f_2),\qquad \dot{\mathbf s}=\mathbf A\mathbf s+\mathbf Bu,\ \ \mathbf A=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf0&\mathbf I\\ \mathbf D_0^{-1}\mathbf F&\mathbf0\end{pmatrix},\ \mathbf B=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf0\\ \mathbf D_0^{-1}\mathbf H\end{pmatrix}. \tag{8}\]

05The balancing controller (LQR)

The controller is \(u=-\mathbf K\mathbf s\), with \(\mathbf K=\rho^{-1}\mathbf B^{\mathsf T}\mathbf P\). Here \(\mathbf P\) is the positive-definite solution of the Riccati equation \(\mathbf A^{\mathsf T}\mathbf P+\mathbf P\mathbf A-\rho^{-1}\mathbf P\mathbf B\mathbf B^{\mathsf T}\mathbf P+\mathbf Q=0\), which minimises \(J=\int(\mathbf s^{\mathsf T}\mathbf Q\mathbf s+\rho u^2)\,\mathrm dt\) (derived on the page, §7.1). The property used below is that \(W(\mathbf s)=\mathbf s^{\mathsf T}\mathbf P\mathbf s\) is the cost still to pay from \(\mathbf s\). For the linear closed loop \(\mathbf A_\text{cl}=\mathbf A-\mathbf B\mathbf K\), this cost always falls:

\[ \dot W=\mathbf s^{\mathsf T}(\mathbf A_\text{cl}^{\mathsf T}\mathbf P+\mathbf P\mathbf A_\text{cl})\mathbf s=-\mathbf s^{\mathsf T}(\mathbf Q+\rho\mathbf K^{\mathsf T}\mathbf K)\mathbf s<0. \tag{10}\]

Equation (10) is the Riccati equation rearranged: expand \(\mathbf A_\text{cl}\) and insert \(\mathbf K=\rho^{-1}\mathbf B^{\mathsf T}\mathbf P\).

07Why the rods fell and stayed down: the old rule was wrong

FACTThe page currently stops the controller when a rod passes 60° (LOST = Math.PI/3, line 396 of the page) and does nothing afterwards. Your report ("as soon as the arms go down it stops balancing") is exactly that line. INFERENCEThe 60° was not derived from anything. Near the top, a linear controller can only catch states from which a force of at most \(u_\text{max}\) still wins. That set depends on both angles and all three rates, and on \(u_\text{max}\). It is not a cone of angles. Measured from rest with the default machine, the largest recoverable lean is 14°–44° at 60 N, depending on direction, 10.6°–32° at 30 N, and 26°–48° at 200 N. So some leans under 60° were already hopeless, and some states past 60° could have been saved. Once the rods hang, only a different strategy can bring them back up: a swing-up.

08A certified catch region

We want to know when it is safe to hand over to the balancing controller. Use \(W=\mathbf s^{\mathsf T}\mathbf P\mathbf s\) from §5. Its level sets \(\{W=c\}\) are nested ellipsoids around the top. Along the true nonlinear motion with the limited force \(u=\operatorname{sat}(-\mathbf K\mathbf s)\),

\[ \dot W=2\,\mathbf s^{\mathsf T}\mathbf P\,\mathbf f\big(\mathbf s,\operatorname{sat}_{u_\text{max}}(-\mathbf K\mathbf s)\big), \tag{11}\]

where \(\mathbf f\) is the full right-hand side of (7). Here is the argument, Lyapunov's level-set argument. Suppose \(\dot W<0\) at every point of the surface \(W=c\). A motion starting inside can then never cross that surface outward, because at the crossing point \(W\) would have to increase. 09Energy: how a cart can pump or drain the rods

Your idea was to shake the cart back and forth until resonance appears, or slowly calm the rods with the integral of the forces. Its exact form is the energy of the two rods alone. Treat the cart's acceleration \(a=\ddot x\) as the input. The rods' energy, kinetic energy of turning plus potential energy, is

\[ E_p=\tfrac12\dot{\boldsymbol\varphi}^{\mathsf T}\mathbf D_{pp}\dot{\boldsymbol\varphi}+f_1\cos\theta_1+f_2\cos\theta_2,\qquad \mathbf D_{pp}=\begin{pmatrix}d_4&d_5\cos(\theta_1-\theta_2)\\ d_5\cos(\theta_1-\theta_2)&d_6\end{pmatrix}. \tag{12}\]

Differentiate along rows 2–3 of (7). The centripetal terms do no work, because \(\mathbf C\) is built so that \(\dot{\mathbf D}-2\mathbf C\) is skew. Gravity's work is already inside \(V\). What remains is the cart's push on the hinge:

\[ \dot E_p=-a\,\mathcal P,\qquad \mathcal P=d_2\cos\theta_1\,\dot\theta_1+d_3\cos\theta_2\,\dot\theta_2. \tag{13}\]
Check 9.1FACTWolfram: \(\dot E_p+a\mathcal P\) simplifies to 0 exactly, for symbolic parameters.
Check 9.2 · source and limitFACTWith \(m_2\to0\), \(m_1=m\) and \(L_1=2l\), (13) becomes \(\dot E=-m\,a\,l\,\dot\theta\cos\theta\). Åström and Furuta [3], eq. (3), print \(\mathrm dE/\mathrm dt=-mul\dot\theta\cos\theta\) for the single pendulum with pivot acceleration \(u\). Same formula.

Read (13) as a picture. \(\mathcal P\) is the sideways momentum the rods carry because they turn. Accelerating the cart against it, \(a\propto-\mathcal P\), pumps energy in, the way you pump a swing. Accelerating with it, \(a=k\mathcal P\), drains energy at the rate \(k\mathcal P^2\ge0\), which calms the rods. Pumping alone does not choose which way up each rod ends. My own energy-pumping tests (yesterday) never reached the top: they ended near hanging, ran off the rail, or stopped with one rod up and one down. Draining always works, because the lowest energy is the hanging rest.

10The swing-up: plan, track, catch

Plan. From the state the machine is in right now (the six numbers), find a force history \(u(t)\), \(0\le t\le T\), that ends with both rods upright and at rest. The cart must stay within ±1.5 m and the force within \(u_\text{max}\). Shooting the whole history from the start fails: near the top a small error grows like \(e^{10t}\). So I use multiple shooting. Split time into \(M=10T\) intervals of \(\Delta=0.1\) s. The unknowns are the states \(\mathbf z_1,\dots,\mathbf z_M\) at the interval boundaries and one constant force \(u_k\) per interval. Each interval is integrated with RK4 (8 substeps in the study, 4 on the page) to get \(\Phi(\mathbf z_k,u_k)\). Then Levenberg–Marquardt minimises the sum of squares of

\[ \begin{aligned} &w_d\big(\mathbf z_{k+1}-\Phi(\mathbf z_k,u_k)\big) && \text{defects}\\ &w_T\big(\theta_{1,M}-\theta_1^\ast,\ \theta_{2,M}-\theta_2^\ast,\ \dot\theta_{1,M},\ \dot\theta_{2,M},\ 0.3\,\dot x_M\big) && \text{end upright, at rest}\\ &w_\ell\max(0,|x_k|-1.5\,\mathrm m),\quad w_u\max(0,|u_k|-u_\text{max}) && \text{soft limits}\\ &w_r\,u_k,\quad w_s\,(u_k-u_{k-1}) && \text{gentle forces} \end{aligned} \tag{14}\]

The first group, the defects, says "obey the equations of motion". The second says "end upright at rest", with targets \(\theta^\ast=2\pi\,\mathrm{round}(\theta/2\pi)\). The rest are soft limits plus a small preference for gentle forces. The starting guess is a straight line in the angles. CHOICEThe weights \(w_d=100,\ w_T=30,\ w_\ell=20,\ w_u=1,\ w_r=w_s=0.02\) are mine. They make a defect of 1 mrad cost as much as 3 mrad of terminal error, so physics wins. A plan is accepted when every defect is below \(3\times10^{-3}\).

Track. Played back open loop, a plan ends tens of metres away (measured: the cart at −46 m). Near the top errors grow like \(e^{10.4t}\), tenfold every 0.22 s (§4). So a time-varying LQR follows it. Linearise the motion along the plan every 10 ms, run the discrete Riccati recursion backwards from the balancing \(\mathbf P\) at the end, and apply \(u=u_\text{plan}(t)-\mathbf K(t)(\mathbf s-\mathbf s_\text{plan}(t))\). CHOICEThe tracking weights are per 10 ms step, \(\mathbf Q\) and \(R=1\).

Catch. As soon as \(W<0.9\,c^*\) (§8), the balancing LQR takes over. If the plan ends before that, the balancer gets its chance and the 4 s forecast decides.
