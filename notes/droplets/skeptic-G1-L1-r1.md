## What I checked and found right

- (L1.1) and the Davies number: sympy gives C_D·Re² = 4ρ_aΔρ g d³/(3μ²). The Stokes limit C_D = 24/Re gives v = 2Δρ g a²/(9μ) and N_Da = 24 Re.
  The dimensional count (5 quantities, 3 units, 2 groups) is right for a rigid sphere.
- My own code reproduces every number: ρ_a = 1.0458 kg/m³; μ = 1.7711e-5 Pa s; Stokes Re = 1 at a = 40.99 µm; Stokes v(100 µm) = 1.2296 m/s against
  Beard 0.7277 m/s (+69 %); ℓ = 0.0756 µm; Kn = 0.0094; C_sc(16 µm) = 1.0119; τ_p = 20.1 ms; spacing 1.494 mm = 187 radii; W = 0.6434 g/m³;
  Bo(3 mm, 20 °C) = 1.617; joint jumps −0.165 %, −0.193 %, −0.017 %, +0.003 %; Stokes/Beard ratios 0.988, 1.111, 1.273, 1.690, 2.939, 7.226;
  the seven terms at d = 0.2 mm: −3.187, 5.896, −0.054, −0.207, −0.720, 0.632, −0.144.
- Coefficients: all thirteen match disdrodb `rain.py` (b₆ included) and the dossier's OCR table. IAPWS σ: the formula matches R1-76(2014). Regime ranges match Beard's abstract.
- Steady fall is justified by numbers (τ_p ≈ 20 ms against growth over hundreds of seconds). The terminal-speed balance is a legitimate quasi-steady law here.
- §8's warning against extrapolation is right and, if anything, too mild (#7, #16).
