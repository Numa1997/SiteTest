## Notes (none changes a verdict)

1. "Doubling the speed of a 100 um drop multiplies its drag by 2.44". 2.44 is 2^n with the local exponent n = d ln F/d ln v = 1.289 on Beard's curve (2^1.289 = 2.444). The actual factor for a finite doubling along the curve is 2.505 (Schiller-Naumann: 2.484). The "quarter of the way from 2 to 4" is right for the exponent (n = 1.29 lies 29 % of the way from 1 to 2). The wording is slightly loose (2.44 versus 2.50, 2.5 %).
2. Single-collector times reproduced: 9.955 min (Stokes), 13.019 min (drag balance), ratio 1.308 and bounds 1.117 to 1.696 (text 9.96, 13.02, 1.31, 1.12, 1.70). The weighted-mean argument is right: both times use the same positive weight w(R) = 4 rho R^2/((R+r)^2 W) (E = 1).
3. Beard's curve itself is a fit (cannot be derived); only its consistency with (2.4)/(2.5) and with an independent correlation was tested. Its comparison with Gunn and Kinzer's data was not re-done here (no data file in scope).
4. Re = 15 for Stokes' speed at 100 um: (100/40.99)^3 = 14.5, "15" is a round-up.
