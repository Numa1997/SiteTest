The question is how encounters transfer mass between sizes, and how ice collisions can change particle number without creating mass. Start with the guided theory: the cloud context, notation and encounter geometry are introduced before the forces, population balances and numerical scheme. The solver explores a prescribed homogeneous box. Its size threshold and model assumptions are explicit; the curves are not a prediction of rainfall from a real cloud.

02The model changes and where to understand them

derived equations · adopted closures · conditional results

  1. Forces and speed. Derive terminal settling, check the Reynolds number, then distinguish the finite-Reynolds empirical fit from a first-principles result. Chapters 3–4.
  2. Encounter and merger. Airflow deflection and liquid coalescence are different processes. The Stokes and energy ratios are defined before the efficiencies. Chapters 5–6.
  3. Populations. Derive the SCE from binary gains and losses, and recover collector drift through a small-jump expansion. Chapters 7–8.
  4. Numerics. Define the logarithmic mass spectrum, derive the two-bin deposition and limiter, and distinguish conserving moments from resolving the spectrum. Chapter 9.
  5. Ice and interpretation. The app uses event-based fragmentation, while the thesis appendix implements time-based loss. Define the size diagnostic and reservoirs before reading the curves. Chapters 10–12.

Built on the Bachelor thesis Collisional Ice Multiplication Processes in Clouds (N. Koudsie, Leipzig 2022).
