Abstract

Computed On 18 physically admissible mountings, a single pendulum supplies 9.55–57.1 mW and exceeds the equal-mass, equal-reach double pendulum at every point. Double/single ratios are 0.6351–0.7354. A matched nonlinear example gives 0.379 W on a regular orbit and approximately 0.244 W on a longer chaotic window. Chaos confers no advantage in this comparison.

Audit note. One of the 18 is not quite admissible: at 400 rpm with the pivot at 90 %, the pendulum lying along the blade reaches 1.403 m on a 1.337 m blade, 66 mm past the tip. Every pivot is inside the rotor, as the report says; the bob is not.

Derived The generator removes energy from the rotor. At fixed angular speed, the rotor must replace the harvested energy plus any change in stored mechanical energy. This mechanism cannot increase net turbine output.

Assumed A 50% electrical conversion efficiency and a specified low-duty-cycle sensor schedule are reference design assumptions. Derived Datasheet typical currents give a 37.584 $\mu$W reference load. Computed Ideal power is sufficient, but centrifugal acceleration excludes many mountings for the specified accelerometer.

Question and scope

Assumed Consider a sensor node on a blade whose hub turns at prescribed constant angular speed. A pendulum attached to the blade responds to gravity and the acceleration of its moving pivot. Generators brake joint motion. The useful question is whether this small, local power supply is worth its mechanical complexity, rather than whether it creates additional turbine energy.

Assumed The ideal model uses point masses, rigid massless rods, a planar rotor, viscous generator torques and no aerodynamic forces, bearing friction, electrical thresholds or structural flexibility. The single comparator places the same total mass at the same total reach. These assumptions make the mechanical comparison controlled; they do not describe a qualified blade installation.

Computed The supplied three numerical anchors were reproduced before modifying the study. Corrected results come from 111 nonlinear runs and 49 linear evaluations. All 54 original chaos-scan parameter sets were rerun. The five unfinished legacy middle-joint exponents remain explicitly “not measured”.

Derived Every substantive statement is marked Derived (algebra or source-based calculation), Computed (numerical result), or Assumed (scenario or design choice). Source specifications are used as stated typical values, not measurements of a prototype.

1 Geometry and the Lagrangian

Assumed Let $s=\Omega t$, $\mathbf e(a)=(\cos a,\sin a)$, and take inertial $+y$ upwards. Both absolute rod angles $\theta_i$ increase anticlockwise from $+x$. The pivot, first mass and second mass have positions

$$\begin{align} \mathbf x_p&=r_p\mathbf e(s),& \mathbf x_1&=\mathbf x_p+l_1\mathbf e(\theta_1),& \mathbf x_2&=\mathbf x_1+l_2\mathbf e(\theta_2).\tag{1}\end{align}$$

Derived Write $M=m_1+m_2$, $L=l_1+l_2$, $A_d=Ml_1^2$, $B_d=m_2l_2^2$, $D_d=m_2l_1l_2$ and $\delta=\theta_1-\theta_2$. Differentiating the positions and using $\mathbf e'(a)\cdot\mathbf e'(b)=\cos(a-b)$ gives

$$\begin{align} T={}&\tfrac12M\Omega^2r_p^2+\tfrac12 A_d\dot\theta_1^2+\tfrac12B_d\dot\theta_2^2+D_d\cos\delta\,\dot\theta_1\dot\theta_2\notag\\ &+\Omega r_p\big[Ml_1\dot\theta_1\cos(\theta_1-s)+m_2l_2\dot\theta_2\cos(\theta_2-s)\big],\tag{2}\\ V={}&g\big[Mr_p\sin s+Ml_1\sin\theta_1+m_2l_2\sin\theta_2\big].\tag{3}\end{align}$$

Derived The cross terms in $T$ are essential: each mass moves both with the pivot and relative to it. Dropping those terms would lose the forcing caused by the rotor. The gravity potential is the sum of each mass times its inertial height.

Assumed With damping coefficients $c_0,c_1\ge0$, Rayleigh’s dissipation function is

$$\begin{equation} \mathcal R=\tfrac12c_0(\dot\theta_1-\Omega)^2+\tfrac12c_1(\dot\theta_2-\dot\theta_1)^2.\tag{4}\end{equation}$$

Derived The dissipative generalized torque is $-\partial\mathcal R/\partial\dot\theta_i$. An ideal generator converts precisely this dissipated mechanical power into harvest. A real generator has additional losses, accounted for later through an assumed efficiency.

2 Equations and dimensionless coordinates

Derived Apply $\frac{d}{dt}\partial T/\partial\dot\theta_i-\partial T/\partial\theta_i+\partial V/\partial\theta_i+\partial\mathcal R/\partial\dot\theta_i=0$. The two inertial equations are

$$\begin{align} 0={}&A_d\ddot\theta_1+D_d\cos\delta\,\ddot\theta_2+D_d\sin\delta\,\dot\theta_2^2\notag\\ &+Ml_1[\Omega^2r_p\sin(\theta_1-s)+g\cos\theta_1]\notag\\ &+c_0(\dot\theta_1-\Omega)+c_1(\dot\theta_1-\dot\theta_2),\tag{5}\\ 0={}&B_d\ddot\theta_2+D_d\cos\delta\,\ddot\theta_1-D_d\sin\delta\,\dot\theta_1^2\notag\\ &+m_2l_2[\Omega^2r_p\sin(\theta_2-s)+g\cos\theta_2]+c_1(\dot\theta_2-\dot\theta_1).\tag{6}\end{align}$$

Derived For example, the $D_d\sin\delta\,\dot\theta_2^2$ term combines derivatives of the cross kinetic energy. The $\Omega^2r_p$ terms result from pivot acceleration and pull the arms towards the outward blade direction. Gravity supplies time-periodic forcing in blade coordinates.

Derived Define $q_i=\theta_i-s$, $u_i=dq_i/ds$, $a=l_1/L$, $b=l_2/L$, $\beta=m_2/M$, and

$$\begin{equation} R=\frac{r_p}{L},\quad G=\frac{g}{\Omega^2L},\quad\Gamma=\frac{\Omega^2r_p}{g}=\frac RG,\quad \mu=\frac{m_2}{m_1},\quad\lambda_\ell=\frac{l_2}{l_1},\quad\kappa_i=\frac{c_i}{ML^2\Omega}.\tag{7}\end{equation}$$

Derived $R$ measures pivot radius relative to pendulum reach; $G$ measures gravity relative to rotor acceleration at that reach; $\Gamma$ is the centrifugal/gravitational acceleration ratio. The rotor radius is denoted $R_{\rm rot}$, and the turbine tip-speed ratio $\lambda_T$, avoiding two different uses of $R$ and $\lambda$.

Derived Divide the equations by $ML^2\Omega^2$, set $A=a^2$, $B=\beta b^2$, $D=\beta ab$, and $d=q_1-q_2$. Then

$$\begin{align} Aq_1''+D\cos d\,q_2''={}&-D\sin d(1+u_2)^2-a[R\sin q_1+G\cos(s+q_1)]\notag\\ &-\kappa_0u_1+\kappa_1(u_2-u_1),\tag{8}\\ Bq_2''+D\cos d\,q_1''={}&D\sin d(1+u_1)^2-\beta b[R\sin q_2+G\cos(s+q_2)]\notag\\ &-\kappa_1(u_2-u_1).\tag{9}\end{align}$$

Derived This is the unchanged supplied model. The determinant $AB-D^2\cos^2d$ is positive for positive masses and rods. Both generators use $c_0=c_1=c$; pivot-only uses $c_1=0$; middle-only uses $c_0=0$.

3 Energy cost and linear response

Derived The ideal mechanical harvest and dimensionless power are

$$\begin{equation} P_h=c_0\Omega^2u_1^2+c_1\Omega^2(u_2-u_1)^2,\qquad \widehat P=\frac{P_h}{ML^2\Omega^3}.\tag{10}\end{equation}$$

Derived Reconstruct the pivot reaction from Newton’s law. Its transverse force combination and the torque on the rotor are

$$\begin{align} F_\perp={}&Ml_1(\ddot\theta_1\cos q_1-\dot\theta_1^2\sin q_1) +m_2l_2(\ddot\theta_2\cos q_2-\dot\theta_2^2\sin q_2)+Mg\cos s,\tag{11}\\ \tau_{\rm rotor}={}&-r_pF_\perp+c_0\Omega u_1,\qquad P_{\rm rotor}=-\Omega\tau_{\rm rotor}.\tag{12}\end{align}$$

Derived Substitution into $d(T+V)/dt$ gives

$$\begin{equation} \frac{dE}{dt}=P_{\rm rotor}-P_h,\qquad \overline P_{\rm rotor}=\overline P_h+\frac{E(t_b)-E(t_a)}{t_b-t_a}.\tag{13}\end{equation}$$

Derived A chaotic finite window can store energy; its mean rotor input need not equal its mean harvest exactly. For a period-one steady orbit, the storage term vanishes. Gravity is conservative and returns to the same rotor phase each period: it does not supply a net free energy source.

Reading note. The conclusion holds; the reason given is looser than it looks. Gravity’s work is minus the change of the masses’ gravitational potential energy, and on a chaotic trajectory the same rotor phase does not bring the bobs back to the same heights. Their change of height is part of the storage term $E(t_b)-E(t_a)$ in Eq. (13); for bounded motion its long-time mean vanishes, and that is why gravity supplies no net energy over a long run.

Reading note. In the single pendulum, the moving pivot supplies a torque that turns the rod back toward the outward radial line, gravity gives it a push once per rotor turn, and the generator resists the motion across the joint. The linear calculation below treats this as a driven oscillator. In the double, the same three ingredients become matrices: accelerating or braking one rod also acts on the other.

Derived Linearize about $q_i=0$ and retain first order in gravity forcing. With $\mathbf q=\Re(\mathbf z e^{i\Omega t})$,

$$\begin{equation} (\mathbf K-\Omega^2\mathbf M+i\Omega\mathbf C)\mathbf z=\mathbf f, \qquad \overline P_{h,\rm lin}=\tfrac12\Omega^2\mathbf z^*\mathbf C\mathbf z,\tag{14}\end{equation}$$
$$\begin{equation}\begin{gathered} \mathbf M=\begin{pmatrix}A_d&D_d\\D_d&B_d\end{pmatrix},\quad \mathbf K=\Omega^2\begin{pmatrix}Ml_1r_p+D_d&-D_d\\-D_d&m_2l_2r_p+D_d\end{pmatrix},\\ \mathbf C=\begin{pmatrix}c_0+c_1&-c_1\\-c_1&c_1\end{pmatrix},\quad \mathbf f=-g\binom{Ml_1}{m_2l_2}. \end{gathered}\tag{15}\end{equation}$$

Derived For the equal-mass, equal-reach single comparator, $q''+\kappa q'+Rq=-G\cos s$. Therefore

$$\begin{equation} \frac{\overline{\widehat P}_{\rm lin}}{G^2}=\frac{\kappa}{2[(R-1)^2+\kappa^2]},\quad \kappa_* = |R-1|,\quad \overline P_{*,\rm lin}=\frac{MLg^2}{4\Omega|r_p-L|}.\tag{16}\end{equation}$$

Reading note. Away from exact resonance, more damping first adds useful braking, then holds the motion back so strongly that the power falls again. The optimum of Eq. (16), $\kappa_*=|R-1|$, balances the damping against the difference between stiffness and inertia at the forcing frequency; the two act a quarter cycle apart, so neither can cancel the other.

Derived The apparent singularity at $R=1$, $c\to0$ signals breakdown of the small-response model. It is not unlimited extractable power.

Assumed Linear theory is accepted here only when a nonlinear confirmation has peak $|q_i|<0.3$ rad and power disagreement below 2%. Otherwise a local nonlinear periodic-branch damping search is used, bounded between half and twice the linear-optimal damping. No result is claimed to be a global optimum over attractors.

4 Corrected fast-rotor mappings

Assumed Use $v=8$ m/s, $\lambda_T=7$, $\rho=1.225$ kg/m$^3$, and $C_p=0.45$. The scenario sets

$$\begin{equation} R_{\rm rot}=\frac{\lambda_Tv}{\Omega}=\frac{56\ {\rm m/s}}{\Omega},\qquad P_T=\tfrac12\rho\pi R_{\rm rot}^2v^3C_p.\tag{17}\end{equation}$$

Audit note. The PDF prints the numerator of Eq. (17) as “56 mm/s”: the “\r” of “\rm” was read as a carriage return. It is 56 m/s, as shown here. Every number computed from the formula is right.

7 Mass distribution and similarity

Assumed A separate dimensionless slice holds $R=1.2$, $G=0.01$, $M$ and $L$ fixed while varying $\mu\in\{0.01,0.1,1\}$ and $\lambda_\ell\in\{0.1,0.316,1\}$. Each double uses both generators at its linear-optimal damping; each power is confirmed with a nonlinear run.

Computed The best sampled geometry is $\mu=1$, $\lambda_\ell=0.1$: $\kappa=0.2327$ and $\overline{\widehat P}=\ensuremath{9.809\times10^{-5}}$. The nine geometries remain below the matched single optimum. The rotor mapping used for this dimensionless stress test is a coordinate conversion, not an additional selected turbine mounting.

Derived In the heavy-first-mass limit $\mu\to0$, the second mass contributes little inertia or forcing. The result approaches a heavy pendulum at reach $l_1$, not at the full reach $L$. Comparing it to a mass at $L$ without noting this difference would change the physical question.

Derived Similarity follows because the differential equations depend only on dimensionless parameters. Scale every mass by $3$, every rod and pivot radius by $4$, angular speed by $1/2$, and damping by $24$. With unchanged $g$, $R,G,\mu,\lambda_\ell,\kappa_i$ remain fixed. Thus

$$\begin{equation} \frac{P_{h,\rm new}}{P_{h,\rm old}}= \frac{(3M)(4L)^2(\Omega/2)^3}{ML^2\Omega^3}=6.\tag{18}\end{equation}$$

Computed The test gives a power ratio 6 and a maximum dimensionless state difference $\ensuremath{2.05\times10^{-10}}$ at relative tolerance $10^{-10}$ and absolute tolerance $10^{-12}$. The state difference depends on tolerance and floating-point arithmetic; it is not a universal similarity constant. Scaling only total mass or total rod length while changing their internal ratios would not preserve the model.

8 Chaos and the matched power comparison

Assumed The original scan has 48 combinations of $\Omega\in\{2,4,6\}$ rad/s, $r_p\in\{0.05,0.20\}$ m, and eight dampings, plus six refinements. It deliberately probes forcing outside the corrected blade-fraction mappings. Mixing powers between different $G$ values would confound chaos with a change in forcing.

Derived Benettin’s test evolves a nearby trajectory at fixed forcing phase. Every quarter period, its separation is renormalized to $\epsilon$ in the $(q_1,q_2,u_1,u_2)$ norm. The conditional exponent is

$$\begin{equation} \widehat\Lambda=\frac1{\Delta s}\sum_j\log\frac{\|\delta\mathbf x_j\|}{\epsilon},\qquad\Lambda=\Omega\widehat\Lambda.\tag{19}\end{equation}$$

Assumed A nonperiod-one candidate is called chaotic only if $\widehat\Lambda>0.002$ and all four 50-period exponent blocks are positive, after 8 alignment periods. Period-one trajectories use a shorter 24-period stability check. “Regular” means the chaos criterion was not met; it does not mean that periodicity has been proved.

13 Verdict: sufficient power, conditional usefulness

Computed The ideal mechanical result is favourable for a very small local load: the selected single pendulum supplies 9.55–57.1 mW at the corrected mountings. At assumed 50% conversion, the output is 4.78–28.5 mW, versus a 37.584 $\mu$W typical-current reference. Even the separate assumed 1 mW planning load is below these ideal outputs. The numerical model therefore supports power sufficiency for the stated duty cycle, not a hardware reliability claim.

Derived Every harvested joule is supplied by the rotor after accounting for mechanical storage. For the best corrected point, ideal harvest is $\ensuremath{7.2\times10^{-5}}$ of the assumed turbine power. Real aerodynamic drag, friction and regulation can increase the cost. This device is not worth building to raise turbine efficiency or net turbine electricity.

Computed There is no demonstrated reason here to prefer a chaotic double pendulum. The equal-mass, equal-reach double loses at all 18 corrected mountings. At the matched nonlinear forcing $R=1,G=3.065$, the regular branch supplies about 0.379 W, while the longer chaotic window supplies about 0.244 W. The latter fluctuates with averaging window and initial condition. Added hinges, wiring and moving components would need to earn their complexity through a separately demonstrated benefit.

Assumed A prototype is conditionally worth considering only as a local sensor supply where wiring, rotating power transfer or battery servicing is sufficiently costly. The first candidate should be the mechanically simpler single pendulum. Use a slow-rotor mounting that meets the accelerometer range with vibration margin, or select and budget a suitable higher-range sensor. Assume no extra turbine output and no safety credit from the ideal calculations.

Assumed Before committing to a blade installation, the generator must produce the assumed near-viscous braking torque at the actual joint speeds, overcome its own electrical threshold, and deliver useful power after leakage and storage losses. The available electrical margin should be measured with the full sampling, radio startup, reception and retry schedule. Cold start and a still-air interval require an explicit storage design.

Assumed The attachment must also tolerate centrifugal loading, vibration, fatigue and rotor imbalance; keep the entire swept pendulum and enclosure within the blade’s available envelope; and prevent a detached mass becoming a hazard. The fixed-$\Omega$, zero-aerodynamic-drag model provides none of these qualifications. The point-mass, massless-rod approximation omits the hardware mass that may dominate such a small generator.

Derived The deciding experiment is a blade-equivalent rotating rig: measure joint torque and speed, rotor reaction work, net regulated electrical energy, sensor saturation, and startup/storage behaviour under the intended duty cycle. If wiring or a maintained battery is cheaper and more reliable, reject this mechanism. If local autonomy offsets the mechanical burden and the measured energy margin survives those tests, a single-pendulum supply is plausible. The present study justifies that narrow engineering trial; it does not justify a turbine retrofit or a chaos-based power claim.
