05What in the report is wrong

none changes a conclusion · ranked by severity

  1. moderate Eq. 17 prints the rotor radius as 56 mm/s ÷ Ω. It is 56 m/s ÷ Ω: a lost "\r" in "\rm" turned the unit into millimetres. Every number that uses the formula is right.
  2. moderate The chaotic power's error bar, ±0.003 W, is the spread inside one run. Three valid integrations of the same run give 0.2439, 0.2470 and 0.2530 W. Honest statements: about 0.25 ± 0.01 W over 200 turns, 0.245 ± 0.005 W over 800, ratio to the regular orbit 0.65 ± 0.01. The conclusion, chaos gives less, stands.
  3. low One sentence says Γ "decreases with angular speed" at a fixed blade position. Γ = (r_p/R_rot) λ_T v Ω / g grows with Ω: 1.435 at 0.838 rad/s, 71.8 at 41.9 rad/s.
  4. low The abstract calls all 18 mountings physically admissible. At 400 rpm with the pivot at 90 %, the pendulum reaches 1.403 m on a 1.337 m blade: 66 mm past the tip.
  5. low The best harvest, 57.1 mW, sits at the edge of the sampled grid (fastest rotor, pivot nearest the hub). Linear theory grows as 1/|r_p − L| there, so it is the best sampled point, not an optimum.
  6. low The regular panel of the Poincaré figure is smeared by 0.97 mrad because angles were stored in single precision; the caption says it collapses to a point. The motion itself repeats.
  7. low Peak swing angles were read from 16 samples per turn; the continuous peaks are up to 1.5 % larger. No validity flag changes.
  8. low CHANGES.md labels the old short-window ratio 0.6436 as the old long-window one.

This site corrects all of these where it states the results; the Theory page marks each correction where it occurs.
