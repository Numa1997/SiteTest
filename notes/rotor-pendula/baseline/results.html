01The 18 mountings

3 speeds × 3 blade positions · fast and slow rotors · single and double

A turbine's blade tip runs about seven times faster than the wind (tip-speed ratio λT = 7). At a wind of 8 m/s that fixes the rotor radius once the speed is chosen: Rrot = 7 × 8 m/s ÷ Ω = 56 m/s ÷ Ω. A fast rotor is therefore small, and a slow one large. On each rotor the pivot sits at 30, 60 or 90 % of the blade.

02One rod against two

same mass, same reach · where the generators sit

At every mounting the double pendulum gives 64–74 % of the single one's power. A second joint is not a second energy supply: the hinge generator feels only the difference between the two rods' swings, which stays about a third of the first rod's, and at its best it gives 2.4 % (at R = 1.2) down to 0.02 % (at R = 10) of what a pivot generator gives. Theory chapter 7 derives this.

03Chaos

2 of 48 chaotic · the matched comparison · honest error bars

A double pendulum can move chaotically: two starts that differ by a hair drift apart exponentially. The study tests each case with the largest Lyapunov exponent (Theory chapter 9). In the matched group (R = 1, G = 3.065, same pendulum, damping varied) the best regular orbit harvests 0.379 W; the chaotic one, over 800 turns, about 0.244 W. Chaos gives less, not more.

How sure is the chaotic number? The same 200-turn run gives 0.2439 W with one integrator back end and 0.2530 W with another, and an independent model gives 0.2470 W. The study's ±0.003 W is the spread within one run, not this larger uncertainty; read the chaotic power as about 0.24–0.25 W.

04The sensor on the blade

37.6 µW needed · 18–215 g on fast rotors

The reference node samples an accelerometer continuously at 100 Hz and, once a second, wakes a radio microcontroller for 1 ms of computing and 1 ms of sending. With the datasheets' typical currents:

At an assumed 50 % conversion efficiency the pendulum must supply 75.2 µW of mechanical power; every mounting gives over a hundred times that. Power is not the limit. Force is: the accelerometer reads at most ±8 g, while the pivot of a fast rotor sits in 18 to 215 g of centrifugal acceleration.
