10The waiter: carrying without spilling

A good waiter does not carry a full glass the way you drag it. They start gently, keep a steady pace, and if the beer begins to rock they move the tray a little to calm it. This section builds a machine that does the same: a motor under the glass and a controller that watches the slosh.

The machine

The glass stands on a carriage that runs on rails in \(x\) and \(y\). Each axis is driven by a moving-coil motor, the loudspeaker’s principle: a coil of wire of length \(\ell\) in a magnetic field \(B\) feels the Lorentz force \(F = B\ell I\) when a current \(I\) flows, so the force is set by the current (Fig. 9 derives this actuator in detail). On the page \(B\ell = 12\) N/A and the current is limited to \(I_\text{max} = 2.5\) A, a force of 30 N at most. The carriage has a mass of 0.60 kg and the empty glass 0.42 kg (both our choice for the page). Every \(T_s = 10\) ms the controller reads the carriage’s position and speed and the state of the slosh, and sets the current, which then stays constant until its next look.

The stout pushes back

Push the carriage and the stout lags, and a lagging stout pushes back on the glass. To find how hard, write Newton’s law for everything the motor moves: carriage, glass and stout. The motor’s force equals the rate of change of their total momentum. The carriage and glass move with \(x_g\); the stout moves with them, plus the shift of its centre of mass inside the glass, which the slosh produces. A raised surface \(\eta\) moves the volume \(\eta\,dA\) of stout, so the stout’s centre of mass shifts sideways by \(\int x\,\eta\,dA\) divided by its volume. For the \(m = 1\) modes, with \(x = r\cos\theta\), this integral is \(\sum_n \pi Q_n N_n a_n\), where \(N_n = \int_0^R\psi_n^2\,r\,dr\). With \(M\) the mass of carriage, glass and stout together,

\[ M\ddot x_g + \rho\pi\sum_n Q_nN_n\,\ddot a_n = F . \tag{19} \]

Put \(\ddot a_n\) from (12) into (19), and the term \(-\lambda_nQ_n\ddot x_g/(1 + \kappa_n)\) in it gathers with \(M\ddot x_g\):

\[ M_e\,\ddot x_g = F + \rho\pi\sum_n Q_nN_n\left(2\gamma_n\dot a_n + \omega_n^2 a_n\right), \qquad M_e = M - \sum_n m_n, \quad m_n = \frac{\rho\pi\lambda_nQ_n^2N_n}{1 + \kappa_n} . \tag{20} \]

The \(m_n\) are the sloshing masses: the part of the stout that does not simply ride along but swings like a pendulum inside the glass. For the default pint, \(M = 1.523\) kg, of which the stout is 0.503 kg; the slowest mode’s sloshing mass is 79 g, the next two 2.2 g and 0.5 g, so the motor feels \(M_e = 1.442\) kg computed. Check: momentum is conserved. With \(F = 0\), (19) says \(M\dot x_g + \rho\pi\sum Q_nN_n\dot a_n\) stays constant: a sloshing glass on frictionless rails rocks back and forth against its own stout.

The controller

Collect the carriage’s position and speed and the three \(m = 1\) modes into a state \(\mathbf s = (x_g, \dot x_g, a_1, \dot a_1, a_2, \dot a_2, a_3, \dot a_3)\). Equations (12) and (20) are linear in \(\mathbf s\) and \(F\), so \(\dot{\mathbf s} = \mathsf A\mathbf s + \mathbf bF\). Holding \(F\) constant for one sample turns this into the map \(\mathbf s_{k+1} = \mathsf A_d\mathbf s_k + \mathbf b_dF_k\), with \(\mathsf A_d = e^{\mathsf AT_s}\) and \(\mathbf b_d\) the push of one held force. The system is controllable: the matrix \((\mathbf b, \mathsf A\mathbf b, \dots, \mathsf A^7\mathbf b)\) has rank 8, so one motor can steer the carriage and all three modes, although the two faster modes couple so weakly that the controller can do little about them computed.

The controller sets \(F_k = -\mathsf K(\mathbf s_k - \mathbf s_\text{path})\): a spring and damper pulling the carriage toward a planned path, and a correction for each mode. The gains \(\mathsf K\) are chosen to make the total cost

\[ J = \sum_k\left[\frac{(x_g - r)^2}{\sigma_x^2} + \frac{\eta_w^2}{\sigma_\eta^2} + \frac{F^2}{\sigma_F^2}\right], \qquad \eta_w = a_1 + a_2 + a_3 , \tag{21} \]

as small as possible, where \(r\) is the planned position and \(\eta_w\) the rise of the stout at the wall in the direction of travel. Each \(\sigma\) is a tolerance: a distance from the path, a wave at the wall and a force that we are willing to accept. This is the linear–quadratic regulator; its gains come from the discrete Riccati equation, which the page solves again whenever a slider moves (Fig. 11 derives it). On the page \(\sigma_x = 2\) cm, \(\sigma_\eta = 1\) mm and \(\sigma_F = 2\) N.

The path matters as much as the gains. A sudden jump of the target would make the controller kick the carriage, and the kick itself would start the slosh. So the target is approached along a path \(r(t)\) that follows it like a critically damped spring of rate \(\Omega_r\), \(\ddot r = \Omega_r^2(x_\text{target} - r) - 2\Omega_r\dot r\), and the controller adds the force the path itself needs, \(M\ddot r\). The total force is \(F = M\ddot r - \mathsf K(\mathbf s - \mathbf s_\text{path})\), limited to \(B\ell I_\text{max}\).

Key result · what the controller buys

Carried 20 cm along the same smooth path, the stout rises 8.0 mm at the wall when nothing watches it, enough to push the head over the rim, and only 2.6 mm with the waiter, which arrives within 2 mm of the target in 1.45 s, using at most 2.7 N (0.23 A). A quick hand, \(\Omega_h = 22\) rad/s, raises a wave of 128 mm in the linear model: a flood. The waiter also halves the time the slosh needs to die, from 2.0 s to 1.0 s computed.

How it is checked. The page computes its gains in the browser; an independent design in Python (SciPy’s Riccati solver) gives the same gains to \(3\times10^{-10}\), and the page’s own integrator reproduces the 20 cm carry of Figure 5, peak wave and arrival time, to the digits quoted computed.

What the sliders teach

Raise \(\Omega_r\) and the carry gets quicker but the wave grows: the waiter trades time against spill. Shrink \(\sigma_\eta\) and the controller works harder on the slosh, at the cost of a less faithful path. Lower the current limit until the force saturates, and the waiter can no longer keep its promise: a controller is only as good as the force it can apply. Stretch \(T_s\) and the controller looks too rarely to catch a 3 Hz slosh. Opinion: the honest limit of this waiter is the model it trusts. It assumes it knows the slosh state exactly; a real one would have to estimate it, for example from a camera or a level sensor, and it would fight the nonlinear swirl of section 9 with a linear law.
