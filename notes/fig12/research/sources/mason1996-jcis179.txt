Notation: phi = volume fraction of DISPERSED (oil) phase; a = droplet radius; sigma = interfacial tension;
phi_eff = phi corrected for the water film between drops. For a foam, phi is the GAS fraction; liquid fraction = 1 - phi.

--- p. 439, abstract ---
"The phi dependence of gamma_y is independent of a and exhibits a minimum near the critical volume fraction,
phi_c ≈ 0.635, associated with the random close packing of monodisperse spheres. We show that the yield stress,
tau_y, increases dramatically as the volume fraction increases above phi_c; tau_y also scales with the Laplace
pressure, sigma/a, where sigma is the interfacial tension."

--- p. 443, yield strain minimum ---
"This minimum occurs at phi_eff = 0.63 ... Below phi_c, the yield strain increases to gamma_y ≈ 0.045 near
phi_eff = 0.58 ... Above phi_c, the yield strain increases to more than gamma_y ≈ 0.1 as phi_eff approaches 1"

--- p. 443, yield stress law (THE FORMULA) ---
"in the limit as phi_eff -> 1, the yield stress approaches 0.1 sigma/a, about one-sixth the measured limit for G'.
... Above phi_c, the scaled yield stress can be empirically fit by a quadratic form,
   tau_y/(sigma/a) = C (phi_eff - phi_c)^2,
as shown by the solid line in Fig. 4 with C = 0.51 and phi_c = 0.62."
"... not surprising, since gamma_y and G' each rise nearly linearly above phi_c; thus their product, the scaled
yield stress, is well described by this quadratic form."

Data range (p. 443, Fig. 4 caption): radii a = 0.25, 0.37, 0.53, 0.74 µm; silicone oil in water, SDS.
No uncertainty is printed for C = 0.51 or phi_c = 0.62 (phi_c elsewhere 0.635; "experimental error in measuring
phi_eff" is mentioned, p. 443).
