[1] K. M. Case & W. C. Parkinson, "Damping of surface waves in an incompressible liquid", J. Fluid Mech. 2, 172-184
  (1957). doi:10.1017/S0022112057000051. Abstract: "The damping of surface waves of small amplitude in liquid
  contained in cylinders has been calculated. Viscous dissipation in an assumed laminar boundary layer was taken to
  be the primary cause of damping. Experimental results were obtained for the logarithmic decrement as a function of
  the ratio of liquid height to cylinder radius for several water-filled cylinders. Theory and experiment were found
  to be in good agreement."
[2] L. M. Hocking, "The damping of capillary-gravity waves at a rigid boundary", J. Fluid Mech. 179, 253-266 (1987).
  doi:10.1017/S0022112087001514. Abstract (excerpt): "An edge condition that models to some extent the dynamics
  associated with moving contact lines, but not contact-angle hysteresis, is given by making the slope of the free
  surface at contact proportional to its velocity. ... it is shown that the dissipation associated with the surface
  forces can exceed that produced by viscosity."
[3] M. Henderson (sic: D. M. Henderson) & J. W. Miles, "Surface-wave damping in a circular cylinder with a fixed
  contact line", J. Fluid Mech. 275, 285-299 (1994). doi:10.1017/S0022112094002363. Abstract (excerpt): "calculated on
  the assumptions of a fixed contact line, Stokes boundary layers, and either a clean or a fully contaminated surface.
  ... brimfull, sharp-edged cylinder of radius 2.77 cm and depth 3.80 cm. ... The difference between the predicted
  and observed damping ratio for the dominant mode with a clean surface was 20%; this difference was significantly
  larger for the higher modes with a clean surface and for all of the modes with a contaminated surface."
[4] J. W. Miles & D. M. Henderson, "A note on interior vs. boundary-layer damping of surface waves in a circular
  cylinder", J. Fluid Mech. 364, 319-323 (1998). doi:10.1017/S0022112098001189. Excerpt: "interior damping may be
  comparable with boundary-layer damping for surface waves in small cylinders ... The corresponding calculations for
  a fully contaminated (inextensible) surface reduce the discrepancy between calculation and experiment but ...
  leave a significant residual discrepancy."
[5] D. M. Henderson, "Effects of surfactants on Faraday-wave dynamics", J. Fluid Mech. 365, 89-107 (1998).
  doi:10.1017/S0022112098001086. Excerpt: "Measured damping rates increased with increasing film concentration as
  predicted ... the measured damping rates for large-slope waves on these films were equivalent to those of waves on
  a clean surface. ... Contact-line effects are incorporated, using an empirical value for contact-line speed"
[6] R. Cocciaro, S. Faetti & M. Nobili, "Capillarity effects on surface gravity waves in a cylindrical container:
  wetting boundary conditions", J. Fluid Mech. 231, 325-343 (1991). doi:10.1017/S0022112091003415. Excerpt: "The
  damping of the fundamental surface mode is found to exhibit nonlinear behaviour which is in qualitative agreement
  with the predictions of the Miles (1967) theory of capillary damping."
[7] R. Cocciaro, S. Faetti & C. Festa, "Experimental investigation of capillarity effects on surface gravity waves:
  non-wetting boundary conditions", J. Fluid Mech. 246, 43-66 (1993). doi:10.1017/S0022112093000035. Excerpt: "a
  systematic experimental investigation of the static and dynamic properties of surface waves in a cylindrical
  container where the free surface makes a static contact angle [value lost in metadata; 62 deg per Viola 2016
  thesis p.186] ... A satisfactory agreement is found to exist between theory and experiment."
