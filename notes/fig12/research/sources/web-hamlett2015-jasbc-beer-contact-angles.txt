"The beers used in this study were Guinness Original (4.2% ABV), Banks's Bitter (3.8% ABV),
Bass No 1 (4.4% ABV) and Harvest Pale (4.3% ABV) purchased in 500mL bottles ... They were used at room temperature and none of the beer bottles contained a widget."
"2mL of surfactant solution or beer, which had been allowed to degas by pouring 10ml of beer into a glass vial which was the left open for 72 hrs"
(NOTE: Guinness Original = the CO2-only bottled stout, not Guinness Draught; beer degassed 72 h; room temperature.)

--- method (verbatim) ---
"rendered hydrophilic by immersion in 30% hydrochloric acid (Fisher Scientific, UK) for 16 hrs. The glass containers were then rinsed using copious amounts of water and dried at 80°C for 3 hrs."
"The static (θW), advancing (θWA) and receding (θWR) contact angles of a water droplet on flat glass microscope slides subjected to the same chemical treatments as the vials were measured using a Krüss DSA 10 goniometer (Hamburg, Germany) and Krüss DSA software."
"Table 2 confirms that glass microscope slides cleaned in HCl exhibit beer contact angles (θB) less than 90° and those Grangers' modified microscope slides display contact angles greater than 90°"
"The necessity to check the beer contact angles is that the surface tension of beer is less than water as is shown by the beer contact angles (Table 2) being less than the ones of water droplets on equivalent surfaces (Table 1)."

CAVEAT (reader's note): the row labels came out of the text layer below the numbers; the assignment of row 2 = advancing, row 3 = receding is
the order of the labels in the extraction. For Guinness on clean glass the "advancing" value (22.3°) is below the static value (28.5°), which is
unusual; the published JASBC table should be checked before this is used as hysteresis.
