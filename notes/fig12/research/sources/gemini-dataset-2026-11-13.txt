# Empirical Physics Dataset: Guinness Stout & Beer Foam Measurements

All measurements below are reported in metric units. Every entry includes the measured value and unit, experimental method, liquid used, temperature, direct source link, and a short exact quote from the source, along with explicit notes on any sub-parameters that were not found and the repositories searched.

## 1. Surface Tension of Guinness or Stout (Drop Counting, Capillary Tube, Ring, Hanging Drop)

### 1A. Guinness Stout — Hanging / Pendant Drop Method
* **Value and unit:** Surface tension $\gamma = 44.2\text{ mN/m}$ ($0.0442\text{ N/m}$); interfacial dilatational modulus $E = 18.7\text{ mN/m}$ ($0.0187\text{ N/m}$).
* **How it was measured:** Hanging drop (pendant drop) tensiometry using a Sinterface PAT1 tensiometer.
* **Liquid used:** Degassed Guinness stout beer.
* **Temperature:** $20^\circ\text{C}$ ($293.15\text{ K}$).
* **Link:** https://pubs.rsc.org/sm/article/17/14/3937/708463/Shear-modulus-and-yield-stress-of-foams and https://www.researchgate.net/publication/349974781_Shear_modulus_and_yield-stress_of_foams_Contribution_of_interfacial_elasticity
* **Short exact quote:** `"pendant drop method (PAT1, Sintaface)"` / `"Guinness stout beer, Degassed; at 20 $^\circ$C"` / `"Guinness stout, 44.2, 18.7"`

### 1B. Beer — Drop-Weight / Drop-Counting Method
* **Value and unit:** Surface tension $\sigma = 41.55 \pm 1.39\text{ mN/m}$ ($0.04155 \pm 0.00139\text{ N/m}$); liquid density $\rho_{\text{beer}} = 1012\text{ kg/m}^3$.
* **How it was measured:** Drop-weight (drop counting/weighing) method originally measured by A. Rudin (1956, *Journal of the Institute of Brewing*, Vol. 62, pp. $27\text{--}30$) and collated in Pereira et al. (2023).
* **Liquid used:** Lager-style beer ($92\text{--}96\text{ wt.\%}$ $\text{H}_2\text{O}$).
* **Temperature:** $25^\circ\text{C}$ ($298.15\text{ K}$).
* **Link:** https://royalsocietypublishing.org/rsos/article/10/6/230376/92159/The-physics-of-dancing-peanuts-in-beerThe-physics and https://epub.ub.uni-muenchen.de/132940/1/rsos.230376.pdf
* **Short exact quote:** `"Beer-vapour surface tension was also retrieved from the literature ($\sigma = 41.55 \pm 1.39\text{ mN m}^{-1}$) [6]."` and `"6. Rudin A. 1956 Measurement of surface tension of beer by the drop-weight method. J. Instit. Brewing 62, 27-30."`

## 2. Contact Angle of Beer or Stout on Glass (Clean Glass vs. "Beer-Clean" Glass)

### 2A. Beer on Clean Glass Substrate
* **Value and unit:** Contact angle $\Psi_{\text{glass}} = 23.20^\circ \pm 2.80^\circ$ ($0.405 \pm 0.049\text{ rad}$) on glass in air (compared with $\Psi_{\text{peanut}} = 46.48^\circ \pm 2.43^\circ$ on a peanut skin in air; supplementary contact angle measured through the gas phase $= 180^\circ - \Psi$).
* **How it was measured:** Sessile drop goniometry ($10$ replicate measurements of beer droplets placed on a flat glass surface in air, imaged with a $5\text{ mm}$ scale bar).
* **Liquid used:** Lager-style beer.
* **Temperature:** $25^\circ\text{C}$ ($298.15\text{ K}$).
* **Link:** https://royalsocietypublishing.org/rsos/article/10/6/230376/92159/The-physics-of-dancing-peanuts-in-beerThe-physics and https://epub.ub.uni-muenchen.de/132940/1/rsos.230376.pdf
* **Short exact quote:** `"Contact angle ($\Psi$) measurements between the involved phases (solid-liquid-gas) were carried out using the sessile drop method [8]. ... The average values of 10 measurements are $\Psi_{\text{peanut}} = 46.48 \pm 2.43^\circ$ and $\Psi_{\text{glass}} = 23.20 \pm 2.80^\circ$"`

### 2B. Difference Between Standard Clean Glass and "Beer-Clean" Glass
* **Value and unit:** Clean glass exhibits an acute wetting contact angle between $0^\circ$ and $90^\circ$ (approaching $0^\circ$ where the meniscus grazes a perfectly clean glass wall). An exact numerical degree difference comparing a standard washed glass to a "beer-clean" glass specifically for stout was **not found** (looked in Royal Society Open Science, ResearchGate, Master Brewers Association of the Americas, Brewers Association Draught Quality resources, Lonerider, Druthers Brewing, and KegWorks).
* **How it was measured:** Industry "Sheeting Test" (dipping the glass in water to verify unbroken continuous film wetting with near-zero contact angle versus droplet bead formation caused by trace lipid/detergent films), "Salt Test" (sprinkling salt on the wet interior wall to detect hydrophobic grease patches), and "Lacing Test" (checking whether foam adheres in parallel rings during consumption); washing standards specify $54.4^\circ\text{C}$ to $60.0^\circ\text{C}$ ($130^\circ\text{F}$ to $140^\circ\text{F}$) with non-fat detergent and chemical sanitizer, or $82.2^\circ\text{C}$ ($180^\circ\text{F}$) high-temperature sanitization, followed by chiller storage at $2.2^\circ\text{C}$ to $4.4^\circ\text{C}$ ($36^\circ\text{F}$ to $40^\circ\text{F}$) or room temperature.
* **Liquid used:** Rinse water and poured beer/stout.
* **Temperature:** $3^\circ\text{C}$ to $7^\circ\text{C}$ serving temperature; $2.2^\circ\text{C}$ to $4.4^\circ\text{C}$ glass chiller temperature.
* **Link:** https://loneriderbeer.com/guide/beer-clean-glassware and https://www.druthersbrewing.com/a-beer-clean-glass-what-it-is-and-why-it-matters/ and https://www.arvindguptatoys.com/arvindgupta/dunk-doughnut.pdf
* **Short exact quote:** `"Sheeting test. Dip the glass in water and lift it out. On a clean glass, water coats the surface in an even sheet. Droplets or dry patches mean a film is still there."` and `"if the glass is perfectly clean the meniscus will appear to just graze the glass surface"`

## 3. Bubble Size in a Guinness Head / Disperse Foam

### 3A. High-Speed Microscope Measurement of Canned Draught Guinness Bubbles & Liquid Properties
* **Value and unit:** Mean bubble diameter $d = 61\text{ }\mu\text{m}$ ($0.061\text{ mm}$), with $50\text{ }\mu\text{m}$ typical dispersed diameter and literature comparison markers at $94\text{ }\mu\text{m}$ and $122\text{ }\mu\text{m}$ in Fig. S2; initial dispersed bubble volume concentration $\alpha = 8\%$ ($0.08$); degassed Guinness liquid density $\rho = 1006\text{ kg/m}^3$; dynamic viscosity $\mu = 2.1 \times 10^{-3}\text{ Pa}\cdot\text{s}$; kinematic viscosity $\nu = 2.03\text{ mm}^2/\text{s}$ ($2.03 \times 10^{-6}\text{ m}^2/\text{s}$) above $10^\circ\text{C}$ and extrapolated to $\nu = 2.23\text{ mm}^2/\text{s}$ at $5^\circ\text{C}$.
* **How it was measured:** High-speed video camera fitted with a microscope lens across three experimental runs (greater than $3000$ bubbles measured to fit a log-normal probability density distribution); kinematic viscosity measured via a rolling-ball viscometer (Anton-Paar Lovis 2000M/ME) and liquid density via an oscillating U-tube density meter (Anton-Paar DMA 35N) on $80\text{ g}$ samples degassed in a centrifugal evaporator at $500\text{ G}$ and $100\text{ mbar}$ ($10\text{ kPa}$) for $20\text{ min}$.
* **Liquid used:** Canned Guinness Draught ($330\text{ mL}$ widget can).
* **Temperature:** $5^\circ\text{C}$ to $20^\circ\text{C}$ ($25^\circ\text{C}$ for synthetic hollow-glass-particle test fluid).
* **Link:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6450914/ and https://www.researchgate.net/publication/332367939_Supplementary_Material_1/data/5cafe5904585156cd7916346/41598-2019-42094-MOESM1-ESM.pdf
* **Short exact quote:** `"Note that we measured the density and viscosity of liquid in Guinness beer ourselves and found that the liquid density was $\rho = 1006\text{ kg/m}^3$ and viscosity was $\mu = 2.1 \times 10^{-3}\text{ Pa}\cdot\text{s}$ (see also Supplementary Fig. S1). The mean diameter of bubbles in Guinness was $61\text{ }\mu\text{m}$, and the volume-concentration of the bubbles was $\alpha = 8\%$."`

### 3B. Guinness vs. Lager Bubble Diameter and Interbubble Distance
* **Value and unit:** Guinness mean bubble diameter $= 59 \pm 15\text{ }\mu\text{m}$ ($0.059 \pm 0.015\text{ mm}$) with mean interbubble spacing $= 0.13\text{ mm}$ ($130\text{ }\mu\text{m}$), compared with Heineken lager mean bubble diameter $= 459 \pm 31\text{ }\mu\text{m}$ ($0.459 \pm 0.031\text{ mm}$) with mean interbubble spacing $= 2.1\text{ mm}$.
* **How it was measured:** Microscopic high-speed video imaging and statistical particle sizing of $N = 301$ Guinness bubbles and $N = 220$ Heineken bubbles.
* **Liquid used:** Canned draught Guinness stout and Heineken lager.
* **Temperature:** Standard laboratory/serving conditions.
* **Link:** https://link.aps.org/doi/10.1103/PhysRevE.103.063103
* **Short exact quote:** `"Guinness beer | $59 \pm 15$, 301 | 0.13 ... Heineken beer | $459 \pm 31$, 220 | 2.1"`

### 3C. Bubble Radius, Stokes Velocities, and Precritical Nucleation Scale
* **Value and unit:** Characteristic bubble radius $r = 61\text{ }\mu\text{m}$ ($0.061\text{ mm}$); beer density $\rho_{\text{beer}} = 1007\text{ kg/m}^3$; gas bubble density $\rho_{\text{bubble}} = 1.223\text{ kg/m}^3$; dynamic viscosity $\mu = 2.06 \times 10^{-3}\text{ Pa}\cdot\text{s}$; Stokes velocity components $u_{\text{Stokes}} = 3.45 \times 10^{-4}\text{ m/s}$ and $v_{\text{Stokes}} = 3.95 \times 10^{-3}\text{ m/s}$. In high-speed nucleation experiments, the microscope frame width is $270\text{ }\mu\text{m}$ ($0.27\text{ mm}$), and a precritical bubble radius $R = 0.25\text{ }\mu\text{m}$ expands to $R \approx 0.31\text{ }\mu\text{m}$ when liquid pressure drops from $100\text{ kPa}$ ($1\text{ bar}$) to $50\text{ kPa}$ ($0.5\text{ bar}$), while critical radius $R_c$ drops from $0.29\text{ }\mu\text{m}$ to $0.24\text{ }\mu\text{m}$.
* **How it was measured:** High-speed microscopy measured frame-by-frame in MATLAB and hydrodynamic drift-flux parameter tables.
* **Liquid used:** Guinness Draught stout.
* **Temperature:** $8^\circ\text{C}$ ($281.15\text{ K}$).
* **Link:** https://eprints.hud.ac.uk/id/eprint/34109/1/sinking.bubbles.pdf and https://www.iaeng.org/publication/WCE2011/WCE2011_pp160-164.pdf and https://miis.maths.ox.ac.uk/395/1/ESGI70_7.pdf
* **Short exact quote:** `"$\rho_{\text{beer}}$ $1007\text{ kg m}^{-3}$ ... $\rho_{\text{bubble}}$ $1.223\text{ kg m}^{-3}$ ... $\mu$ $2.06 \times 10^{-3}\text{ Pa s}$ ... $r$ $61\text{ }\mu\text{m}$ ... $\phi_{\text{Head}}$ $0.80$"` and `"The width of the entire frame is approximately $270\text{ }\mu\text{m}$. bubble is measured frame by frame using MATLAB."`

## 4. Foam Wetness (Weight / Foam-to-Liquid Ratio) and Foam Stability (NIBEM, Rudin)

### 4A. Foam-to-Liquid Ratio in the Settled Head vs. Freshly Poured Bubbly Mixture
* **Value and unit:** Settled stout head bubble volume fraction $\phi_{\text{Head}} = 0.80$ ($80\%$ gas by volume, meaning a $20\%$ or $0.20$ volumetric liquid fraction in the head, or a $5:1$ foam-to-liquid volume ratio). In the freshly poured bubbly Guinness dispersion prior to phase separation, the gas volume fraction is $\alpha = 0.08$ ($8\%$ gas, $92\%$ liquid by volume). An experiment where someone weighed an isolated spoonful of Guinness foam in grams was **not found** (looked in ResearchGate, PubMed Central, Reddit r/Guinness, r/cocktails, and Homebrewtalk; r/cocktails only documented adding $1\text{ g}$ soy lecithin and $1\text{ g}$ xanthan gum per $200\text{ mL}$ of Guinness to spoon stabilized foam without weighing the spoonful).
* **How it was measured:** Pouring canned Draught Guinness into an overflowing $210\text{ mL}$ measuring cup within $10\text{ s}$ of opening, leveling the top foam off with a spatula, wiping exterior drops, and weighing the cup on a digital scale against degassed Guinness liquid ($\rho = 1006\text{ kg/m}^3$); head volume fraction $\phi_{\text{Head}} = 0.80$ reported in Huddersfield stout settling hydrodynamics.
* **Liquid used:** Canned Guinness Draught ($330\text{ mL}$ widget can) and draught stout.
* **Temperature:** $5^\circ\text{C}$ to $20^\circ\text{C}$.
* **Link:** https://www.researchgate.net/publication/332367939_Supplementary_Material_1/data/5cafe5904585156cd7916346/41598-2019-42094-MOESM1-ESM.pdf and https://eprints.hud.ac.uk/id/eprint/34109/1/sinking.bubbles.pdf
* **Short exact quote:** `"To measure the bubble volume-concentration, we poured Guinness into a 210-ml measuring cup that overflowed within 10 seconds after opening a can. The foam above the cup was removed with a spatula, and drops attached at the outer wall of the cup were wiped immediately. The measuring cup was placed on a digital weight scale, and then the weight of bubbly Guinness was measured."` and `"$\phi_{\text{Head}}$ $0.80$"`

### 4B. Wet Foam Height vs. Dry Foam Height During Drainage
* **Value and unit:** Initial wet foam layer height $= 24.6\text{ mm}$ out of a total initial foam height of $\sim 70\text{--}80\text{ mm}$ generated from a $100\text{ mL}$ pour, monitored over $600\text{ s}$ ($10\text{ min}$) of liquid drainage.

* **Liquid used:** Bottled dark beer (Osjecka pivovara d.d., Croatia) compared with pale lager.
* **Temperature:** Ambient laboratory temperature.
* **Link:** https://e-university.tu-sofia.bg/e-publ/files/12597_Paper_UKRAINIAN_FOOD_JOURNAL_2020_V.9_Is.4.pdf
* **Short exact quote:** `"difference between light and dark beer, where the samples of dark beer showed a higher initial height of the wet part of the foam (24.6 mm)"`

## 6. Tilted, Shaken, Walked With, or Upside-Down Pint Measurements

### 6A. Glass Tilt Angles, Roll-Wave Cascade Velocity, and Settling Time
* **Value and unit:** Initial pour glass tilt $= 45^\circ$ ($0.785\text{ rad}$); total two-part pour and settling duration $= 119.5\text{ s}$ ($90\text{--}120\text{ s}$ settling phase); tulip pint glass wall inclination angle $= 15^\circ$ over vertical height $30\text{ mm}$ to $70\text{ mm}$; laboratory inclination angle $\beta$ swept from $0^\circ$ (vertical) to $70^\circ$, with roll-wave gravity-current instabilities forming strictly when $5^\circ \lt \beta \lt 20^\circ$ and propagating downward at velocity $\approx -35\text{ mm/s}$, driven by a clear bubble-free liquid boundary layer at distance $y = 0.1\text{ mm}$ from the inclined wall and travelling waves at $y = 0.5\text{ mm}$ and $y = 1.0\text{ mm}$.
* **How it was measured:** High-speed video, Particle Image Velocimetry (PIV) with $15\text{ }\mu\text{m}$ fluorescent resin tracer particles, and laser photo-bleaching molecular tagging ($447\text{ nm}$ laser sheet) in a pint glass and inclinable trapezium/rectangular cells.
* **Liquid used:** Canned Guinness Draught ($330\text{ mL}$ widget can) and tap Guinness Draught.
* **Temperature:** $6^\circ\text{C}$ (pub serving temperature) and $5^\circ\text{C}$ to $25^\circ\text{C}$ (laboratory test range).
* **Link:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6450914/ and https://www.guinness-storehouse.com/en/whats-hoppening/most-popular-questions-about-guinness

The texture appearing in a glass propagates downwards with a travelling velocity of $\sim -35\text{ mm/s}$ ... For $5\text{ degrees} \lt \beta \lt 20\text{ degrees}$, however, the texture appeared"`

### 6B. Sloshing Damping by Foam Under Horizontal Oscillation / Cart Motion
* **Value and unit:** First-mode sloshing frequency $f_1 \approx 3.1\text{ Hz}$ with foam (versus $3.3\text{ Hz}$ without foam); liquid-only damping rate $1/\tau_\ell \approx 2\text{ s}^{-1}$ for liquid depth $H = 40\text{ mm}$ ($4\text{ cm}$), cell width $w = 3\text{ mm}$, and length $L = 70\text{ mm}$ ($7\text{ cm}$); under continuous harmonic lateral excitation of amplitude $X_0 = 1\text{ mm}$, the free-surface sloshing wave amplitude reaches $\sim 10\text{ mm}$ ($1\text{ cm}$) without foam, whereas adding $\xi_c = 5 \pm 2$ layers of bubbles (diameter $D = 3\text{ mm}$) suppresses sloshing by viscous dissipation against the container walls.
* **How it was measured:** Mechanical horizontal shaker driving rectangular and 3D cells with high-speed video tracking of the foam-liquid interface and vertical velocity profiles across the wall boundary layer.
* **Liquid used:** Model foaming solution ($90\%\text{ v/v}$ water, $5\%\text{ v/v}$ glycerol, $5\%\text{ v/v}$ Dawn surfactant) formulated to model beer foam sloshing resistance when carrying a pint.
* **Temperature:** Room temperature ($\sim 20^\circ\text{C}$).
* **Link:** https://sauretlab.me.ucsb.edu/sites/default/files/papers/2015_Sauret_PhysFluids.pdf and https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.6542
* **Short exact quote:** `"However, beer does not slosh as readily as water, which suggests that foam could be used to damp sloshing. ... Indeed, without foam, the free surface has a maximum amplitude of the order of 1 cm; the addition of only $\xi = 5$ layers of bubbles leads..."`

### 6C. Upside-Down Pint Trick Videos & Critical Tilt Before Settled Head Moves
* **Value and unit:** $180^\circ$ inversion in the "upside-down Guinness challenge" (inverting a full pint glass using a flat card/crisp packet and plate, then pulling the sheet out so atmospheric pressure and meniscus surface tension hold the liquid) and "the flip" ($180^\circ$ vertical inversion of an opened Guinness widget can inside the glass during pouring). An exact angular measurement in degrees of how far a settled Guinness pint can be tilted before the foam head shears or slides was **not found** (looked in YouTube descriptions, Reddit r/Guinness, Irish Star, and fluid mechanics journals; sources note qualitatively that a sharp tilt "bruises" the head without giving a degree threshold).
* **How it was measured:** Video recordings of pub demonstrations and home-pour methods ($60\text{ s}$ and $11\text{ s}$ video clips).
