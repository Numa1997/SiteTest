Introduction: "Fluids with viscosities independent of shear rates are termed "Newtonian fluids". Wort and beer
viscosities normally have little, if any, shear dependence."
Introduction: "beer viscosity can vary between 1.45-1.96 mPa • s at 20°C"
Materials and Methods, "Determination of wort and beer viscosities": "Viscosities of wort and beer were measured with a
Bohlin VOR controlled-rate rheometer ... by using a C-14 coaxial cylinder geometry ... shear rates of 91.9, 184, 367,
581, 731 and 921 s-1 ... Measurement temperature was 20°C for wort and 5°C for beer unless otherwise specified. Shear
stress values were measured and the viscosity was obtained from linear regressions of the dependence of shear stress
on shear rate (r2 ≥ 0.99; n = 3). Previously, Oonsivilai had reported possible shear thickening in commercial worts at
shear rates below 100 s-1. However, the wort and beer samples were found to be Newtonian at the higher range of shear
rates (91.9-921 s-1) employed in this study."
Methods: beers were reconstituted "Degassed "beer" samples ... which contained 5.0% v/v of ethanol and 3.3% w/w of real
extract at pH 4.2" (model beers from beer concentrate, not a commercial stout).
Abstract: "Shearing wort and beer at approximately 13,000 s-1 for 35 s was found to increase the wort viscosity but
reduce beer viscosity."
