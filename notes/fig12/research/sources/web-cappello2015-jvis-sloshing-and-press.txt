TITLE: Damping of liquid sloshing by foams: from everyday observations to liquid transport
AUTHORS: Jean Cappello, Alban Sauret, François Boulogne, Emilie Dressaire, Howard A. Stone
PUBLISHED: J. Visualization 18, 269-271 (2015); DOI 10.1007/s12650-014-0250-1; arXiv:1411.2123
URL READ: https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.2123 (full text v1 via Exa search result, 2026-11-13)
LICENCE: arXiv non-exclusive distribution licence
SAMPLE: model foam (90 % water, 5 % glycerol, 5 % Dawn), monodisperse 3 mm bubbles, Hele-Shaw-like cell 70 x 92 x 16 mm. NOT beer.
(The companion Phys. Fluids 27, 022103 paper is already in sources as sauret2015-pof27.txt.)

--- verbatim ---
"It is a common observation that beer does not slosh as readily as water, which suggests that the presence of foam could be used to damp sloshing, as illustrated in Figure 1."
"Figure 1: Photographs of a glass of coffee (left) and a glass of beer (right). The glass is filled to a height H1 of liquid and a foam layer of thickness H0 lies in the top of the beer."
"The experimental setup consists of a rectangular cell of height H=92 mm, length L=70 mm and width w=16 mm."
"The frequency of the oscillation is independent of the foam thickness: f=3.22 ± 0.11 s−1 but the timescale τ over which the oscillations decay decreases with the foam thickness: τ=1.81 ± 0.05 s without foam, τ=0.63 ± 0.04 s for 6 mm of foam and τ=0.37 ± 0.01 s for 20 mm of foam."
"The increase of damping, 1/τ is likely due to viscous dissipation as the foam is displaced along the sidewalls of the container."

--- APS DFD 2014 abstract H16.00009 (https://meetings.aps.org/Meeting/DFD14/Session/H16.9), verbatim ---
"2 to 3 layers of bubbles are sufficient to significantly damp the oscillations. For more than 5 layers of bubbles, the original vertical motion of the foam becomes mainly horizontal."
