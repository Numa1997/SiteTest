--- p.7 (Sec. IV A) ---
"In all experiments, no topological rearrangements (T1) are observed. As a result, the foam behaves like a
visco-elastic fluid without plastic deformation."
"In the presence of a foam, the spectrum exhibits a peak located around f1 ≃ 3.1 Hz. This value is similar to the
frequency of the first mode in the absence of foam, which is predicted to be 3.3 Hz from equation (1). This result
suggests that the presence of foam does not significantly modify the characteristic frequency of the system."
"This result indicates that the elasticity at the interface between the air and the foam can be neglected at first
order thus leading to no supplementary contribution in the dispersion relation (1)."
"without foam, the free-surface has a maximum amplitude of the order of 1 cm; the addition of only xi = 5 layers of
bubbles leads to a maximum amplitude of the order 1 mm, which is a factor of ten reduction." (forcing at f1, X0 = 1 mm)
"the damping coefficient 1/tau increases exponentially toward a plateau over a typical rescaled length corresponding
to 5 layers of bubbles."
Eq. (8): "1/tau = 1/tau_l + (1/tau_f^inf) [1 - exp(-xi/xi_c)]"
"where 1/tau_f^inf = 9 ± 3 s^-1, a plateau 1/tau_l + 1/tau_f^inf = 11 ± 3 s^-1 and xi_c = 5 ± 2 for the considered
experimental parameters. The damping coefficient of the foam solution alone is estimated to be 1/tau_l ≃ 2 s^-1"
(H = 4 cm, w = 3 mm, L = 7 cm)

--- p.8 (velocity profile in the foam) ---
"For fewer than two layers of bubbles the velocity is mostly vertical. The velocity becomes more horizontal as the
number of layers increases, except for bubbles near the vertical walls, which have a non-negligible vertical velocity
due to the boundary conditions. ... the mean bubble velocity decreases exponentially <|v_b|> ∝ exp(-z/(xi_c D)) with
a characteristic length scale of xi_c D = 16.8±1.8 mm corresponding to xi_c ≃ 5 layers of bubbles."

--- p.9 (Sec. IV B, wall friction model) ---
"the friction force of the Plateau borders on two solid walls can be estimated as f_b = K gamma_f Ca^(2/3), where
Ca = mu_f v_b / gamma_f is the Capillary number and K is a constant for given geometrical parameters describing the
Plateau borders. The typical Capillary number considered in our experiments is of order Ca ~ 3 × 10^-3 ...
Note that the present scaling for the friction force is no longer valid for Ca > 10^-2 due to inertial effects"
Eq. (10): "<|E_f'|> = K' Σ <v_b^(5/3)>  with K' = K gamma_f^(1/3) mu_f^(2/3)."
p.10: "We note that the order of magnitude of the lengthscale over which the velocity decreases has the same order of
magnitude as the length scale associated with the first mode of resonance 1/k1 = L/pi ≃ 22 mm."
Eq. (14): "1/tau_f = [12 pi xi_c K' <|cos(wt)|^(5/3)> / (5 rho w L (v0max)^(1/3))] [1 - exp(-5 xi/(3 xi_c))]"
Eq. (15)-(16): prefactor fit "= 2.76 cm/s which leads to K ≈ 1.9 m/s with v0max ≈ 0.1 m/s. The value of K can be
compared with the phenomenological law K = 5.13 (R_p/sqrt(A))^(-0.48)"  ... "we obtain a prediction of K = 9.1 m/s."
[Note: K is printed with units m/s in the paper although f_b = K gamma Ca^(2/3) suggests dimensionless; reported as printed.]
"the strongest variation of the liquid fraction happens between the first and the second layer of bubbles because the
first layer is directly in contact with the liquid bath"

--- p.11 (Sec. IV C, confined 3D) ---
"we consider that only the Plateau borders in contact with the walls contribute to the energy dissipation and that the
bubbles in the bulk are simply advected by the bubbles in contact with the walls. Thus ... the damping coefficient,
1/tau_f, should scale as the inverse of the width of the cell, 1/w."
"The exponential trend leads to a value of the plateau w/tau_f^inf = 1.6 ± 0.3 cm s^-1 whereas the best fit suggests
that the plateau has a value of w/tau_f^inf ≃ 3.1 cm s^-1."
"the saturation value of the rescaled damping coefficient w/tau_f seems to slightly increase with the width of the
cell. This observation indicates that the dissipation in the bulk could add a small contribution"
p.12: "the cells used in this study are narrow, which means that the number of bubbles in the transverse direction
does not exceed 6 bubbles. Thus, further investigation of 3D foams is necessary"
