--- arXiv p. 1, the LLD law ---
"The relative size of viscous drag and capillary retention in the film is measured by the capillary number
Ca = U eta/gamma, where eta is the viscosity and gamma the surface tension. At the foot of the film, LLD introduced
the requirement that the film be matched smoothly to a static capillary meniscus ..., whose size is controlled by the
capillary length l_c = √(gamma/rho g). In the limit of small Ca, this matching yields
   h_LLD = 0.946 l_c Ca^(2/3)"
Fig. 1 caption: "Ca = 9.27 × 10^-3; the solid line is the classical LLD solution, which predicts h_f = 57 µm."

--- arXiv p. 2, thick-film branch (partial wetting) ---
"h_f ≃ (3Ca)^(1/2) − (1/2) Q Ca^(2/3)   (5)"  (lengths in units of l_c), Q ∈ [0, Qmax], "Qmax ≈ 1.376".
Validity: lubrication theory, small Ca, stress-free (clean) liquid-air interface; perfectly wetting -> LLD film.
