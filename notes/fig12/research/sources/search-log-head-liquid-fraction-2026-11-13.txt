SEARCH LOG: measured liquid fraction (or density) of a Guinness / nitrogenated-stout head. 13 November 2026.
Roads, in the order the owner asked (plain search and fetch first, crawlers only last):
1. WebSearch x8 (standard and extended): "beer foam liquid fraction measured conductivity Guinness nitrogen foam density head";
   "beer foam liquid fraction stout nitrogen"; "Guinness head foam density g/ml"; "beer foam liquid content percent ... Ronteltap";
   "nitro cold brew / nitrogenated beer foam liquid fraction"; "Experimental and Numerical Investigation of Beer Foam".
2. WebFetch: Krüss AR275 (three light beers, 25 °C; no liquid fraction, no stout); Google Patents US4279938 (Guinness 1981; N2 0.015-0.035
   and CO2 0.8-1.8 vols/vol; no foam numbers); US9420822 (Sapporo; no foam numbers); Straight Dope "beer head data" (no data);
   ResearchGate Lyu et al. 2023 (403). USPTO PDF (403).
3. Local: Ronteltap 1989 thesis text (edepot.wur.nl/202245), p.85: "Suppose that a foam initially is 3 cm high, and contains 60% gas and
   40% liquid. For fresh beer foam these figures are quite realistic. Also suppose that as a result of drainage the foam contains 90% gas
   and 10% liquid after a certain period of time ... The decrease in foam height may take place in about two minutes" (CO2-beer foam,
   bubbles of order 200 µm; an illustration, not a measurement). p.102: "The density of the foam is defined as the ratio of the liquid in
   the foam and the foam volume ... can be measured in various obvious ways" (no stout values).
4. Exa search x1 (crawler road, used last): found only items already held, plus:
   - Guinness patent US4279938 (freepatentsonline): "In the latter--draught--case the foam is more creamy which can be objectively
     measured by a high foam density." (no number)
   - Maruszewski, Marquette bachelor essay: "For a stable foam, a surface tension of about 44 dynes is desirable." (44 mN/m; consistent
     with Völp & Willenbacher's 44.2 mN/m for Guinness)
   - ESGI 70 (Wits copy): "N2 is 100 times less soluble than CO2 at 8 °C" (by mass concentration; our molar ratio from Henry constants
     is 66).
   - Helm & Richardt 1936 J. Inst. Brew.: foam measured as "the quantity of beer found in the foam" (Carlsberg and Blom methods); no stout.
   - Constant 1992 J. Am. Soc. Brew. Chem. 50, 37: poured-foam density 0.14-0.24 g/mL at 1 min, commercial (CO2) beers (abstract).
RESULT: no published direct measurement of the liquid fraction or density of a Guinness or nitro-stout head was found. The derived value
(guinness-materials.md 2.5; wolfram/05): 0.24-0.36, from 34 µm bubbles held by surface tension against the head's weight, with 25-110 min
needed to drain to equilibrium. CO2-beer foams (bubbles ~10x larger) are drier at 1 min (0.14-0.24 g/mL, Constant 1992) because they
drain ~100x faster; this is consistent with, not a contradiction of, the derived range.
