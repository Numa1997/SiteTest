"The static shear modulus of a series of concentrated, well-characterized oil-in-water emulsions has been determined
and is found to be accurately represented by G = 1.769 σR32φ13(φ − 0.712),
   [ G = 1.769 (σ/R32) φ^(1/3) (φ − 0.712) ]
where σ is the interfacial tension, R 32 is the surface-volume mean drop radius, and φ is the volume fraction of the
dispersed phase. The equation is believed to have universal applicability, and should be equally valid for foams,
although the values of the numerical constants may depend slightly on the drop size distribution and the thickness
of the aqueous films between the droplets."

Corroboration (secondary): Paruta-Tuarez et al., Ind. Eng. Chem. Res. 50, 10359 (2011), Eqs. (6)-(7), quoted by Exa:
"E(phi) = 1.769 phi - 1.259 = 1.769 (phi - 0.712)" and "G = 1.769 sigma/R32 phi^(1/3) (phi - 0.712)".
Validity: Princen & Kiss data are for phi > 0.74 (Saint-Jalmes & Durian 1999, p. 1420: "experiments have only been
performed with high phi emulsions (phi > 0.75)").
[Inference: at phi = 1 the formula gives 1.769 x 0.288 = 0.509 σ/R32, the "0.51" often quoted; 0.51 is the dry-limit
value, not the prefactor.]
