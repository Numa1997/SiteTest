--- Sec. 2.2, channel-dominated (immobile surfaces) ---
"Assuming immobile PB surfaces, a Poiseuille flow is obtained inside these solid tubes ... In this case,
k(e) = Kc e L^2." "v = Kc rho g L^2 e/mu (2)" "for immobile PB walls, the value of Kc depends only on the PB geometry,
and numerical simulations give Kc = 1/150 ≈ 0.0067. This drainage regime is called the 'channel dominated' regime."

--- Sec. 2.2, node-dominated and series resistances ---
"k(e) = Kn √e L^2" "v = Kn rho g L^2 √e/mu (3)" "The value of Kn is expected to be of the same order of magnitude
as Kc." "L^2/k(e) = 1/(e Kc) + 1/(√e Kn)   (4)"

--- Sec. 2.4.1 (protein vs SDS, Fig. 5: D = 2 mm, casein vs SDS) ---
"interfaces covered by proteins usually have much higher interfacial elasticities and viscosities than surfactant
layers. For a small surfactant, v is proportional to √e (consistent with the node-dominated regime, eqn (3)), while
v ∝ e for the protein foam (as predicted for the channel-dominated regime, eqn (2))." Also: Kc and Kn extracted from
data "are not constant and depend on the system and bubble size."
