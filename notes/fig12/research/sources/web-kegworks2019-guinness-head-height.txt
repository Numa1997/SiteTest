TITLE: How to Pour a Guinness the Right Way...But Does it Really Matter?
AUTHOR: "Chris", KegWorks blog (draught-equipment retailer)
URL: https://www.kegworks.com/blog/how-to-pour-a-guinness-the-right-way-but-does-it-really-matter
DATE: 26 February 2019; DATE READ: 2026-11-13 (Exa web_fetch_exa, full page)
LICENCE: none stated
SAMPLE: Guinness Draught from a nitro tap at 35 psi, 20-oz Guinness Imperial Pint glass; one pour per method; head measured after 2 min settling.
METHOD: photograph + ruler, read in sixteenths of an inch. Single pours, no repeats.

--- verbatim ---
"Per Fergal Murray, Guinness master brewer and global ambassador, the head size on your Guinness should be between 18-20mm"
"We let each beer sit for 2 minutes after the process of the pour was complete. This allowed each beer to fully settle, and for Guinness to turn to its dark stout color. We then took a photo of each and measured the head size of each pour."
"We also used the same 20-oz Guinness Imperial Pint Glass for each beer."
"We poured all of these Guinness beers at 35 PSI."
"The amount of foamy head from the tried-and-true, proper way to pour a Guinness, measured in at 11/16" exactly."   [= 17.5 mm]
"The foam atop the Irish pour was measured at exactly 13/16", slightly above the ideal Guinness head size described by Fergal Murray."   [= 20.6 mm]
"The standard busy bartender pour produced a beer head size of exactly 14/16", a whopping 3/16" larger than our Guinness standard pour."   [= 22.2 mm]
"When looking at the three beers, it was clear that the bubbles on the Guinness pour were also the tightest."
"Let the beer surge, or cascade. According to Guinness, this process takes exactly 1 minute and 32.5 seconds."
"The official word from Guinness is that this entire 6-step process takes 119.5 seconds in total."
