p.47: "We measured viscosity using a calibrated Cannon-Fenske viscometer. The viscosities of (1) Budweiser beer
(containing CO2), (2) White Star Moet & Chandon Champagne (containing CO2), and (3) Guinness beer (containing N2) are,
respectively, 1.44, 1.67, and 1.40 times that of water."  (measurement temperature not stated)
p.48: "Guinness beer contains about 2 bars of N2 and 0.014 bars of CO2 (personal communication with the manufacturer).
Although the gas pressure is similar, the concentration of gas in Guinness beer is much lower than in Budweiser beer
because the solubility of N2 is only 1/65 times that of CO2."
p.49: "Hence, bubble growth rate in undegassed Guinness beer is much lower, only about 1/50 that in undegassed Budweiser
beer. Therefore, once released from a widget, the bubbles do not grow much at all in Guinness beer."
p.49: "a bubble with a radius of 0.5 mm rises in beer at 90 mm/s, but a bubble with a radius of 0.03 mm rises at only
1.0 mm/s."
READER NOTE: 0.014 bar CO2 conflicts with Guinness patents (0.8-1.8 vol/vol CO2, i.e. ~0.6-0.8 bar at 6 °C) and
with ESGI (0.8 bar); treat it as an error or mis-transcription.
