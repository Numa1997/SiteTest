Notation: phi = oil (dispersed) volume fraction; r = undeformed droplet radius; sigma = interfacial tension
(9.8 dyn/cm here); G'_p = plateau storage modulus; phi_eff = phi(1 + 3h/2r), h = water film thickness.
Symbols re-rendered from font glyphs.

--- p. 2051, abstract ---
"The elastic shear modulus of monodisperse emulsions is shown to exhibit a universal dependence on droplet volume
fraction phi when scaled by the Laplace pressure of the droplets, increasing as phi(phi - phi_c), where
phi_c ≈ 0.635, the value of random close packing of solid spheres."

--- p. 2052, dry limit ---
"A random, dry foam, is predicted to have G' ≈ 0.55 sigma/r [2]. Our results suggest that G' approaches
0.6 sigma/r, in excellent agreement with this prediction."

--- p. 2053, linear rise ---
"Here G'_p decreases approximately linearly with phi_eff - phi_c ... The data for Pi also exhibit an approximately
linear increase above phi_c, although with more scatter, until they diverge near phi_eff ≈ 0.8."

--- p. 2054, the scaling law ---
"Since the density of springs varies as phi_eff, we expect G'_p ~ phi_eff (phi_eff - phi_c) sigma/r; this does
describe the data, as shown by the solid line in Fig. 4."

NOTE (what is NOT printed): this paper gives NO numerical prefactor for G'_p = A phi(phi - phi_c) sigma/r.
The often-quoted "1.7" is not in the text. [Inference: matching the stated dry limit 0.6 sigma/r at phi = 1 with
phi_c = 0.635 gives A = 0.6/0.365 = 1.64 ≈ 1.6-1.7.] Data range: phi_eff ≈ 0.55-0.9, r = 0.25-0.74 µm (Fig. 3).
