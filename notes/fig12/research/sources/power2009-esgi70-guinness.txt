p.143: "Guinness Draught is unusual in that it is packaged and dispensed using a gas mixture of carbon dioxide (CO2) and
nitrogen (N2) ... at 3.8 bar and 8 C the gas mixture is about 79 mole % N2 and 21 mole % CO2. This yields the desired
CO2 concentration of ~ 2 g l^-1 and the desired N2 concentration of ~ 0.072 g l^-1"
p.143-144: "N2 is 100 times less soluble than CO2 at 8 C. ... The small bubbles give Guinness Draught a finer 'creamier'
head of foam than that on a carbonated beer like Guinness Extra Stout. Furthermore, the head is longer-lasting since
Ostwald ripening, the main process by which foam becomes coarser, is slowed down by a factor of 100 due to the lower
solubility of N2 [3]." [ref 3 = Bamforth, J. Inst. Brew. 110, 259 (2004)]
sec.2 (p.~147): "(gamma ≈ 40 mN m^-1 [3]) and an initial supersaturation ratio of 2.8, which is typical for draught
Guinness, the critical radius is 0.3 um." [surface tension value taken from Bamforth 2004, not measured here]
p.169: "As time progresses further, the mean bubble radius drops to about 50 um because the bubble growth rate decreases
