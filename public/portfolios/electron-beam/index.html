Project 07 · Electron optics

The Electron Beam, from Tip to Measurement

Numa Koudsie · lecture notes and simulations · every number computed

Question. Why does anyone build an electron beam, and what must the machine deliver? Chips have features a few nanometres wide and one defect can kill a chip, so every step is inspected and measured. Light cannot resolve such features; the electron wavelength at kilovolt energies is picometres. The beam is a probe scanned over the wafer, and what comes back gives edge position, height and material. The machine must give a spot thinner than the feature, enough current for a clean picture, a beam gentle enough for fragile resist, and speed, and these demands trade against each other. The chapters expose those trade-offs one by one.

Reading guide for the reviewer

What this page shows
A chain of short chapters that follows an electron from a metal tip to a measured line width on a wafer. Each chapter starts from a physical picture, derives its formulas step by step, and ends with a simulation whose sliders are the symbols of the formulas. The last chapters connect all stages into one machine and ask which parameter matters most.
What it models
The motion of a charged particle in electric and magnetic fields, field emission from a tip, acceleration, a magnetic lens with an analytic field, the aberrations that set the spot size, the scattering of electrons in a solid, and the statistics of a noisy line profile.
What it leaves out
No field solver: lens fields are an analytic model, not a finite-element or boundary-element computation. No aberration-corrected optics. No space-charge interaction between beam electrons unless a chapter says so. No proprietary detail and no model of any commercial tool.
Where each number comes from
Constants are CODATA 2018. Every number in the text is computed by the page’s own code and checked against an independent Python version. Published data are shown beside the model with their source, and the gap between model and data is stated.
