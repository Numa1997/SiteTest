Lecture note · Fig. 13 · stabilising an unstable periodic orbit

Catching the butterfly: theory and method

Numa Koudsie · 9 November 2026 · code: tools/fig13/gift/pendulum.js (in the browser), tools/fig13/butterfly_control.py and butterfly_physics.py (the reference)

Abstract. The butterfly is a periodic orbit of the double pendulum: struck from rest with \(p = (3.557, 12.211)\ \mathrm{kg\,m^2/s}\), it returns to its start after \(T = 2.659\ \mathrm{s}\) with the lower rod 24 turns further on. It is unstable, but barely: an error grows by a factor 1.0198 a lap. We show how a single motor at the top pin, acting as a torque \(\tau\) on the upper rod, can bring the pendulum from anywhere into this orbit. Two exact identities carry the method: the motor changes the energy at the rate \(\tau\,\dot\theta_1\) and the total angular momentum at the rate \(\tau\) minus gravity's torque. With them the motor pumps the energy to the butterfly's 390.8 J and sizes the slow swing; then it waits, motor off, for the free motion to pass close; then a feedback designed on the orbit itself, a periodic linear-quadratic regulator, catches and holds it; then it lets go. A miss drains the pendulum back into chaos for a fresh try. Measured on 300 random starts (three sets of 100): all caught and held, half of them within 18 s after the motor switches on, the slowest after 229 s.

01The picture

A child on a playground swing who wants to go higher does not fight the swing. She waits for the right moment in each swing and pushes a little, in step with the motion. And a juggler does not grab a falling club anywhere: he lets it come to his hand and then closes his fingers.

Catching the butterfly is both of these. The double pendulum is a slow pendulum (the upper rod, swinging out to ±157°) carrying a fast spinner (the lower rod, whirling round 24 times a lap). The motor at the top pin can only push on the upper rod. It pushes in step with the motion to give the pendulum the right energy and the right size of swing, as the child does. Then it lets the motion come close by itself, as the juggler does, and only then holds it with small, quick corrections. When the corrections have done their work, it lets go, and the pendulum keeps drawing the butterfly for minutes on its own, because the orbit is only barely unstable.

Two facts make this possible, and both are exact. Torque times angular speed is power: a push on a rod that is turning the same way adds energy, one against the motion takes it away. And a torque at the pin is the only thing besides gravity that changes the pendulum's total angular momentum. Everything below follows from these two facts and from looking at the butterfly as a closed loop in the four-dimensional space of the pendulum's states.

02The machine and its equations

Two equal uniform rods, a frictionless pin at the top and one between the rods, gravity, and a motor at the top pin.

Each rod has mass \(m = 1\ \mathrm{kg}\) and length \(l = 1\ \mathrm{m}\); \(g = 9.8\ \mathrm{m/s^2}\). The angles \(\theta_1, \theta_2\) are measured from straight down. A uniform rod about its centre has moment of inertia \(I = ml^2/12\), and its centre lies at \(a = l/2\) from its upper end. The kinetic energy is that of the upper rod turning about the pin plus that of the lower rod's centre moving and the lower rod turning about its centre; the potential energy is that of the two centres of mass:

\[ \mathcal L = \tfrac12 A\,\dot\theta_1^2 + \tfrac12 B\,\dot\theta_2^2 + C\cos(\theta_1-\theta_2)\,\dot\theta_1\dot\theta_2 + U_1\cos\theta_1 + U_2\cos\theta_2, \tag{1} \] \[ A = ma^2 + I + ml^2 = \tfrac43\ \mathrm{kg\,m^2},\quad B = ma^2 + I = \tfrac13\ \mathrm{kg\,m^2},\quad C = mla = \tfrac12\ \mathrm{kg\,m^2},\quad U_1 = g(ma+ml) = 14.7\ \mathrm{N\,m},\quad U_2 = gma = 4.9\ \mathrm{N\,m}. \tag{2} \]

The conjugate momenta \(p_i = \partial\mathcal L/\partial\dot\theta_i\) are \(p_1 = A\dot\theta_1 + C\cos(\theta_1-\theta_2)\dot\theta_2\) and \(p_2 = B\dot\theta_2 + C\cos(\theta_1-\theta_2)\dot\theta_1\). Solving this linear pair for the angular speeds and forming \(H = \sum p_i\dot\theta_i - \mathcal L\) gives the Hamiltonian

\[ H = \frac{B\,p_1^2 - 2C\cos(\theta_1-\theta_2)\,p_1p_2 + A\,p_2^2}{2\,\big(AB - C^2\cos^2(\theta_1-\theta_2)\big)} - U_1\cos\theta_1 - U_2\cos\theta_2 . \tag{3} \]

The motor exerts a torque \(\tau\) between the fixed support and the upper rod. Its virtual work is \(\tau\,\delta\theta_1\), so it enters as a generalised force on \(\theta_1\) only, and Hamilton's equations become

\[ \dot\theta_i = \frac{\partial H}{\partial p_i},\qquad \dot p_1 = -\frac{\partial H}{\partial\theta_1} + \tau,\qquad \dot p_2 = -\frac{\partial H}{\partial\theta_2}. \tag{4} \]

One number sets the regime: the energy compared with what gravity can give or take. The potential energy ranges over \(\pm(U_1+U_2) = \pm 19.6\ \mathrm{J}\). The butterfly carries \(E^* = 390.78\ \mathrm{J}\), twenty times more. At such energies gravity steers the motion but does not dominate it; at energies of the order of \(U_1+U_2\) the double pendulum is chaotic almost everywhere. The motor's limit is \(|\tau| \le 60\ \mathrm{N\,m}\), about four times \(U_1\).

03The target: an unstable periodic orbit

The butterfly is not a place but a loop: a closed path in the space of states \(s = (\theta_1, \theta_2, p_1, p_2)\).

Struck from rest with \(p^* = (3.557313,\ 12.211005)\ \mathrm{kg\,m^2/s}\), that is with angular speeds \(\dot\theta_1 = -25.30\ \mathrm{rad/s}\), \(\dot\theta_2 = +74.59\ \mathrm{rad/s}\), the pendulum returns to exactly its starting state after

\[ s(T) = s(0) + (0,\ 2\pi\cdot 24,\ 0,\ 0),\qquad T = 2.659405\ \mathrm{s}. \tag{5} \]

Equation (5) is a boundary condition in time; the strike was found by Newton's method on it (tools/fig13/butterfly.py), closing to about \(10^{-11}\). Call the orbit \(s^*(t)\).

Stability. Follow a nearby state \(s^*(0)+\delta s\) for one lap. To first order the error obeys the variational equation \(\dot{\delta s} = J(s^*(t))\,\delta s\), with \(J = \partial f/\partial s\) the Jacobian of the right-hand side of (4); integrated over a lap it gives the monodromy matrix \(\Phi(T)\), \(\delta s(T) = \Phi(T)\,\delta s(0)\). Its eigenvalues, the Floquet multipliers, computed from the exact Jacobian (SymPy) and DOP853 at relative tolerance \(10^{-12}\), are

\[ \mu = 1.01977,\quad 0.98062,\quad 1 \pm 0.0017i,\qquad \det\Phi(T) = 1 \pm 2\times10^{-12}. \tag{6} \]

The pair near 1 belongs to every periodic orbit of a conservative system: a shift along the orbit stays a shift, and a change of energy changes the period (it would be a Jordan block at exactly 1; the 0.0017 is the numerical splitting of that block). The determinant 1 is Liouville's theorem: the flow keeps phase-space volume. The other pair, \(\mu\) and \(1/\mu\), says the butterfly is a saddle: one direction grows by 1.98 % a lap, one shrinks by the same factor. An error of \(10^{-6}\) needs \(\ln(10^5)/\ln 1.0198 \approx 590\) laps, 26 minutes, to reach 0.1. Measured on the full equations, on 40 pendulums the motor had caught and released (section 11): they stayed within 0.1 of the orbit for 403 s in the median (about 150 laps), the shortest for 41 s, the longest for more than 800 s. That is shorter than the 2 % a lap suggests, because the loop stretches a small error unevenly: within the first few laps it can grow twenty times before the slow growth takes over. One run followed for 1500 laps (66 minutes) kept its wings between 135.55° and 135.83°, never over the top: the exact drawing is lost within minutes, the butterfly shape is not.

Why catching is needed at all

A saddle is never reached by the free motion from outside: the states that flow into it form a thin surface (its stable manifold), and a chaotic or a regular motion crosses near it only now and then. Without friction, nothing can make the free pendulum settle on the butterfly; with friction, it would lose the 390.8 J. Something has to put energy in, choose the swing, and correct the last error: the motor.

04The motor's two levers

One torque, two exact effects.

Energy. Along a solution of (4),

\[ \frac{dH}{dt} = \sum_i\Big(\frac{\partial H}{\partial\theta_i}\dot\theta_i + \frac{\partial H}{\partial p_i}\dot p_i\Big) = \sum_i\Big(\frac{\partial H}{\partial\theta_i}\frac{\partial H}{\partial p_i} - \frac{\partial H}{\partial p_i}\frac{\partial H}{\partial\theta_i}\Big) + \frac{\partial H}{\partial p_1}\,\tau = \tau\,\dot\theta_1 . \tag{7} \]

The brackets cancel term by term; what is left is the power of the motor, torque times the angular speed of the rod it drives.

Angular momentum. Let \(P = p_1 + p_2\). The kinetic part of (3) depends on the angles only through \(\theta_1-\theta_2\), so its derivatives with respect to \(\theta_1\) and \(\theta_2\) are equal and opposite and drop out of \(\dot p_1 + \dot p_2\):

\[ \frac{dP}{dt} = \tau - U_1\sin\theta_1 - U_2\sin\theta_2 . \tag{8} \]

This is the torque balance about the pin for the whole pendulum: the motor's torque and gravity's torque, nothing else. Without gravity \(P\) would be conserved (Noether's theorem for rotations about the pin).

What \(P\) measures. On the butterfly the lower rod spins fast, and the sum \(P\) swings slowly with the upper rod, between 1.23 and 15.77 kg·m²/s each lap. Averaged over a whirl of the lower rod, the term \(U_2\sin\theta_2\) nearly cancels and (8) reads \(\dot P \approx \tau - U_1\sin\theta_1\): \(P\) is the momentum of the slow pendulum. Its largest value over a swing, \(P_{\text{reach}} = \max|P|\), measures how big the swing is; on the butterfly \(P^*_{\text{reach}} = 15.77\ \mathrm{kg\,m^2/s}\).

05Pump and steer

First give the pendulum the butterfly's energy and the butterfly's swing.

The law the motor follows in this phase is

\[ \tau = \Big(k_E\,(E^*-E) + k_I\,\tfrac{1}{20}\!\int\!(E^*-E)\,dt\Big)\,\dot\theta_1 \;+\; \Big(k_A\,(P^*_{\text{reach}}-P_{\text{reach}}) + k_I\!\int\!(P^*_{\text{reach}}-P_{\text{reach}})\,dt\Big)\,P, \tag{9} \]

clipped to \(|\tau| \le 60\ \mathrm{N\,m}\), with \(k_E = 0.01\), \(k_A = 0.05\), \(k_I = 0.005\) (SI units), \(P_{\text{reach}}\) taken over the last 3 s.

The energy term is energy shaping, the method Åström and Furuta used to swing a pendulum up. Alone, it gives by (7) \(\dot E = k_E(E^*-E)\dot\theta_1^2\). With \(V = \tfrac12(E-E^*)^2\),

\[ \dot V = (E-E^*)\,\dot E = -k_E\,(E-E^*)^2\,\dot\theta_1^2 \le 0, \tag{10} \]

so the energy error never grows and shrinks whenever the upper rod moves: a Lyapunov function. The push is in step with the rod's own motion, like the child on the swing.

The swing term pushes along \(P\) when the swing is too small and against it when too big; by (8) it feeds or brakes the slow pendulum. The integrals are memories against a steady error, as in the PID control of Fig. 9: a proportional push alone needs a leftover error to keep pushing. The two terms interact (energy given to the swing is energy taken from the spin, at fixed total), so (9) has no simple Lyapunov proof; it is tuned and measured (section 11).

When \(|E-E^*| < 5\ \mathrm{J}\) and \(|P_{\text{reach}} - P^*_{\text{reach}}| < 3\ \mathrm{kg\,m^2/s}\), and at least 3 s after the motor started, the pendulum is moving like a butterfly of nearly the right size; its phase, where it is along the loop compared with the spin, is still wrong.

06Wait, and the mirror butterfly

The motor switches off and watches.

This is the idea of Ott, Grebogi and Yorke: a motion that wanders near an unstable orbit comes close to it from time to time by itself; one waits for that moment and then uses small corrections only. Here the distance from the orbit is

\[ d(s) = \min_{k,\ \sigma=\pm1}\ \Big(\sum_{i=1}^{4} Q_i\,\big[s_i - \sigma\,s^*_i(t_k)\big]^2\Big)^{1/2},\qquad Q = (1,\ 1,\ 0.05,\ 0.05), \tag{11} \]

with the angles compared modulo \(2\pi\), over the 2400 stored points \(s^*(t_k)\) of the lap: the distance to the nearest point of the loop, whatever the timing. Angles count in radians and momenta in kg·m²/s, with the weight 0.05 because a momentum error of 1 kg·m²/s matters about as much as an angle error of 0.2 rad.

The mirror. The Hamiltonian (3) is even: \(H(-\theta,-p) = H(\theta,p)\). So if \(s(t)\) solves (4) with torque \(\tau(t)\), then \(-s(t)\) solves it with \(-\tau(t)\). The mirror butterfly \(-s^*(t)\), the same drawing flipped left to right, is an orbit just as good, held by the same gains with the torque reversed. That is the \(\sigma = \pm1\) in (11). When the distance falls below 0.6, the catch begins. If nothing comes within 0.6 in 12 s, it is a miss (section 8).

07The catch: feedback designed on the orbit★

Near the loop the motion is nearly linear, and a linear controller can be designed exactly for it.

Linearising along the orbit. Divide the lap into \(M = 2400\) steps of \(h = T/M = 1.108\ \mathrm{ms}\), the motor's control period. Over step \(k\), with the torque held constant,

\[ \delta s_{k+1} = \Phi_k\,\delta s_k + b_k\,\tau_k,\qquad \Phi_k = \frac{\partial s_{k+1}}{\partial s_k}\Big|_{s^*},\quad b_k = \frac{\partial s_{k+1}}{\partial\tau}\Big|_{s^*}. \tag{12} \]

The product \(\Phi_{M-1}\cdots\Phi_0\) is the monodromy matrix of (6). The 2400 matrices are periodic in \(k\): the problem is a linear system with periodic coefficients, the discrete twin of Floquet theory.

One motor, four directions. The controllability Gramian over one lap,

\[ W = \sum_{k=0}^{M-1} \Phi_{M-1}\cdots\Phi_{k+1}\;b_k\,b_k^{\mathsf T}\;\big(\Phi_{M-1}\cdots\Phi_{k+1}\big)^{\mathsf T} h, \tag{13} \]

is the matrix whose quadratic form tells how much a unit of torque effort moves the end-of-lap state in each direction. Its eigenvalues are \(3.5\times10^{-8},\ 2.6\times10^{-7},\ 2.0\times10^{-5},\ 4.0\times10^{-5}\): all positive, so the pendulum is controllable through the top pin alone, although underactuated; the smallest belongs to a change of the energy (its eigenvector lies within 8° of the gradient of \(H\)), the costliest thing to change.

The periodic linear-quadratic regulator. Choose the torques to minimise

\[ \mathcal J = \sum_k \Big(\delta s_k^{\mathsf T}\,Q\,h\,\delta s_k + R\,\tau_k^2\Big),\qquad R = 10^{-3}\ \mathrm{(N\,m)^{-2}}, \tag{14} \]

a balance between the error and the effort. Dynamic programming backwards from the end gives the Riccati recursion for a cost matrix \(\Pi_k\) and the gains,

\[ \Pi_k = Qh + \Phi_k^{\mathsf T}\Pi_{k+1}\Phi_k - \frac{\Phi_k^{\mathsf T}\Pi_{k+1}b_k\;b_k^{\mathsf T}\Pi_{k+1}\Phi_k}{R + b_k^{\mathsf T}\Pi_{k+1}b_k},\qquad K_k = \frac{b_k^{\mathsf T}\Pi_{k+1}\Phi_k}{R + b_k^{\mathsf T}\Pi_{k+1}b_k},\qquad \tau_k = -K_k\,\delta s_k . \tag{15} \]

Run backwards around the lap again and again, \(\Pi\) settles on a periodic solution (here after 5 laps); its gains \(K_k\) are the controller. With them, the closed-loop monodromy \(\prod_k(\Phi_k - b_kK_k)\) has all four multipliers of modulus 0.074 to 0.081: an error shrinks to 8 % of itself each lap, where the free orbit would have let it grow by 2 %.

Following the loop, not a clock. The reference \(s^*(t_k)\) used in \(\delta s_k = s - \sigma s^*(t_k)\) is re-chosen every step as the nearest point of the loop (11), searching 30 steps around the last one. A clock-locked reference would ask the motor to correct the timing too. The timing can only be changed by changing the energy and waiting while the period shifts, and (13) shows the energy to be the costliest direction; tried, it saturated the motor and lost the pendulum. The re-indexed reference lets the pendulum keep its own timing and corrects only the error across the loop.

How far away it can catch. Measured (section 11), from 200 random states at each distance: from 0.1 the catch holds 199 times, from 0.2 171, from 0.3 140, from 0.4 112, from 0.6 62, from 0.8 26. The threshold 0.6 is a trade. A catch tried from that far holds about one time in three, and a slip (distance above 1) is a miss and a new try; but a closer pass is rarer, and waiting for it costs more time than the lost tries (with the threshold at 0.2 only 29 of 40 starts were caught within 400 s).

08Hold, let go, and miss

The motor holds the pendulum at least four laps and until it is within 0.005 of the loop, then switches off.

Held, the corrections shrink below 0.1 N·m; released, the pendulum is free and keeps drawing the butterfly by the slow instability of section 3. Measured on 300 released runs: within 0.005 of the loop at the release by this rule, and four free laps later 0.0017 in the median, nine in ten below 0.016, the worst 0.058.

A miss (steering not settled within 12 s, nothing near within 12 s of waiting, or a catch that slips past 1) makes the motor drain the pendulum back down to 60 J with the energy law (9) aimed at 60 J (at most 6 s), and start again. At 60 J the double pendulum is chaotic, and chaos scrambles the next approach: each try is a fresh draw from the many ways of having the right energy and swing. The search is a sequence of independent tries, each with a fair chance.

09Flowers: the same motor, a different lock

The flower hunt found ten flowers and three more butterflies (The Flower Hunt). The reader can ask the motor for any of them. The flowers need a different law, and it works better than the butterfly's.

The picture. A skater in a spin pulls her arms in and turns faster, but nothing pushes her round: how much she turns, her angular momentum, stays what it was. A double pendulum that whirls fast is nearly that skater. Gravity can give or take 39 J at most between the lowest and the highest pose; a flower carries 262 to 970 J. Gravity is a ripple on it. Without gravity two things would stay exactly constant with the motor off: the energy \(E\), and by (8) the angular momentum about the pin, \(P = p_1 + p_2\).

Measured on the ten flowers: over a lap \(P\) stays within 1 to 8 % of its mean \(\bar P\) (standard deviation 0.3 to 0.8 against means of 7.6 to 27.3 kg·m²/s). On the butterfly it runs between 1.2 and 15.8. So the orbits fall into two kinds, and design() tells them apart by that spread: swinging (the butterflies: the upper rod swings to and fro) and whirling (the flowers: both rods go round).

Why two numbers are enough for the picture. Without gravity nothing marks a direction in space. Turn the whole pendulum about the pin by any angle \(\psi\) and it moves exactly as before. So every motion with the flower's energy and the flower's \(\bar P\), with the lower rod overtaking the upper one in the same sense, draws the same flower, turned by some angle. Set \(E\) and \(\bar P\) and the pen already draws the flower. A third number, the turn \(\psi\), puts it in place.

Setting two things with one torque. Take \(\tau = a\,\dot\theta_1 + b\). By (7) and (8), averaged over a few petals (gravity averages out),

\[ \dot E = a\,\langle\dot\theta_1^2\rangle + b\,\langle\dot\theta_1\rangle,\qquad \dot P = a\,\langle\dot\theta_1\rangle + b . \tag{16} \]

Ask for the energy and the angular momentum to close on their targets at chosen rates, \(\dot E = c_E\,(E^* - E)\) and \(\dot P = c_L\,(P_{\text{ref}} - \langle P\rangle)\), and solve (16):

\[ a = \frac{\dot E - \langle\dot\theta_1\rangle\,\dot P}{\langle\dot\theta_1^2\rangle - \langle\dot\theta_1\rangle^2},\qquad b = \dot P - a\,\langle\dot\theta_1\rangle . \tag{17} \]

The denominator is the unevenness of the upper rod's turning. If the rod turned perfectly evenly it would be zero, and one torque could not set two things: a steady push would change both together and nothing could separate them. The rod speeds up and slows down with each petal, and that is what the motor uses. In the program \(c_E = 2\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}\), \(c_L = 1.5\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}\), the averages are running means over 0.2 s, and below 0.6 \(E^*\) the motor simply pumps with the energy term of (9). Of the two mirror images the motor takes the one whose lower rod already turns the right way: it holds the upper rod and cannot quickly reverse the lower one.

Turning the flower into place. The angle between the rods and the two momenta do not change when the pendulum is turned as a whole. Matching \((\theta_1 - \theta_2,\ p_1,\ p_2)\) to the stored lap gives the place \(k\) on the lap, and then the turn is \(\psi = \theta_1 - \theta_1^*(t_k)\). A little too much angular momentum makes the whole flower creep forward:

\[ \dot\psi = c_\psi\,\big(\langle P\rangle - \bar P\big). \tag{18} \]

\(c_\psi\) is measured once per flower: strike it slightly differently along the direction \((-\dot\theta_2,\ \dot\theta_1)\) in \((p_1, p_2)\), which leaves the energy alone, run one lap and read the turn. It comes out between 0.58 and 4.46 rad/s per kg·m²/s. So the motor asks for a slightly wrong angular momentum on purpose,

\[ P_{\text{ref}} = \pm\bar P - \frac{k_\psi}{c_\psi}\,\psi,\qquad\text{so that}\qquad \dot\psi = -k_\psi\,\psi,\quad k_\psi = 0.8\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}, \tag{19} \]

never more than 12 % off \(\bar P\). The turn dies away and the detuning with it. This is a phase-locked loop, the circuit that keeps a radio on its station: run a little fast while behind, a little slow while ahead. The flower counts as caught when the distance (11) falls below 0.2, and as lost above 0.7; a lost flower still has its energy and angular momentum, so the motor steers on without starting over.

Four flowers are caught with the gains of section 7 instead: the 5 petals at 768 J and at 669 J, the unstable 7 petals at 313 J and the 8 petals at 506 J. On these the upper rod stops and turns back during the lap, the turn loop (18) has no steady whirl to work on, and the catch of section 7 takes over at \(d < 0.6\) once \(E\) and \(\bar P\) are set.

Measured · 60 starts for each pattern

The page's own start and 59 poses as a finger leaves them, Catch it pressed after 4 s; "held" means still caught six seconds (or six laps) later; 300 s allowed. Each of the ten flowers: held in 60 of 60, never needing to start over; half within 2.6 to 5.7 s of the press, the slowest 14 s. The butterflies stay on the wait-and-catch of sections 5 to 8 and are slower: 12 turns 56 of 60 (median 12 s); 18 turns 60 (8 s); 24 turns 60 (21 s, slowest 122 s); 32 turns 60 (35 s, slowest 161 s). For the 24-turn butterfly the first catch holds in about six of ten tries; the others slip and the motor starts over.

How well they are held. The six flowers on the lock stay within 0.04 to 0.09 of the loop on average (0.18 at worst) with a mean torque of 0.3 to 1.1 N·m: the lock keeps trimming and never quite rests. The four caught with gains settle towards zero torque, like the butterfly. After letting go, 20 s later: the stable flowers (3, 5 and 8 petals) are still within 0.04; the neutral ones have drifted by up to 0.6 (the flower turns slowly); the unstable 7-petal flower is gone (1.5), as it must be.

10The program

A state machine of five phases, and three computations behind it.

Before the film starts (PEND.design(), about 0.1 s in the browser): integrate the butterfly from its strike, store its 2401 states; obtain each \(\Phi_k, b_k\) of (12) by central differences of one control step (\(\varepsilon = 10^{-6}\) in the state, \(10^{-3}\ \mathrm{N\,m}\) in the torque); run the recursion (15) around the lap until \(\Pi\) changes by less than \(10^{-9}\) of itself in a lap; keep the 2400 gain rows \(K_k\).

While the film plays (Run.advance): every control step of 1.108 ms, compute the torque from the phase's law, hold it, and integrate (4) over the step with four classical Runge–Kutta sub-steps of 0.277 ms; record the state every four steps for the drawing. The film's clock asks for the state at its own time and the run integrates just ahead of it (0.15 s, so a press is answered at once), so the motion is computed as you watch, never recorded.

for each control step k (h = T/2400):
    E, w1, P      = energy(s), dtheta1/dt, p1 + p2
    reach         = max |P| over the last 3 s
    phase chaos:  tau = 0                                   until the reader presses Catch it or taps a pattern
    (a flower: laws (17) and (19) instead of pump and wait; caught at d < 0.2, lost at d > 0.7)
    phase pump:   tau = law (9)                             -> wait when |E - E*| < 5 J and |reach - 15.77| < 3
                  (a drain after a miss aims law (9) at 60 J)
    phase wait:   tau = 0; d, nearest point, mirror sign = (11)  -> catch when d < 0.6, miss after 12 s
    phase catch:  re-index to the nearest point; tau = -K[k] (s - sign S[k])   (15)
                  miss if d > 1; held until the reader presses Let go
                  (the gift film, ?auto=1: on after 6 s, let go after 4 laps when d < 0.005)
    tau = clip(tau, -60, 60) N m
    integrate (4) over h with 4 RK4 sub-steps, tau held

12Equation to screen

What each drawn thing is.

  • The rods: \(\theta_1, \theta_2\) of the live run; the pen at the tip draws \(x = l(\sin\theta_1+\sin\theta_2)\), \(h = -l(\cos\theta_1+\cos\theta_2)\); the trail is the last 0.8 s while free or searching, the last two laps once caught.
  • The motor: six stator coils glow in proportion to the current \(|I| = |\tau|/k_t\); the motor constant \(k_t = 2.5\ \mathrm{N\,m/A}\) is assumed (a direct-drive torque motor; 60 N·m at 24 A); it is not part of the model, which takes the torque as given. The four rotor magnets turn with \(\theta_1\). The arc beside the motor is \(\tau\), its length \(|\tau|/60\ \mathrm{N\,m}\).
  • The faint butterfly is \(s^*(t)\), the target; it fades once the pendulum is held.
  • Energy is \(H\) of (3); the bar's mark is \(E^*\). Distance is \(d\) of (11), shown from the waiting phase on.
  • The clock: the free pendulum at real speed; the motor at work and the butterfly at half speed (the butterfly's lower rod turns nine times a second); a new try after a miss at double speed. The physics does not change with the clock; only what one second of film shows.

13Limits and the next model

An ideal pendulum and an ideal motor.

The pins have no friction; the motor makes exactly the torque asked, at once, held over each 1.1 ms; the controller knows the state exactly. A real machine needs: friction, which at 9 whirls a second would drain the butterfly within seconds and make the motor work all the time, not only to catch; the motor's electrical dynamics (inductance, back-emf, \(V = R_mI + \Lambda\dot I + k_t\dot\theta_1\), as in Fig. 9) between the command and the torque; measurement, and an observer that estimates \(p_1, p_2\) from encoder angles; and limits on speed. The method has no proof of global success: the measured catch rate is a statistic over 100 starts, not a theorem. Natural next steps are a transverse (orbital) linearisation that removes the timing direction from the design itself (Shiriaev and co-workers), and model-predictive control that plans the approach over a lap instead of waiting for it.

Problems
  1. A pendulum hanging still is struck with \(p_1 = 2\), \(p_2 = 4\) kg·m²/s. Its kinetic energy is \(K = \tfrac{6}{7}p_1^2 - \tfrac{18}{7}p_1p_2 + \tfrac{24}{7}p_2^2\), and hanging straight down its potential energy is −19.6 J. Show that its energy is 18.1 J. The butterfly has 390.8 J. How much must the motor add?
  2. The motor can give at most 60 N·m. Suppose the upper rod turned steadily at 10 rad/s and the motor pushed with it all the time. Use \(\dot H = \tau\,\dot\theta_1\) to find how long the pumping would take. The live catch needs far longer. Give two reasons from the text.
  3. Start from Hamilton's equations with the torque \(\tau\) added to \(\dot p_1\), and derive \(\dot H = \tau\,\dot\theta_1\) in three lines. Which terms cancel, and why does the torque on the upper rod alone still change the energy of both rods?
