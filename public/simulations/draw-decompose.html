From physics to pixels

How a pen stroke becomes 256 complex numbers, how those become a spectrum, and why a handful of circles already draws the shape.

01 · Physical picture

A bead on a bent wire

Bend a thin wire of uniform density into the shape you drew and slide a bead around it at constant speed, once per loop. The bead’s motion is periodic, and any periodic motion is a sum of harmonics, like the overtones of a plucked string. In the plane each harmonic is a uniform circular motion that turns a whole number of times k per loop, clockwise or anticlockwise. The figure finds those circles and chains them tip to tail, so the last tip rides the wire again.

Most of the shape lives in a few slow, large circles: the k = ±1 pair alone draws an ellipse of the right size and tilt. Faster, smaller circles add corners and wiggles. Fig. 8b lists the circle radii; the slider K decides how many you keep. This is Fig. 7 run backwards: there the circles are given and the shape follows; here the shape is given and the circles follow.

It is kinematics, not dynamics. No force makes the bead move this way; the circles are a complete description of the motion, not an explanation of it. That is also why Ptolemy’s epicycles fitted the planets so well without being the right physics.

02 · Model

A closed curve as a periodic complex function

Put the drawing in the complex plane, z = x + iy, and parametrise it by a time t that runs once from 0 to 2π around the curve. A closed curve is then a 2π-periodic function z(t), and any such function with finite energy has a Fourier series

z(t) = Σk=−∞∞ ck eikt,   ck = (1/2π) ∫02π z(t) e−ikt dtk: whole turns per loop · k > 0 anticlockwise, k < 0 clockwise

Here t is the bead’s loop parameter (one loop = 2π), not a physical clock; the animation simply plays it at one loop per Tloop seconds. Each term ck eikt is a phasor exactly as in Fig. 7: radius |ck|, angular frequency k, starting angle arg ck. Unlike a real signal, a curve in the plane is complex, so c−k is not the conjugate of ck: clockwise and anticlockwise circles are independent, and both are needed.

Why the exponentials pick out one circle each

The functions eikt are orthonormal on a loop:

(1/2π) ∫02π eijt e−ikt dt = δjk

Multiply the series by e−ikt and average over a loop: every circle with j ≠ k spins a whole number of times relative to the probe and averages to zero, and only ck survives. That is the formula for ck above, and it is the reason each coefficient can be computed on its own.

03 · Derivation

From integral to sum, and what truncation costs

The discrete transform

The page samples the curve at N = 256 equally spaced parameter values tn = 2πn/N. The integral becomes a Riemann sum:

ck ≈ (1/N) Σn=0N−1 zn e−2πikn/Nthe discrete Fourier transform, normalised by 1/N

On the sample points the orthogonality is exact, (1/N) Σn e2πi(j−k)n/N = δjk for j and k distinct modulo N, a finite geometric series that sums to zero. So keeping every coefficient reproduces all N samples exactly.

Parseval: the wire’s moment of inertia, circle by circle

Square and average the discrete series. Cross terms vanish by the same orthogonality, leaving

(1/N) Σn |zn|² = Σk |ck|²mean squared distance from the centre = sum of squared radii

Because the samples are equally spaced along the wire and the mean has been removed, the left side is the wire’s moment of inertia about its centre of mass divided by its mass, I/m. Each circle carries the share |ck|² of it, with no cross terms. Now keep only a set S of K coefficients. The error at the samples is the sum of the dropped circles, and by the same identity its mean square is the share of I/m you dropped:

eRMS² = (1/N) Σn |zn − zS(tn)|² = ⟨|z|²⟩ − Σk∈S |ck|²

So the best K-term approximation, in the least-squares sense, keeps the K largest |ck|. That is why the page sorts by amplitude. The panel shows the error twice: once computed point by point, once from this formula. They agree to rounding, which is a check on the whole pipeline.

TryPick Square and set K = 4. For a square traced at constant speed only k = 1 − 4m survive (k = 1, −3, 5, −7, …), because of its four-fold symmetry. Watch the spectrum: every fourth bar is lit, the rest are near zero.

How fast the radii fall

Integrate the coefficient formula by parts: ck = (1/ik) · (1/2π) ∫ ż(t) e−ikt dt. Each time the curve can be differentiated once more without a jump, another factor 1/k appears. A smooth heart has radii that drop off very fast; a square, whose velocity jumps at the corners, only falls as 1/k²; an open stroke, which the series must close with a jump, falls as 1/k and rings near the jump (Gibbs phenomenon).

04 · Numerical method

Resample, centre, transform, sort

  • Resampling: the raw stroke has uneven spacing (you draw faster in some places). The page walks along the polyline and places N = 256 points at equal arc length, by linear interpolation. Equal arc length means the pen moves at constant speed, so t is proportional to distance travelled.
  • Centring: the mean of the samples is subtracted, so c0 = 0 and the shape is drawn about the centre of the figure.
  • Transform: a direct sum over n for each k from −60 to 60 (k = 0 omitted), 120 × 256 ≈ 31 000 complex products, done once per shape. A fast Fourier transform is not needed at this size.
  • Sorting: coefficients are sorted by |ck| in descending order; the first K are used, and the largest circle sits at the centre of the chain.

Every frame, the parameter advances as t ← t + 2π Δt / Tloop, with Δt the real time between frames, and the chain of K phasors is evaluated in closed form. There is no integration and no accumulated error.

05 · On screen

Units and colours

The drawing is stored in scale-free units: one unit is 0.46 of the shorter side of the figure, the origin at its centre and y pointing up. Resizing the window therefore never distorts a shape. Points closer than 0.006 units to the previous one are skipped while drawing.

After decomposition the page finds the outermost point drawn over a whole loop: the largest |joint| + radius over all circles at 256 instants, and the farthest point of your centred drawing. If that exceeds one unit, the view zooms out so it lands at 46 % of the shorter side; it zooms out at once and back in with a 0.4 s time constant when you lower K. Nothing leaves the figure.

The traced reconstruction is burgundy and fades over one loop; your centred drawing is a dashed ink line; each circle is rose. In Fig. 8b each stem is |ck| scaled to the largest one, burgundy if the term is among the K in use.

06 · Limits

What the numbers do not tell you

  • Only |k| ≤ 60 are computed, so even K = 120 is not exact: the ⟨|z|²⟩ readout tells you how much is missing.
  • The error is measured at the 256 sample points. Between them a truncated series can overshoot, most visibly next to corners and jumps.
  • An open stroke is closed by the series with a straight jump from end to start; the ringing there does not shrink as K grows, it only gets narrower (about 9 % overshoot, Gibbs).
  • Arc-length resampling cuts corners by at most one sample spacing: for a long stroke that spacing is the total length divided by 255.
