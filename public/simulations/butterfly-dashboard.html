The Wave and the Circle.

A point that goes round a circle traces a sine wave in time. Here that happens in one picture: the plane on the left is the wall the motion happens in, the waves are its height and its sideways position against time, joined to it by straight lines. Then the paper with the wave on it is turned a quarter turn, and the wave becomes the circle again. First a wheel, then a double pendulum that draws a butterfly.

Study note · Fig. 13 · the wave and the circle

One motion, three shadows

1. The plane the motion lives in

Hold a torch level and let a bicycle wheel turn in front of a wall: the shadow of a spoke tip on the floor slides to and fro, its shadow on the side wall goes up and down. Neither shadow is a circle; both are waves. The circle is only seen from the front.

Put the front view in numbers. Call the sideways position \(x\) and the height \(h\), both in metres, and write the point as one complex number,

\[ z = x + i\,h . \tag{1} \]

The plane of \(z\) is not an abstraction here: it is the wall the motion happens in. For a point on a wheel of radius \(r\) turning at angular speed \(\omega\),

\[ z(t) = r\,e^{i\omega t},\qquad x = r\cos\omega t,\qquad h = r\sin\omega t . \tag{2} \]

For the double pendulum of two rods of length \(l\), each angle measured from straight down, the lower rod's tip sits at \(x = l(\sin\theta_1+\sin\theta_2)\), \(h = -l(\cos\theta_1+\cos\theta_2)\). Since \(-i\,e^{i\theta} = \sin\theta - i\cos\theta\),

\[ z(t) = -i\,l\left(e^{i\theta_1(t)} + e^{i\theta_2(t)}\right). \tag{3} \]

The two rods are two rotating arrows added head to tail; the plane on the left of the figure is the real wall the pendulum swings in. Nothing about it is a momentum picture: the kick \((p_1,p_2)\) only chooses which motion happens (Fig. 13).

2. The waves and the straight lines

The height \(h = \operatorname{Im} z\) is drawn against time to the right of the plane, at the same height scale, the newest instant at the left edge and earlier ones further right. The line joining the moving point to the head of that wave is horizontal at every instant, because both ends are at the same \(h\). The sideways position \(x = \operatorname{Re} z\) is drawn below the plane, time running downward, and joined by a vertical line, because both ends share \(x\). For the wheel these are the sine and the cosine of (2).

3. Turning the paper

Give every instant a third coordinate, the time axis: \(\tau = k\,a\), where \(a\) is how long ago the instant was and \(k\) is a scale in pixels per second. The motion becomes a curve in space, \((x, h, \tau)\): a helix for the wheel. Each panel is a view of that one curve. Turn the view by \(\varphi\) about the vertical axis through the middle of the panel, \(\tau_c\):

\[ \text{screen } x = \tau_c + (\tau-\tau_c)\cos\varphi + x\sin\varphi,\qquad \text{screen } y = h . \tag{4} \]

At \(\varphi = 0\) the panel shows \((\tau, h)\), the wave. At \(\varphi = 90^\circ\) the time axis points at the reader, every \(\tau\) lands on the same place, and the panel shows \((x, h)\): the circle, or the butterfly. In between, the wave is seen at a slant and looks like a spring. The height never changes under this turn, so the horizontal line stays horizontal throughout. The panel below turns the same way about a horizontal axis, keeping \(x\), so its line stays vertical.

The turn is a rotation, so it is undone by turning back: a circle seen edge-on along time is a wave, and a wave seen end-on along time is a circle. Which picture appears depends only on where one looks from.

4. What the butterfly needs

The butterfly is the pendulum kicked with \(p = (3.557, 12.211)\) kg·m²/s from hanging still. Its state returns to the start after \(T = 2.659\) s (to about \(10^{-11}\), found by Newton's method on Hamilton's equations; see The Flower Hunt), so its path closes after 24 petals. It is shown here at a quarter of real speed. In a frictionless model it repeats for ever; a real one loses energy to friction, and this orbit is weakly unstable (a small error grows by about 2 % per lap), so it drifts after a minute or two.
