Resonant Tunnelling.

One thin barrier lets a slow quantum particle through about six times in a hundred. Put a second identical barrier a few nanometres behind the first, and at just the right energy the particle passes with certainty — a hundred times in a hundred. The wave builds up in the gap, rings for a while, and leaks out the far side. This is the resonant-tunnelling diode, the fastest solid-state oscillator we have. Every frame here was built from the exact rules of one-dimensional scattering applied to data already computed; the page only plays it back.
