From physics to pixels

What the arms are, why two of them draw a rose, when the curve closes, and how every point on the screen is computed.

01 · Physical picture

A small hand bolted to a big hand

Take a clock and bolt a small, fast hand to the very tip of the minute hand. The tip of the small hand no longer draws a circle: it loops around the big circle like a bead on a spinning ring. Bolt a third hand to the tip of the second and the loops grow loops. This is the machine Ptolemy used for the planets, and it is exactly the chain of arms in the figure.

You know the motion from a fairground: the arm of a carousel turns slowly, the cup at its end spins fast, and your head traces a flower on the ground. If the cup spins against the arm, the flower closes into petals; if it spins with the arm, you get wide lazy loops. Two equal hands spinning in opposite directions swing the tip back and forth through the centre: that is the rose.

02 · Model

A sum of uniform circular motions

A point in the plane is a complex number z = x + iy. One arm of length A rotating uniformly at angular frequency ω, starting at angle φ, is a phasor:

zk(t) = Ak ei(ωk t + φk) = Ak [cos(ωkt + φk) + i sin(ωkt + φk)]A ≥ 0: radius · ω: rad/s, sign = sense of rotation · φ: starting angle

Mounting arm k + 1 on the tip of arm k means adding vectors, so the last tip is

z(t) = Σk=1N Ak ei(ωk t + φk)

The order of the arms changes the picture of the chain but not the tip, because addition commutes. Each arm is a free rigid rotor, L = ½ I θ̇², whose equation of motion θ̈ = 0 gives θ = ωt + φ; there are no forces between the arms. Everything you see is kinematics: the shape is fixed by the list (Ak, ωk, φk).

Velocity and cusps

Differentiating term by term, each arm contributes a velocity perpendicular to itself:

ż(t) = Σk i ωk Ak ei(ωk t + φk)multiplying by i rotates by 90°

The pen stops (ż = 0) only where these rotated vectors cancel. That is where the curve has a cusp, the sharp tip of a star.

03 · Derivation

Roses, beats, closure and symmetry

Two equal arms: the rose

Take A1 = A2 = A and factor out the mean rotation. With Σ = ω1 + ω2, Δ = ω1 − ω2 and zero phases,

z = A (eiω₁t + eiω₂t) = A eiΣt/2 (eiΔt/2 + e−iΔt/2)  = 2A cos(Δt/2) · eiΣt/2

In polar coordinates the angle is θ = Σt/2 and the radius is r = 2A cos(Δt/2). Eliminating t gives

r = 2A cos(n θ),   n = Δ / Σrhodonea: n petals if n is odd, 2n if n is even

The presets are built this way (the phases −π/2 and +π/2 only rotate the rose). 3-petal: ω = 4 and −2, so Σ = 2, Δ = 6, n = 3. 4-petal: ω = 3 and −1, n = 2, four petals. 8-petal: ω = 5 and −3, n = 4, eight petals.

Same direction: beats

If both arms turn the same way at nearly the same rate, Σ is large and Δ small. The same factorisation now reads as a fast rotation eiΣt/2 inside a slow envelope |z| = 2A|cos(Δt/2)|. The distance from the centre swells and collapses with period 2π/|Δ|, exactly like two tuning forks slightly out of tune. Beating pair uses ω = 5 and 6.

When does the curve close?

z(t + T) = z(t) for all t requires eiωkT = 1 for every k, so ωkT must be a multiple of 2π for all k at once. If all ωk are integer multiples of a largest common frequency g, the smallest such T is

T = 2π / g,   g = gcd(ω1, …, ωN)

If two frequencies have an irrational ratio, no T exists and the curve never closes: it fills an annulus densely. The sliders move in steps of 0.01 rad/s, so the page computes g on the integers 100ωk; the period readout can then be very long.

Rotational symmetry

Shift time by τ: z(t + τ) = Σ Ak ei(ωkt + φk) eiωkτ. If the extra factors eiωkτ are all equal, the whole curve is just rotated by that common angle. They are equal when every difference (ωj − ωk)τ is a multiple of 2π, so τ = 2π/M with M = gcd of the differences, and the rotation angle is ω1τ = 2π ω1/M. Reduce ω1/M = p/q: the curve maps onto itself under a rotation by 2π p/q, so it has q-fold symmetry.

TryStar has ω = 1, −4, 6. Differences −5 and 5, M = 5, ω1/M = 1/5: five-fold. 3-petal rose: M = 6, ω1/M = 4/6 = 2/3: three-fold. Change one ω by 1 and watch the symmetry readout jump.

Average distance: Parseval

Expand |z|² = z z̄. The diagonal terms give Σ Ak²; the cross terms oscillate as cos((ωj − ωk)t + φj − φk) and average to zero whenever the frequencies differ:

⟨|z|²⟩t = Σk Ak² + 2 Σj<k AjAk ⟨cos(…)⟩ = Σk Ak²valid when all ωk are distinct

This is the energy readout. It is the same bookkeeping as Parseval's theorem in Fig. 8, seen from the other side.

04 · Numerical method

Exact evaluation, no integration

Because the motion is known in closed form, nothing is integrated. Every frame the clock advances t by Δt · speed · (dance factor), with Δt the real time between frames (capped at 50 ms), and the page evaluates N cosines and N sines. There is no accumulated error: t = 100 s is as accurate as t = 1 s.

  • Live trail: the last up to 6000 tip positions, one per frame.
  • Final shape: the curve sampled at n equally spaced times over one period (or over the chosen span), 700 samples per 2π of time, clamped between 1500 and 12 000.
  • Period and symmetry: integer gcd (Euclid's algorithm) on 100ωk. Arms with A = 0 are ignored.

Morphing is a low-pass filter

When you move a slider in Final shape mode, each sampled point p relaxes toward its new target q once per frame:

p ← p + α (q − p)   ⇒   pm − q = (1 − α)m (p0 − q)m frames later the gap has shrunk by (1 − α)m ≈ e−αm

That is a first-order linear filter with a time constant of about 1/α frames: α = 0.08 settles to 1/e in about 12 frames, 0.2 s at 60 frames per second. The in-between curves are straight-line blends of sample points, not curves any real set of phasors would draw.

05 · On screen

Complex plane to pixels, sound to radii

The origin sits at the centre of the figure and the imaginary axis points up, so a point z becomes pixel (cx + s·x, cy − s·y). The scale is s = 0.44 · min(width, height) / R, where R is the farthest anything is drawn from the origin: the larger of Σ A′k (the radii as drawn, Auto Dance included), which bounds every joint and every circle edge by the triangle inequality, and the farthest point still on the trail. R jumps up at once when something would leave the figure and relaxes down with a time constant of 0.6 s, so the picture never spills over and does not jitter. In Final shape mode R is the largest |z| on the curve. Zoom only zooms out (0.3× to 1×).

Auto Dance

An audio file you choose is played through the browser's Web Audio analyser: a fast Fourier transform of 2048 samples gives 1024 frequency bins, each about 21.5 Hz wide at 44.1 kHz. Three bands are averaged and normalised to [0, 1]: bass 20–150 Hz, mid 150–4000 Hz, high 4–16 kHz, each smoothed as e ← e + 0.15 (raw − e) per frame.

  • Arms with |ω| at or below the median get radius A · (1 + ease(bass) · drive · (1 − 0.7|ω|/|ω|max)); the slow arms breathe with the bass. The per-circle bounce curve is the easing function applied to the bass level.
  • Faster arms get A · (1 + high · drive).
  • Mid energy speeds up time: Δt is multiplied by 1 + mid · speed drive.
  • A beat is declared when the raw bass jumps by more than 35 % above the previous frame, exceeds 0.22, and at least 280 ms have passed. All radii are then multiplied by 1 + burst, decaying linearly to 1 over 10 frames. BPM is 60 000 divided by the median of the last 8 beat intervals in ms.

Colours: traced curve in burgundy, arms in ink tones, each arm's circle in rose. The recorder stores the list (A, ω, φ, curve) every 50 ms against the audio time; replay eases toward each snapshot over 50 ms.

06 · Limits

What this leaves out

  • Pure kinematics: the arms carry no mass and exert no forces. For dynamics see the double pendulum, Fig. 2.
  • The period readout assumes ω is exactly a multiple of 0.01 rad/s. Truly incommensurate frequencies never close, but a screen can only ever show finitely many.
  • The beat detector is a heuristic threshold, not a tempo tracker; it misses soft beats and can double-count busy bass lines.
  • Band smoothing is per frame, so on a 120 Hz screen the meters respond twice as fast as on a 60 Hz screen.
  • While Auto Dance is on, radii change during the drawing, so the live trail is no longer a single closed curve.
