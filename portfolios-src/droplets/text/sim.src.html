Lucky drops

An exact simulation of every merger in a small volume of the same cloud (modes cut at three standard deviations, chapter 6). Panel 4 · energy, chance and place

A cloud with a shape

A vertical slice of air 2 km wide and 1.5 km high. Each dot stands for many drops of one size: droplets blue, collectors ochre, drizzle wine. A drop moves with its fall speed, the air's mean motion (one overturning cell, rising 1 m/s in the middle) and a stochastic element: the eddies too small to draw, an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck velocity whose strength and memory come from the dissipation rate ε. Inside the shape the air is saturated; outside it drops evaporate. Drops collide only with drops in their own 50 m cell. Lecture notes, chapter 11.

Press Start. Click inside the picture to pin a probe; its values appear below the picture.
No probe pinned.

This panel uses 32 super-droplets per cell to stay quick; the runs of chapter 11 use 128, which the box test there shows to be within 2 % of the collection equation (at 32 the box is about 10 % slower).

Panel 5 · energy and entropy

One landscape, three rules

The same 10 cm³ of cloud (3010 drops) merged three ways: by the kernel of chapters 1 to 4, by the Metropolis rule that only asks whether energy falls (every pair alike), and by steepest descent (always the largest pair). Every merger lowers the surface energy, so all three end in one drop; the path is what differs. Lecture notes, chapter 10.

Each press draws new drops.
