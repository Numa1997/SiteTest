Panel 4 · energy, chance and place

A cloud with a shape

A vertical slice of air 2 km wide and 1.5 km high. Each dot stands for many drops of one size: droplets blue, collectors ochre, drizzle wine. A drop moves with its fall speed, the cloud's mean motion (an overturning flow that rises at 1 m/s in the middle and never crosses the outline) and a stochastic element: eddies whose strength and memory come from the dissipation rate ε and the eddy size. Inside the outline the air is saturated; outside it drops evaporate. Drops collide only with drops in their own 50 m cell. Lecture notes, chapter 12.

Press Start. Click inside the picture to pin a probe; its values appear below the picture.

Panel 5 · energy and entropy

One landscape, three rules

The same volume of cloud merged three ways: by the collision kernel of chapters 1 to 4, by a rule that accepts any merger that lowers the free energy with every pair equally likely (an uninformed lottery), and by steepest descent (always the largest pair). Every merger lowers the surface free energy, so all three end in one drop; the path is what differs. Lecture notes, chapter 11.

Each press draws new drops.

A rule that also proposed splits would accept one only if the air were hotter than about 10¹² K (problem 6).
