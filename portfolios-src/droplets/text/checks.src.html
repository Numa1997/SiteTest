How the numbers are tested

Checks

The model is one block of JavaScript that runs in this page and in Node. Three kinds of test stand behind every number in the notes: exact solutions the model must reproduce, independent recomputations in Python (constants typed again, integrations by SciPy, derivations by SymPy), and a script that recomputes every number quoted in the notes and finds it in the text.

One of these tests taught something worth passing on. The second, independent solver of the collection equation first gave $t_{90}$ = 18.3 min instead of 13.5. The physics was the same; the difference was one line of bookkeeping. A step may not take more drops out of a bin than it holds, and the first version counted every merger of a large drop as a loss to its bin. But a 1 mm drop that sweeps up an 8 µm droplet stays a 1 mm drop: it almost never leaves its bin, so its bin loses almost nothing. Counting the gross loss throttled exactly the largest, fastest-growing drops. Counted net, the two solvers agree to 0.013 min. In a numerical scheme, as in the physics, what matters is what actually moves.
