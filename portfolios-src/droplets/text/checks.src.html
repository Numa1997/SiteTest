One of these tests taught something worth passing on. The second, independent solver of the collection equation first gave $t_{90}$ = 18.3 min instead of 13.5. The physics was the same; the difference was one line of bookkeeping. A step may not take more drops out of a bin than it holds, and the first version counted every merger of a large drop as a loss to its bin. But a 1 mm drop that sweeps up an 8 µm droplet stays a 1 mm drop: it almost never leaves its bin, so its bin loses almost nothing. Counting the gross loss throttled exactly the largest, fastest-growing drops. Counted net, the two solvers agree to 0.013 min.

Golovin's exact solution

Chapter 5 tests the grid against the one kernel for which (5.1) can be solved exactly, the sum kernel $K = b(m + m')$. With it the gain term of (5.1) is $\tfrac12 b\,m\int_0^m n(m')\,n(m - m')\,dm'$ (because $m' + (m - m') = m$), and the loss term is $b\,n(m)\,(mN + L)$, with $N(t) = \int n\,dm$ the number of drops and $L = \int m\,n\,dm$ the water, which is constant. Take the Laplace transform $F(p, t) = \int_0^\infty n(m, t)\,e^{-pm}\,dm$. A convolution becomes a product, and a factor $m$ becomes $-\partial_p$, so

$$\frac{\partial F}{\partial t} = -\frac b2\,\frac{\partial F^2}{\partial p} + bN\,\frac{\partial F}{\partial p} - bLF. \tag{C.1}$$

At $p = 0$, $F = N$, and (C.1) gives $dN/dt = -bLN$: the number falls as $N = N_0e^{-bLt}$. Subtract that from (C.1) and write $G = F - N$:

$$\frac{\partial G}{\partial t} + bG\,\frac{\partial G}{\partial p} = -bLG. \tag{C.2}$$

This is Burgers' equation without viscosity, with a damping term: each value of $G$ travels in $p$ at the speed $bG$ while it decays as $e^{-bLt}$. Along such a characteristic, $G = G_0(p_0)\,e^{-bLt}$ and $p = p_0 + G_0(p_0)\,(1 - e^{-bLt})/L$. For the exponential start $n_0 = (N_0/\bar m)\,e^{-m/\bar m}$, $G_0(p) = -N_0 p\bar m/(1 + p\bar m)$. Eliminating $p_0$ and transforming back to $m$ is the step Golovin (1963) carried out; the result, with $\theta = 1 - e^{-bLt}$, is

$$n(m, t) = \frac{N_0\,(1 - \theta)}{m\sqrt{\theta}}\;e^{-(1 + \theta)\,m/\bar m}\;I_1\!\Big(\frac{2m\sqrt{\theta}}{\bar m}\Big), \tag{C.3}$$

with $I_1$ the modified Bessel function. It satisfies (5.1) (checked by substituting it numerically, and its number and water reproduce $N_0e^{-bLt}$ and $L$ to $10^{-6}$). The spectrum error quoted in chapter 5 is $\varepsilon = \sum_k |M_k - M_k^{\rm exact}|/L$, the summed difference of the water in each bin, as a fraction of all the water.

The drift of the stochastic element (12.3)

Evenly spread air must stay evenly spread. In the stationary Fokker–Planck equation for position and eddy velocity, put the density that says so: a Gaussian in $\mathbf u'$ of variance $\sigma^2(\mathbf x)$, times a constant. The damping $-C_0\varepsilon\,\mathbf u'/(2\sigma^2)$ and the noise of strength $C_0\varepsilon$ cancel each other for this density, so what remains must vanish: $-\nabla_x\cdot[(\mathbf U + \mathbf u')p] - \nabla_u\cdot[\mathbf a\,p] = 0$. SymPy (derive/L10_drift.py) shows that $a_i = \tfrac12\partial_i\sigma^2 + u_i'\,(\mathbf u' + \mathbf U)\cdot\nabla\sigma^2/(2\sigma^2)$ makes it vanish exactly, with $\mathbf U$ any divergence-free flow, and that the one-dimensional drift applied to each component separately does not.
