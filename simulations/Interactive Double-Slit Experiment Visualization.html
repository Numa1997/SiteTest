01 · Physical picture

One wave, two doors

A flat wave of light arrives from below and meets a wall with two narrow slits. Each slit lets a little of the wave through and becomes a new source of circular wavelets, and the two sources are perfectly in step because the same incoming crest reaches both at once. At any point P beyond the wall, one wavelet has travelled a little further than the other. If the extra distance is a whole number of wavelengths, crest meets crest and P is bright; if it is a half-number, crest meets trough and P is dark. The rose arcs in the figure are those wavelets; the burgundy bar is the extra distance.

02 · Model

Two coherent point sources

Put the origin midway between the slits, x along the wall and y away from it. Then

S₁ = (−b/2, 0),   S₂ = (+b/2, 0),   P = (−d tan α, d)b: slit separation · d: distance of P from the wall · α: angle of P from the normal

The distance from the midpoint to P is r = d / cos α. Each slit is treated as a point source of the same amplitude and phase; the field is a scalar with wavenumber k = 2π/λ.

  • Monochromatic, coherent illumination: one λ, one fixed phase relation.
  • Point slits: no single-slit diffraction envelope (see Limits).
  • Only ratios matter: b/λ, d/b and α. The lengths are shown in px of the drawing.

03 · Derivation

Path difference, exactly and approximately

The exact lengths

L₁ = |S₁P| = √((d tan α − b/2)² + d²)L₂ = |S₂P| = √((d tan α + b/2)² + d²)δs = L₂ − L₁

Geometrically, the dashed arc is a circle around P through S₁. It cuts the longer path S₂P at Q with |PQ| = L₁, so the remaining piece S₂Q, the burgundy bar, is exactly δs.

A useful exact identity

Subtract the squares: L₂² − L₁² = (d tan α + b/2)² − (d tan α − b/2)² = 2 b d tan α. Since L₂² − L₁² = (L₂ − L₁)(L₂ + L₁),

δs = 2 b d tan α / (L₁ + L₂)   (exact)

The simulation computes δs from this form, not as L₂ − L₁. Near the axis the two lengths are almost equal, and subtracting two nearly equal floating-point numbers throws away significant digits; the rationalized form has an exact zero at α = 0 and keeps full relative precision for tiny path differences. At these sizes the gain is only about 10⁻¹³ px, but it is the right numerical habit.

The far-field (Fraunhofer) limit

Far away, both paths have length ≈ r = d/cos α, so L₁ + L₂ ≈ 2d/cos α and

δs ≈ 2 b d tan α · cos α / (2d) = b sin α

To see how good this is, write L²₁,₂ = r² ∓ b r sin α + b²/4 and expand √(1 + u) = 1 + u/2 − u²/8 + u³/16 − … with ε = b sin α / r and β = b²/(4r²). The terms that survive in L₂ − L₁ are r(ε − εβ/2 + ε³/8), that is

δs = b sin α · [ 1 − b² cos² α / (8 r²) + O(b⁴/r⁴) ]

At the default b = 40 px, d = 200 px, α = 20°, this gives 13.6275 px against the exact 13.6277 px, while plain b sin α gives 13.681 px, 0.4 % too long. The approximation fails when r is not much larger than b: at b = 80 px, d = 100 px, α = 30° it is off by 4.5 %.

From path difference to brightness

Write each wavelet at P as a complex amplitude E₀ ei(kL − ωt). Their sum is

E = E₀ e−iωt (eikL₁ + eikL₂) = E₀ ei(kL₁ − ωt) (1 + eiΔφ),   Δφ = k δs = 2π δs/λI ∝ |E|² = E₀² |1 + eiΔφ|² = 2E₀² (1 + cos Δφ) = 4E₀² cos²(Δφ/2)

I / Imax = cos²(π δs / λ)

Bright fringes (I = Imax) where δs = mλ; dark fringes (I = 0) where δs = (m + ½)λ. In the far field this reads sin θm = mλ/b: the fringe order m is the number of extra wavelengths. On a flat screen at distance D, small angles give bright lines spaced Δy = λD/b apart. Two approximations are made here, in this order: first the far field (b ≪ r) turns δs into b sin θ; only then do small angles allow sin θ ≈ tan θ ≈ θ. And the rule is always fields first: add the two amplitudes, then square. Adding the two intensities instead would give 2E₀² everywhere and no fringes at all.

TryWith b = 40 px and λ = 13.5 px, the first bright fringe sits at sin θ₁ = 13.5/40, θ₁ ≈ 19.7°. Set α to 19.5° or 20°: the verdict reads Constructive. At α ≈ 9.7° it reads Destructive.

Photons and electrons

Send the light one photon at a time and each photon arrives at a single point. Yet the points pile up in exactly this cos² pattern: the formula gives the probability of detection, |amplitude|². The same holds for electrons, atoms and molecules, with λ = h/p, the de Broglie wavelength. This is the sense in which the experiment shows wave–particle duality.

04 · Numerical method

Closed-form geometry, sampled curves

Nothing here needs to be integrated in time; every quantity has a closed form and is recomputed whenever a slider moves. The readouts show L₁, L₂, the exact δs, the approximation b sin α, the order δs/λ and the phase Δφ in units of π. The verdict is constructive when δs/λ is within 0.08 of a whole number, destructive within 0.08 of a half-number, and otherwise reports I/Imax.

Fig. 3b samples the intensity at 301 angles between 0° and 30°. The burgundy curve is the far-field law cos²(π b sin θ / λ); the dashed ink curve uses the exact path difference for a point at the chosen distance d. Where the two separate, the Fraunhofer approximation is breaking down.

05 · On screen

An SVG construction

The figure is drawn in SVG on a 600 × 440 px canvas that scales with the page, so lines stay sharp at any size. The wall lies at y = 370 px with gaps of 8 px for the slits; P is drawn at its computed position. The wavelets are semicircles of radius rn = (n + φ)λ around each slit, where the phase φ advances by 0.8 of a wavelength per second: a slowed-down stand-in for light's 10¹⁴ oscillations per second. Their opacity falls as 0.55(1 − r/430) to suggest the weakening of each wavelet. Below the wall, straight rose lines one wavelength apart are the incoming plane wave, moving in step with the wavelets.

06 · Limits

What the model leaves out

  • Real slits have a width w; each then diffracts on its own, and the fringes are modulated by the envelope [sin(πw sin θ/λ) / (πw sin θ/λ)]².
  • The two amplitudes are taken as equal; really each falls as 1/√L, which slightly reduces the contrast close to the slits.
  • Polarisation and the vector nature of light are ignored; for small angles this does not matter.
  • The animation speed of the wavelets is illustrative only.
