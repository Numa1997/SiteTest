00The butterfly as a machine: its strike, its speeds, its forces

One cell of the sheet, moved by a few thousandths of its strike until the drawing closes. Here is what that pendulum does, in numbers one could measure on a bench.

The strike. The butterfly is the pendulum struck from hanging still with \(p^* = (3.557313,\ 12.211005)\ \mathrm{kg\,m^2/s}\). In the hammer picture of section 2 this is two blows: \(12.21\ \mathrm{N\,s}\) forwards on the free end of the lower rod, which gives \((12.21,\ 12.21)\), and \(8.65\ \mathrm{N\,s}\) backwards on the elbow, which gives \((-8.65,\ 0)\). One blow alone cannot do it: a blow on the lower rod at the distance \(r\) below the elbow gives \(p_2/p_1 = r/l\), and the butterfly needs \(p_2/p_1 = 3.43\), a point 3.43 m down a rod that is 1 m long.

The speeds follow from the momenta. With both rods hanging, \(\cos(\theta_1 - \theta_2) = 1\), and solving the two definitions of section 2 for the angular speeds gives

\[ \dot\theta_1 = \frac{Bp_1 - Cp_2}{AB - C^2} = -25.30\ \mathrm{rad/s},\qquad \dot\theta_2 = \frac{Ap_2 - Cp_1}{AB - C^2} = +74.59\ \mathrm{rad/s}. \tag{1} \]

The upper rod starts backwards at 4.0 turns a second, the lower rod forwards at 11.9 turns a second. The strike supplies \(K = 410.38\ \mathrm{J}\) by ⟦4⟧, so the energy is \(E^* = K - 19.6 = 390.78\ \mathrm{J}\).

One lap. After \(T = 2.659405\ \mathrm{s}\) the pendulum is back in its starting state, the lower rod exactly 24 turns further on. In between, the two rods do very different things. The upper rod behaves like a slow pendulum: it swings out to \(\pm156.7^\circ\) and back, never over the top, its speed between \(-32.7\) and \(+33.1\ \mathrm{rad/s}\). The lower rod behaves like a spinner: it never stops and never reverses, turning at 44.7 to 74.6 rad/s, 9.0 turns a second on average. The tip moves at 38 to 52 m/s, the speed of a car on a motorway. The top pin has to hold all this: it pulls with about 0.9 kN on average and with up to 4.3 kN, 220 times the weight of the two rods (19.6 N).

Why the drawing is a butterfly. Each time the elbow is straight, the tip is at its full reach \(2l\), in the direction of the upper rod: that is the end of a petal. The lower rod overtakes the upper one 24 times a lap, so there are 24 petals, and each points where the upper rod pointed at that moment. The upper rod swings like a pendulum, slow at its turning points, so the petals crowd together there: the outermost ones lie at \(\pm135.6^\circ\) from straight down. Those two crowds are the wings. If the upper rod went over the top, the petals would go all the way round and the drawing would be a flower.

Why it is not chaos. The motion carries 390.8 J. Gravity can give or take at most 19.6 J of it, one part in twenty. So gravity cannot stop a rod or decide which way it goes; it only bends the slow swing of the upper rod. Followed for one lap, an error in the state grows by the factor 1.0198 (section 25): the exact closed drawing is slowly lost, but the butterfly shape is not.
