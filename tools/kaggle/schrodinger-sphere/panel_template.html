A wave packet meets a sphere

A quantum particle, spread out as a wave packet, flies along x towards a sphere of radius R inside which the potential energy is raised to V0. Kaggle solved the equation in three dimensions on __CELLS__ cells for every run below; this page computes nothing, it only plays the stored results. The picture is the plane z = 0 through the sphere's centre.

Probability behind the sphere (x < −R), inside its slab (|x| ≤ R), ahead of it (x > R), and lost into the absorbing border, against time. The vertical line is the frame shown.

Filled dots under a slider: positions computed and stored. Hollow dots: not computed yet.

What is stored, and how the picture differs from it

Units: ħ = m = 1, lengths in the grid unit a, energies in ħ²/(m a²). Kaggle integrates iħ∂ψ/∂t = −(ħ²/2m)∇²ψ + Vψ with the split-step Fourier method (half a potential step, a full kinetic step in Fourier space, half a potential step), __DT__ per step, on a periodic box of side __L__ with an absorbing layer in its outer tenth so that nothing re-enters from the other side.

Stored per run: __NF__ frames of |ψ| in the plane z = 0, on __OUT__ × __OUT__ points, in 256 levels relative to the first frame's peak (so |ψ|² is that level squared), and the phase arg ψ in 256 levels, set to zero where the amplitude is below 3 levels. The curves are computed from the whole 3D ψ, not from the slice. The picture scales the stored levels to the canvas with smoothing; in amplitude mode the colour runs from paper (0) to wine (peak) and is clipped above the first frame's peak.

Checks you can read here: the total probability (sum of the four curves) stays 1 until the packet reaches the border; with V0 = 0 the measured width σy(t) is compared with the free-particle law σ0√(1 + (t/2σ0²)²). The one-dimensional rectangular-barrier formula for a slab of thickness 2R is shown only as a guide for the central ray; a sphere is not a slab.
