# Planning the story

## The teaching order

A viewer can hold one new thing at a time. Order the scenes so that each uses only what the scenes before it gave.
This order has worked; drop the steps a film does not need.

1. **One object, at rest.** The thing itself, large, nothing moving. (A pendulum hanging still.)
2. **What we do to it.** The one action, shown on the object, with its numbers. (A strike: two pushes.)
3. **The picture's axes.** The object shrinks into a window; the axes draw themselves; the action becomes one point.
4. **What one mark means, by example.** Run the example. Show the thing that decides the mark's colour happening.
   (The pendulum and its twin part: the point turns bright.)
5. **The opposite example.** Same test, other outcome. (Twins stay: the point stays dark.) Now the viewer owns the code.
6. **The many.** Marks one by one, slowly at first (a dozen, about 0.45 s apart), then faster and faster while the camera
   moves out, until the whole picture is there.
7. **Why this shape.** One feature per scene, each with the curve or line that explains it drawn on the picture.
8. **The question the shape raises.** (Why does it lean?)
9. **The transformation, in moves.** One move per scene, each with its parameter growing on screen.
10. **What the new axes mean.** Name them in plain words. Show the fact that only now is visible (the 60 degrees).
11. **What the parameter is.** Where the number comes from in the machine.
12. **And back.** Undo it. End on the picture the viewer began with.

## Captions

Three lines, always in this form:

- kicker: `7 · THE PUPIL` (number, middle dot, two or three words, capitals, mono)
- title: `Why the centre is black` (serif, at most six words, a statement or a question)
- sentence: one or two short sentences, at most 28 words together, everyday words, no symbols the film has not shown.

Write the sentence so it is true of what is on screen at that moment. Say the cause, not the name:
"No rod can go over the top, so there is nothing for a hair to decide" teaches; "this is the regular region" does not.

## Timing

| Scene kind | Seconds |
|---|---|
| A still object with a caption | 4 to 5 |
| An action shown on the object | 6 to 7 |
| Axes drawing, object moving to its window | 7 |
| An example that runs (simulation) | 9 to 11 |
| Population, with the camera moving | 12 to 14 |
| A feature explained with a curve | 8 |
| One move of a transformation | 6 to 9 |
| A summary or "what the number is" | 8 |
| The way back | 8 |

Check each scene: seconds must be at least 0.24 times the words of the sentence plus 1.5, and at least 1.5 s longer than its
longest motion, so the picture rests before the caption leaves. A film of 12 to 16 scenes runs 90 to 130 seconds.

## The readout

Under the caption, one or two mono lines with the live quantity of the scene. Examples from the worked film:
`time 4.36 s of 6.67 · energy 32.2 J` and `tips apart by 0.819 m`; `strikes shown: 374`; the 2 by 2 matrix with `s = 0.51`.
Choose the one number the scene is about. It is computed from the same state the picture is drawn from.

## Worked example: "Why the map is an eye" (129.5 s, 16 scenes)

| # | Kicker | Title | On screen | s |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ONE EXPERIMENT | A pendulum, hanging still | the pendulum, large | 4.5 |
| 2 | THE STRIKE | One strike, two numbers | two curved arrows grow on the rods, with p₁ and p₂ | 6.5 |
| 3 | THE SHEET | Every strike is a point | pendulum moves into a window; axes grow; a ring marks the point | 7 |
| 4 | THE TEST | Give it a twin | pendulum and blue twin run for 6.67 s, part; the point fills bright | 10.5 |
| 5 | ANOTHER STRIKE | Here the twins stay together | second point; twins never separate; stays dark | 9 |
| 6 | EVERY STRIKE | Now do it for all of them | 12 dots one by one, then exponential growth to 90,000 while the view widens; crossfade to the map | 13 |
| 7 | THE PUPIL | Why the centre is black | camera in; the 9.8 J curve | 8 |
| 8 | THE IRIS | Where a hair decides | camera out; 39.2 J and dashed 196 J curves | 8 |
| 9 | FAR OUT | Hit hard enough and it is calm again | camera to the whole sheet; the two lines in blue | 9 |
| 10 | THE LEAN | Why does the eye lean? | camera back; a grid fades in | 7 |
| 11 | THE SHEAR | Slide it straight | everything shears, s from 0 to 3/2; matrix in the readout | 9 |
| 12 | THE STRETCH | Round it | ellipses become circles | 6 |
| 13 | THE TURN | Turn it by 62° | everything turns | 7 |
| 14 | SPIN AND FOLD | The eye, straightened | new axes named; the 60° arc | 9 |
| 15 | WHAT THE 3/2 IS | The shear measures the tie | hold; readout `shear 3/2 = C/B` | 8 |
| 16 | AND BACK | Nothing was lost | the three moves in reverse | 8 |

What made it work: the viewer saw one strike become one point before any map appeared; saw with their own eyes what "bright"
means; and saw the matrix change number by number while the picture changed.
